# Report: game preservation law worldwide

Almost every country lets a person who owns a game study how it works. No country lets that person share the game. That one line is the honest worldwide message for GameFrame. The international treaties set only floors. Computer programs are literary works in every WTO member. Copyright lasts at least the author's life plus 50 years. Countries must give "adequate" legal protection to technological locks. Whether a person may run, observe, decompile or rebuild a program is left to national law, within the "three-step test". Most countries outside the US, the EU and the UK have copied one of two models. The EU model lets a lawful user observe, study and test a program, and decompile it only for interoperability. The US trade-agreement model bans breaking access locks, with a closed list of exceptions that always includes interoperability. A smaller group, among them Brazil, Argentina, Egypt, the UAE and South Africa, has no decompilation exception at all. Library preservation exceptions are common, but they serve institutions and often exclude software. No country gives a private owner a preservation right. Lock-breaking is the sharpest divide. Japan has no known interoperability exception to its lock rules and has arrested people for selling edited save files. Mexico has criminal penalties for circumvention for profit. India has a weak ban. Argentina and Chile have none, and Israel appears to have none (not confirmed). For GameFrame, the practical result is that its current rules already sit near the strictest common standard. The site should present preservation as the effect of lawful study of one's own copy, state the limits per region, and keep the rule against bypassing protection worldwide. This report is not legal advice.

## Treaties set floors and leave study to each country

The **Berne Convention** is the base. Its Art. 5(1) gives national treatment, so a foreign game is protected in each member as if it were local. Art. 5(2) forbids formalities, so no registration is needed. Art. 7(1) sets the general term at the author's life plus 50 years. Art. 7(2) lets film works run 50 years from release, and Art. 7(3) does the same for anonymous works. Art. 7(6) allows longer terms. Art. 7(8) adds the "rule of the shorter term", which applies unless national law says otherwise. Art. 9(2) holds the **three-step test**. A country may allow copying "in certain special cases", if this does not conflict with normal exploitation and does not unreasonably harm the author ([WIPO Lex, Berne](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/283698)). The **TRIPS Agreement** binds every WTO member to Berne Arts. 1 to 21. Its Art. 10(1) protects programs, in source or object code, as literary works. Art. 12 sets at least 50 years from publication where the term is not based on a person's life, which covers most company-made games. Art. 13 extends the three-step test to all rights. TRIPS has no anti-circumvention rule ([WTO, TRIPS text](https://www.wto.org/english/docs_e/legal_e/27-trips_04_e.htm)).

The **WIPO Copyright Treaty (WCT)** of 1996 came into force on 6 March 2002. Art. 4 restates that programs are literary works. Art. 10 restates the three-step test. Its Agreed Statement lets parties "carry forward and appropriately extend into the digital environment" their existing exceptions, and "devise new exceptions" for the network age ([WIPO, WCT](https://www.wipo.int/treaties/en/ip/wct/); [WCT Agreed Statements](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/295456)). Art. 11 asks for "adequate legal protection" against circumvention of locks that restrict acts "not authorized ... or permitted by law". Those last words leave room to allow circumvention for a use an exception already permits. The text says nothing about devices or trafficking. The **WPPT** has the same rules for recorded performances in Arts. 16, 18 and 19, which matter for a game's music and voice acting ([WIPO, WPPT](https://www.wipo.int/treaties/en/ip/wppt/)). WIPO's own October 2026 reading confirms that these treaties "allow Member States to adopt well-crafted limitations and exceptions", consistent with the three-step test ([SCCR/49/5](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/mdocs/copyright/en/sccr_49/sccr_49_5.pdf)).

WIPO lists **119 WCT parties**. They include China (in force 9 June 2007), India (25 December 2018), Indonesia (6 March 2002), Malaysia (27 December 2012), Nigeria (4 January 2018), the Philippines (4 October 2002), Thailand (13 October 2022), Türkiye (28 November 2008) and Viet Nam (17 February 2022). Israel, Kenya and South Africa signed but have not ratified. Brazil, Pakistan, Egypt, Iran, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Saudi Arabia are not listed. The Saudi absence is not confirmed. Taiwan cannot join WIPO treaties, but it is a WTO member ([WIPO Lex, WCT contracting parties](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/treaties/ShowResults?search_what=C&treaty_id=16)). No treaty therefore obliges Brazil or Egypt to have anti-circumvention law. The treaties do not forbid studying a program you own. They also guarantee no right to reverse engineer. The site should say both plainly.

### US trade agreements export the strict lock model

The strict model comes from US free trade agreements. **USMCA (CUSMA) Art. 20.66** makes a person liable who "knowingly, or having reasonable grounds to know, circumvents" an access control. It also covers making or trafficking in tools. Criminal penalties apply to wilful commercial acts by anyone other than a non-profit library, archive, school or public broadcaster. Footnote 70 lets a party treat a non-profit museum as an archive. Art. 20.66.3 makes circumvention a separate wrong, independent of infringement. Art. 20.66.4 confines exceptions to a closed list. The first item is "non-infringing reverse engineering activities with regard to a lawfully obtained copy of a computer program ... for the sole purpose of achieving interoperability of an independently created computer program with other programs". Libraries get access only "for the sole purpose of making acquisition decisions". Further exceptions need "substantial evidence" in a formal proceeding, the US triennial model. Art. 20.66.5 lets the interoperability exception cover tools. Art. 20.62 sets terms of at least life plus 70, or 75 years from publication for company works ([USTR, USMCA Chapter 20](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/agreements/FTA/USMCA/Text/20-Intellectual-Property-Rights.pdf)). Canada moved to life plus 70 on 30 December 2022 because of CUSMA ([University of Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/copyright-at-waterloo/news/copyright-term-changes-life-plus-70-years); [Goodmans LLP](https://goodmans.ca/insights/article/canada-extends-basic-term-of-copyright-protection-to-life-of-author-plus-70-years-starting-december-30-2022)). Canadian commentators read Art. 20.66 as leaving no room for a general repair exception ([Lavery](https://www.lavery.ca/en/publications/5389-publication-display-PDF.html)). A witness on Bill C-294 said it blocks general exceptions for non-infringing uses ([openparliament, C-294](https://op.alpheus.openparliament.ca/bills/44-1/C-294/?page=3)).

The same model sits in the US agreements with Jordan, Singapore, Chile, Central America (CAFTA-DR), Australia, Morocco and Bahrain. Chile's text is the most flexible. The CAFTA, Morocco and Bahrain texts do not even require knowing circumvention ([EFF, Seven lessons](https://www.eff.org/pages/seven-lessons-comparison-technological-protection-measure-provisions)). The Australian agreement drove a parliamentary review of lock exceptions ([Australian House of Representatives LACA committee report](https://aphref.aph.gov.au/house/committee/laca/protection/prelims.htm)). The Korean agreement reaches devices "promoted, advertised" for circumvention, which is wider than the DMCA ([Knowledge Ecology blog](https://blog.knowledgegov.org/?p=6640)). When the US left the TPP, the other eleven parties **suspended the term, lock and rights-information articles** in the CPTPP ([Global Affairs Canada, CPTPP Annex II](https://www.international.gc.ca/trade-commerce/trade-agreements-accords-commerciaux/agr-acc/cptpp-ptpgp/annex2-annexe2.aspx?lang=eng); [NZ MFAT, CPTPP vs TPP](https://www.mfat.govt.nz/br/trade/free-trade-agreements/free-trade-agreements-in-force/cptpp/understanding-cptpp/cptpp-vs-tpp); [DFAT, CPTPP suspensions explained](https://www.dfat.gov.au/trade/agreements/in-force/cptpp/outcomes-documents/Pages/cptpp-suspensions-explained)). Japan still moved to life plus 70 on 30 December 2018 ([bilaterals.org / Kyodo](https://bilaterals.org/japan-to-extend-copyright-period)). New Zealand is moving to life plus 70 through its EU agreement instead ([MinterEllison](https://minterellison.co.nz/insights/key-ip-impacts-from-the-eu-and-new-zealand-free-tr)).

In these trade-agreement countries the interoperability exception covers the act of reverse engineering an "independently created computer program". New code that reads the owner's own files fits that wording better than a tool that strips DRM to play or share the original. Preservation by circumvention needs a separate administrative exception, which most of these countries rarely grant.

### Library work at WIPO is still a draft

WIPO's 2017 study by Kenneth Crews covered all 191 members. It found **161 countries with at least one library or archive exception**. 102 allowed preservation copies, 98 replacement copies and 53 a library exemption from the lock rules. Many country charts note that "Computer programs are excluded" from library copying ([SCCR/35/6](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/mdocs/copyright/en/sccr_35/sccr_35_6.docx)). The same study notes fair use clauses in Israel, Liberia, Korea, Sri Lanka and the US. At SCCR/47 in December 2025 the committee agreed to "work toward finding convergences" ([SCCR/47 Summary by the Chair](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/mdocs/copyright/en/sccr_47/sccr_47_summary.pdf)). SCCR/47/10 urged adding museums to these exceptions (search snippet) ([SCCR/47/10](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/mdocs/copyright/en/sccr_47/sccr_47_10.docx)). In May 2026 SCCR/48 received a toolkit on access to heritage collections ([SCCR/48 Summary by the Chair](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/mdocs/copyright/en/sccr_48/sccr_48_summary.pdf)). The toolkit warns that born-digital works "may be subject to technical protection measures whose very purpose is to limit access". It has no section on games ([SCCR/48/5](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/mdocs/copyright/en/sccr_48/sccr_48_5.pdf)). The October 2026 Chair's document defines preservation to include "migration to stable technological formats" ([SCCR/49/5](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/mdocs/copyright/en/sccr_49/sccr_49_5.pdf)). That is the closest any WIPO text comes to describing reimplementation. It is non-binding, it serves institutions only, and SCCR/49 meets from 30 November 2026.

WIPO's only study of games dates from 2013. It found that no country "has clarified or offered definitive answers to the question of video game classification". Argentina, Canada, China, Israel, Italy, Russia, Singapore, Spain and Uruguay treat games mainly as programs. Belgium, Brazil, Denmark, Egypt, France, Germany, India, Japan, South Africa, Sweden and the US protect each part by its own nature. Kenya and Korea treat them mainly as audiovisual works ([WIPO publication page](https://www.wipo.int/publications/en/details.jsp?id=4130); [PDF](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/pubdocs/en/wipo_report_cr_vg.pdf)). WIPO's games page lists nothing newer ([WIPO, Video Games](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/activities/video_games)). Classification matters. It decides which term applies and which software exceptions apply.

No worldwide count of decompilation exceptions exists. Jonathan Band's *Interfaces on Trial 3.0* covers the US cases and *SAS v World Programming* ([PIJIP announcement](https://wcl.american.edu/impact/initiatives-programs/pijip/news/pijip-counsel-jonathan-band-publishes-new-book-interfaces-on-trial-3-0-google-v-oracle-america-and-beyond)). The 2011 volume with Masanobu Katoh covers the Pacific Rim ([MIT Press listing](https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780262538640)). Terms run from the life plus 50 floor up to **life plus 100 in Mexico** ([WIPO Lex, Mexico LFDA](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/3094); [LFDA art. 29](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/29/)).

### UNESCO calls software heritage and grants no permission

UNESCO's 2003 Charter on the Preservation of the Digital Heritage names "software" in its scope. It names "rapid obsolescence of the hardware and software" as a threat. It asks states for "appropriate legal and institutional frameworks" ([UNESCO Charter text (UNESDOC archive)](https://webarchive.unesco.org/web/20170127184223/http://unesdoc.unesco.org/images/0013/001311/131178e.pdf)). It is a declaration of principles and binds no one ([advocatetanmoy.com copy and commentary](https://advocatetanmoy.com/charter-on-the-preservation-of-the-digital-heritage/)). The 2015 Recommendation on documentary heritage underpins the Memory of the World programme ([UNESCO Memory of the World](https://en.unesco.org/memoryoftheworld/recommendation2015); [Consolidated report 2019](https://webarchive.unesco.org/web/20200519225806/https://en.unesco.org/sites/default/files/2015_mow_recommendation_consolidated_report.pdf)). IFLA urged states to change copyright law for heritage institutions during drafting ([IFLA](https://ifla.org/?p=109506)). UNESCO and Inria signed an agreement on 3 April 2017 to back the Software Heritage archive ([Linux.com](https://www.linux.com/news/software-heritage-backed-unesco/); [Wikipedia, Software Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_Heritage)). The 2019 Paris Call treats source code as heritage ([UNESDOC](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000366715)). These texts support calling games heritage. They give no individual a legal permission.

Preservation bodies speak for institutions. EFGAMP lobbied on the EU's 2019 copyright directive ([EFGAMP statement](https://efgamp.eu/the-statement-of-efgamp-on-the-dsm-draft-directive/)). It says the directive's preservation exception covers games ([EFGAMP 2019](https://efgamp.eu/2019/10/22/winner-games-heritage-games-preservation-advanced-by-eu-copyright-directive/)). In August 2025 it said more legal measures are needed ([EFGAMP 2025](https://efgamp.eu/2025/08/06/efgamp-statement-on-stop-killing-videogames/)). The IGDA's 2009 white paper "Before It's Too Late" remains the founding statement ([University of Arizona record](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/before-its-too-late-preserving-games-across-the-industry-academia/); [Cal Poly Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.calpoly.edu/lib_fac/107)). A recent law review note argues for US exemptions that recognise emulation ([Washington and Lee Law Review](https://scholarlycommons.law.wlu.edu/wlulr/vol82/iss2/8)). None of these bodies addresses an individual studying their own copy. The site should not claim their backing.

### The law applies where the person acts

Copyright is territorial under Berne Art. 5 ([WIPO Lex, Berne](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/283698)). A person's act of copying, decompiling or circumventing is judged by the law where they are. The host's duties follow the host's law. **GitHub applies the US DMCA process to every repository**, with no carve-out for non-US users. A claimant who wants content kept down after a counter-notice must file suit. Circumvention claims get review by "technical and legal experts", and GitHub says it will "err on the side of the developer" when validity is unclear ([GitHub DMCA Takedown Policy](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/dmca-takedown-policy)). A counter-notice must consent to US federal court jurisdiction, even from abroad ([17 U.S.C. § 512(g)(3)(D), Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512)). For government requests GitHub geoblocks content per country ([GitHub Transparency Center](https://transparencycenter.github.com/government)). It did so in Russia in 2014 ([TechCrunch 2014](https://techcrunch.com/2014/12/05/to-get-off-russias-blacklist-github-has-blocked-access-to-pages-that-highlight-suicide)). It also restricts some users under US trade sanctions, which is a separate matter from copyright ([GitHub Transparency Center](https://transparencycenter.github.com/government)). A project that holds only original code gives a rights holder little to claim. A reader abroad is still bound by local law when running it.

### What most countries share

Six points hold almost everywhere. Programs are literary works. No commercial game from the 1970s onward is out of copyright in any country covered. "Abandonware" is not a legal category, and ScummVM says the term "is not a proper legal concept in any country" ([ScummVM FAQ](https://docs.scummvm.org/en/latest/help/faq.html)). Most countries ban breaking locks, though the exceptions differ widely. Most allow a back-up or use copy of a program one owns. Library preservation exceptions help institutions only, and a private owner relies on the software exceptions. Distribution of the game is unlawful in every country covered.

## The US, the EU and the UK set the reference models

The **United States** protects interoperability analysis in 17 U.S.C. 1201(f). A person who has "lawfully obtained the right to use" a program may circumvent to identify elements needed for interoperability of "an independently created" program. They may also share tools and information "solely" for that purpose ([17 U.S.C. 1201, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1201)). *Sega v. Accolade* (1992) held disassembly fair use where it was the only way to reach functional elements ([Copyright Office fair use summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/segaenters-accolade-9thcir1992.pdf)). *Sony v. Connectix* (2000) held the same for an emulator's intermediate copies ([Copyright Office fair use summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/sony-connectix-9thcir2000.pdf); [opinion text](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/203/203.F3d.596.99-15852.html)). The exemptions in force from October 2024 let a person restore a server-dead game "for personal, local gameplay". Libraries, archives and museums may preserve games "in a playable form" only on their premises ([eCFR 201.40](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-37/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-201/section-201.40); [Finnegan summary of the final rule](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/ip-updates/final-rule-issued-in-the-us-copyright-offices-ninth-triennial-section-1201-proceeding.html); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/10/28/2024-24563/exemption-to-prohibition-on-circumvention-of-copyright-protection-systems-for-access-control)). The Software Preservation Network and the Library Copyright Alliance won renewal ([SPN/LCA renewal petition](https://copyright.gov/1201/2024/petitions/renewal/Renewal%20Pet.%20-%20Abandoned%20Video%20Games%20-%20Software%20Preservation%20Network%20and%20Library%20Copyright%20Alliance.pdf); [Register's Recommendation, October 2024](https://www.copyright.gov/1201/2024/2024_Section_1201_Registers_Recommendation.pdf); [Copyright Office bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USLOCCOPYRIGHT/bulletins/35be9b1)). Their request for remote research access failed after the ESA opposed it ([SPN/LCA Class 6(b) initial comments](https://www.copyright.gov/1201/2024/comments/Class%206(b)%20-%20Initial%20Comments%20-%20%20Software%20Preservation%20Network%20and%20Library%20Copyright%20Alliance.pdf); [SPN/LCA reply](https://www.copyright.gov/1201/2024/comments/reply/Class%206(b)%20-%20Reply%20-%20Software%20Preservation%20Network%20and%20Library%20Copyright%20Alliance.pdf); [ESA opposition comments](https://www.copyright.gov/1201/2024/comments/opposition/Class%206(b)%20-%20Opp'n%20-%20Entertainment%20Software%20Association.pdf); [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/us-copyright-office-refuses-to-allow-remote-access-to-out-of-print-games); [VGHF statement](https://gamehistory.org/dmca-2024-statement/)). The 2027 cycle opened on 9 June 2026 ([Copyright Office 1201 page for 2027](https://www.copyright.gov/1201/2027/)). Yuzu settled for $2.4 million on circumvention claims ([ChannelNews](https://www.channelnews.com.au/nintendo-settles-lawsuit-with-tropic-haze/)). Company works run 95 years from publication (not confirmed).

The **European Union** has the clearest statute. Directive 2009/24/EC lets a lawful user "observe, study or test" a program (Art. 5(3)). It allows decompilation where indispensable for interoperability (Art. 6). Art. 6 forbids using the information for a program "substantially similar in expression". Art. 8 voids contract terms that override these rights ([Directive 2009/24/EC, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2009/24/oj)). The exact wording is not confirmed against the official text. *SAS v World Programming* (2012) held that functionality, programming languages and data file formats are not protected expression ([SCL report](https://www.scl.org/2451-sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-ltd-report-and-analysis/); [SCL](https://www.scl.org/2436-sas-v-wpl-no-copyright-says-ecj/); [Wikipedia case summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_Institute_Inc_v_World_Programming_Ltd)). *Top System* (2021) allowed decompilation to correct errors ([Cuatrecasas analysis](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/spain/article/decompilation-of-software-for-error-correction-cjeu-judgment-in-top-system)). The Stop Destroying Videogames initiative gathered 1,294,188 verified signatures. On 16 June 2026 the Commission declined to legislate and offered to explore a voluntary code ([Lewis Silkin, 24 June 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/06/24/european-commission-stops-short-of-imposing-mandatory-requirements-following-citi-102n4yy); [Commission meeting page](https://commission.europa.eu/european-citizens-initiative/meeting-organisers-european-citizens-initiative-stop-destroying-videogames-european-commission-2026-02-23-0_en); [European Audiovisual Observatory, IRIS 2026-7](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/10576); [CDE Universidad de Almería](https://www.cde.ual.es/en/la-comision-colaborara-con-la-industria-sobre-la-desactivacion-de-videojuegos-por-parte-de-los-editores/); [Gaming Amigos](https://www.gamingamigos.com/post/eu-commission-declines-new-laws-for-the-stop-killing-games)). In France, UFC-Que Choisir began legal action over shutdowns on 31 March 2026 ([IRIS 2026-7](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/10576)).

The **United Kingdom** mirrors the EU in CDPA s.50B and s.50BA, with the wording not confirmed against the official text ([legislation.gov.uk, s.50B](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/50B); [s.50BA](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/50BA)). Its Stop Killing Games petition reached 189,887 signatures. The government said "There are no plans to amend UK consumer law" ([UK Parliament petition](https://petition.parliament.uk/petitions/702074); [Overclock3D](https://overclock3d.net/news/software/mps-seek-a-revised-response-to-the-uks-stop-killing-games-petition/)). It repeated this in the debate of 3 November 2025 ([Hansard](https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/2025-11-03/debates/C603C07C-1BB5-41EF-A3C1-1AE1FEBBB95D/VideoGamesConsumerLaw); [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2025/11/the-uk-government-shoots-down-stop-killing-games-campaign-in-official-debate)).

## East Asia enforces hardest against sellers and services

East Asia has clear software exceptions in Korea and China, flexible ones in Japan, and fair use in Taiwan and Hong Kong. Enforcement there targets people who sell devices, services or edited data.

### Japan

Japan has no decompilation article. It relies on the 2018 "flexible" exceptions, in force from 1 January 2019. **Art. 30-4 permits uses that are not for enjoying the work's expression**, unless they unreasonably harm the owner ([PrivacyWorld commentary](https://www.privacyworld.blog/?p=10720); [Korea Copyright Commission paper 285 on Art. 30-4](https://www.copyright.or.kr/eng/doc/quarterly_pdf/285%20Implications%20of%20%E2%80%98Smooth%20Rights%20Restriction%20Provisions%E2%80%99%20under%20the%20Japanese%20Copyright%20Act%20as%20Applied%20to%20Digital%20Works%20%20Focusing%20on%20explaining%20Article%2030%204.pdf); [CRIC English translation](https://www.cric.or.jp/english/clj/cloj7.html)). Analysing code is not enjoyment. Playing the game is. The Agency for Cultural Affairs' 2019 guidance lists program analysis as an example (not confirmed). Art. 47-3 lets the owner of a program copy adapt it for use on a computer, and Art. 47-4 covers incidental copies (not confirmed).

Japan runs two lock regimes. The Copyright Act added access controls in 2012 and 2018, and Art. 120-2 penalises dealing in tools (not confirmed). The **Unfair Competition Prevention Act (UCPA)** is the sharper one. Its 2018 amendment reached licence checks and made circumvention services unfair competition ([METI English overview slides, 2018 revision](https://www.meti.go.jp/english/policy/economy/chizai/chiteki/pdf/english_2018rev.pdf); [METI UCPA page](https://www.meti.go.jp/english/policy/economy/chizai/chiteki/index.html)). Press coverage dates the change to 2018 or 2019 ([Inverse/Input](https://www.inverse.com/input/gaming/japanese-police-arrest-man-over-selling-mods-for-zelda-botw); [Nintendo Wire](https://nintendowire.com/news/2021/07/09/breath-of-the-wild-hacker-arrested-in-japan-for-selling-modified-save-games/)). Penalties reach five years in prison and a fine of 5 million yen ([Siliconera](https://siliconera.com/pokemon-sword-and-shield-hacker-arrested-in-japan)). A 2011 amendment added criminal penalties and let customs stop flash carts from 2012 ([Gigazine, 22 Nov 2012, magicon import ban](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20121122-magicom-ban-import)), with dates not confirmed. **No interoperability or preservation exception to Japan's lock rules is known.** That makes Japan the strictest country covered for lock-breaking.

The case law is dense. Nintendo and 54 software makers won an injunction against R4 flash-cart sellers on 27 February 2009 ([Capcom press release, 27 Feb 2009](https://www.capcom.co.jp/ir/english/news/pdf/e090227.pdf)). A second suit followed that October ([Capcom press release, 5 Oct 2009](https://www.capcom.co.jp/ir/english/news/pdf/e091005.pdf); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20091016_majikon)). In July 2013 the Tokyo District Court awarded **95,625,000 yen**, and the judgment became final in January 2016 ([Nintendo release, 9 Jul 2013](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/corporate/release/2013/130709.html); [Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2013/07/10/41983.html); [ScanNetSecurity, 20 Jan 2016](https://scan.netsecurity.ne.jp/article/2016/01/20/37973.html)). Police then turned to edited game data. In February 2021 Nagoya police arrested a man who sold generated Pokémon ([Nintendo Wire, 5 Feb 2021](https://nintendowire.com/news/2021/02/05/japanese-police-arrest-pokemon-sword-and-shield-hacker-who-sold-generated-monsters-online/); [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/hacked-pokemon-seller-arrested-by-japanese-police/)). In July 2021 Niigata police arrested a man who sold modified Breath of the Wild saves and earned about $90,000 ([Nintendo Wire, 9 Jul 2021](https://nintendowire.com/news/2021/07/09/breath-of-the-wild-hacker-arrested-in-japan-for-selling-modified-save-games/)). A third arrest followed in April 2023 ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/?p=41046)). More Switch piracy litigation was reported in October 2025 ([Automaton JP, 6 Oct 2025](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/nintendo-switch-piracy-lawsuit-20251006-360534/)). Every target was a commercial seller.

Libraries may copy under Art. 31. The **National Diet Library** has received physical games by legal deposit since 2000 and holds over 9,600 titles. It said Switch 2 Game-Key Cards do not qualify ([Automaton, NDL and Game-Key Cards](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/nintendo-switch-2-game-key-cards-not-eligible-for-preservation-by-japans-national-diet-library-officials-say/); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/japans-national-library-says-it-wont-be-preserving-switch-2-game-key-cards)). It put its holdings at about 7,200 titles in December 2022 ([Automaton, Akamatsu](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/20230606-19332/)). Councillor Ken Akamatsu has pushed since 2022 for a law to keep games playable ([Automaton, July 2022](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/20220714-14016/); [Automaton, June 2023](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/20230606-19332/)). The term is life plus 70, and 70 years from publication for company works, from 30 December 2018 (not confirmed). Japan has an old ruling system for unfindable owners under Art. 67 (not confirmed). **A wider scheme for "unmanaged" published works came fully into force on 26 May 2026** ([Digital Policy Alert, implementation](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/30237-compulsory-license-system-in-act-no-33-of-2023-partially-amending-the-copyright-act-was-implemented); [ACA, 2023 amendments](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/amendments_2023/); [Japanese Law Translation outline](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/outlines/view/86); [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2343694)). A draft fee of 13,800 yen was proposed in 2025, and its adoption is not confirmed ([Digital Policy Alert, consultation](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/30199-agency-for-cultural-affairs-opened-consultation-on-the-draft-cabinet-order-partially-amending-the-enforcement-order-of-the-copyright-act)). No consumer rule on shut-down games is known. The Game Preservation Society, the Ritsumeikan Center for Game Studies and the Institute of Game Culture Conservation do the preservation work ([Automaton, July 2022](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/20220714-14016/)). A 2023 project set out to rescue rare game artefacts ([Tech Times, June 2023](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/292489/20230611/japan-launches-rescue-mission-preserve-rare-video-game-artifacts.htm)).

### Mainland China

China's Software Regulations Art. 17 lets a person who installs, displays, transmits or stores software **study "the design ideas and principles" without permission or fee**. Art. 6 excludes ideas and methods from protection ([gov.cn State Council text, 2013-02-08](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2013-02/08/content_2602615.htm); [fadada summary of current version](https://www.fadada.com/article/detail-18344)). Art. 17 does not mention decompilation. The Supreme People's Court's 2020 trade-secret rules treat reverse engineering of a product from public channels as a defence ([chinaipr.com, 12 Sep 2020](https://chinaipr.com/2020/09/12/supreme-peoples-court-releases-trade-secret-ji/); [China IP Law Update](https://www.chinaiplawupdate.com/2020/09/chinas-supreme-peoples-court-releases-provisions-on-several-issues-concerning-the-application-of-law-in-trial-of-civil-cases-of-infringement-of-trade-secrets/)). A 2022 Supreme People's Court case on reverse engineering is indexed by WIPO ([WIPO Lex judgment 3397](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/judgments/details/3397)).

The 2020 Copyright Law, in force from 1 June 2021, bans circumvention and tools in Art. 49. **Art. 50(5) allows circumvention for "reverse engineering research of computer software"**, provided no tools go to others ([China Justice Observer, Copyright Law 2020](https://chinajusticeobserver.com/law/x/copyright-law-of-china-20201111)). Software Regulations Art. 24 also bans circumvention (not confirmed). Art. 24(1)(8) lets libraries, archives, memorial halls, museums and galleries copy collection works for preservation. Art. 23 gives company works 50 years from publication ([China Justice Observer](https://chinajusticeobserver.com/law/x/copyright-law-of-china-20201111)). No orphan-works scheme is known. A Shanghai Pudong court treated unreleased game imagery and skill data as trade secrets ([IAM 2026 trade secrets hub](https://www.iam-media.com/hub/trade-secrets-hub/2026/article/china-trade-secrets-litigation-policy-and-latest-developments)). No emulator case is known. Every game sold in China needs an NPPA publishing licence through a local partner ([NZ MFAT market report](https://www.mfat.govt.nz/cn/trade/mfat-market-reports/entering-chinas-mobile-gaming-market-a-guide-to-licensing-and-regulatory-approval)). The NPPA approved 1,771 games in 2025 ([Niko Partners](https://substack.nikopartners.com/p/chinas-nppa-approved-1771-video-games)). A lawful re-release in China is slow, so private study carries much of the preservation load. No consumer rule on shutdowns is known.

### South Korea

Korea has the clearest statute in the region. **Art. 101-4 lets a lawful user decompile a program for interoperability**, where the information is not easily available. The information may not be used for other purposes, given to others, or used to build a substantially similar program ([ITWiki quoting the article](https://itwiki.kr/w/%EB%A6%AC%EB%B2%84%EC%8A%A4_%EC%97%94%EC%A7%80%EB%8B%88%EC%96%B4%EB%A7%81); [KLRI article listing (Korean)](https://elaw.klri.re.kr/kor_service/lawViewTitle.do?hseq=42726)). Korea has followed the EU model since 2000, and the ministry has studied changing the article since 2016 ([Korea Copyright Commission paper 231](https://copyright.or.kr/eng/doc/quarterly_pdf/231%20Law%20and%20Policy%20Study%20on%20Reverse%20Engineering%20Provisions%20under%20the%20Copyright%20Act.pdf)). **Art. 101-3(1)(6) lets a lawful user examine, research or test a program** to find its ideas and principles ([Korea Copyright Commission paper 045](https://www.copyright.or.kr/eng/doc/quarterly_pdf/045%20A%20New%20Analysis%20of%20Reverse%20Engineering%20Regulations%20for%20the%20Prevention%20of%20Hacking.pdf)). Korea also has a general fair use clause, Art. 35-5 (not confirmed).

Art. 104-2, added for the US agreement in 2011, bans circumventing access controls. Its exceptions include reverse analysis of program code. Ministry notices add further exceptions that last three years ([ITWiki summary](https://itwiki.kr/w/%EB%A6%AC%EB%B2%84%EC%8A%A4_%EC%97%94%EC%A7%80%EB%8B%88%EC%96%B4%EB%A7%81); [Korea Copyright Commission paper 254, 2018 anti-circumvention rules](https://www.copyright.or.kr/eng/doc/quarterly_pdf/254%202018%20Anti-circumvention%20Rules%20of%20Korea%20Copyright%20Act.pdf); [paper 320 on exemptions notice](https://copyright.or.kr/eng/doc/quarterly_pdf/320%20A%20Study%20to%20prepare%20exemptions%20notice%20draft%20for%20the%20prohibition%20on%20circumvention%20of%20technological%20protection%20measures.pdf)). Libraries may copy for preservation under Art. 31, and the Minister may license works with unfindable owners under Art. 50 ([WIPO Lex, Korean Copyright Act as amended to Act No. 8101 of 2006](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/188273)). Those texts are from 2006. A regional overview compares these schemes across Asia ([Asia IP](https://asiaiplaw.com/index.php/article/obscure-copyrights-in-asia-and-what-they-mean-for-vintage-films)). The term moved to life plus 70 in 2013 (not confirmed).

Korea punishes private servers under the **Game Industry Promotion Act**. In 2024 the Supreme Court upheld a 5 million won fine for a man who ran a private multiplayer server for GTA San Andreas, a single-player game, and took donations ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/23798/democratic-party-rep-jung-joon-ho-proposes-bill-to-strengthen-penalties-for-private-servers)). NCSoft won a seizure of bank accounts from Lineage private-server operators ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/20315/ncsoft-pursues-legal-action-including-provisional-attachment-of-accounts-against-operators-of-illegal-lineage-private-servers); [Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/40815/ncsoft-seeks-asset-seizure-and-damages-over-lineage-private-servers)). The offence sits in Art. 32(1) ([KLRI listing](https://elaw.klri.re.kr/kor_service/lawViewTitle.do?hseq=49070)), with the text not confirmed. Korea is also the only country in the region with **consumer rules for closed games**. Standard terms in force from 22 March 2024 give refunds for at least 30 days, even for closed games, and a route to compensation ([Korea Times, Nov 2024](https://koreatimes.co.kr/www/nation/2024/11/419_387275.html); [Vixio](https://www.vixio.com/insights/gc-south-korean-reforms-impose-loot-box-transparency-refunds)). Nexon paid a record 11.64 billion won fine over MapleStory odds ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/nexon-to-refund-maplestory-game-users-after-item-probability-error/12321081); [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/ragnarok-online-dev-fined-by-korean-government-for-manipulating-pull-rates-agrees-to-reimburse-affected-players/)). These rules protect money spent. They give no right to keep playing. Games need a rating before sale, and the Nexon Computer Museum in Jeju opened in 2013 (both not confirmed).

### Taiwan

Taiwan has no decompilation article. **Art. 59 lets the owner of a lawful program copy modify it for use on a machine** and keep backups for personal use. Art. 65 is a four-factor fair use clause ([Laws & Regulations Database of the ROC, Copyright Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=J0070017)). The IP Office analyses decompilation as a fair use question ([TIPO PDF](https://www.tipo.gov.tw/public/AttachmentORG/電腦程式還原工程合理使用之界線(89-19).pdf)). The lock rule in Art. 80-2 lists "reverse engineering" among its exceptions. It also excuses uses under Arts. 44 to 63 and fair use ([Wikibooks annotated Art. 80-2](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Annotated_Republic_of_China_Laws/Copyright_Act/Article_80-2)). Dealing in tools can bring a year in prison under Art. 96-1. Art. 48 lets archives and libraries copy to avoid loss. Art. 33 gives company works 50 years from release ([Laws & Regulations Database](https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=J0070017)). No orphan scheme or game case is known. Games need age ratings (not confirmed).

### Hong Kong

Hong Kong's 1997 bill had a decompilation clause. The legislature deleted it and widened fair dealing for research and private study instead ([LegCo Bills Committee minutes, PLC Paper CB(1)39/97-98](https://legco.gov.hk/yr96-97/english/bc/bc14/minutes/bc143004.htm); [BSA submission](https://legco.gov.hk/yr96-97/english/bc/bc14/papers/b141804a.htm)). Section 60 covers back-up copies, and a 2001 bill protected it from licence terms ([LegCo bill c056-e](https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr01-02/english/bills/c056-e.pdf)). The 2007 amendment made dealing in tools a crime ([HK Government press release, 27 Sep 2008](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/200809/27/P200809270122_print.htm); [Sydney University Press chapter](https://open.sydneyuniversitypress.com.au/9781920898724/cldc-new-hope-for-consumers-of-digital-copyright-material-in-hong-kong.html)). **Section 273D excuses circumvention for interoperability of independently created programs**, and the Secretary may add more ([HK Government (CEDB) note for proponents](https://www.cedb.gov.hk/assets/resources/citb/consultations-and-publications/Note_for_Proponents_Circumvention_Eng.pdf); [CEDB consultation paper on exclusion list](https://www.cedb.gov.hk/assets/resources/citb/consultations-and-publications/Consultation_Paper_exclusion_list_Eng_full.pdf); [IAM](https://www.iam-media.com/article/consultation-launched-further-exemptions-circumventing-tpms)). Rights holders asked that new exceptions cover only the act ([IIPA comments](https://iipa.org/files/uploads/2018/01/HongKongCommentsonTPMConsultationPapers.pdf)). In 2002 Nintendo, Sony and Microsoft stopped Lik Sang selling mod chips ([The Register, 30 Oct 2002](https://www.theregister.com/2002/10/30/attack_of_the_mod_squads/)). Customs raided console shops in 2008 ([HK Government press release, 27 Sep 2008](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/200809/27/P200809270122_print.htm)). Library sections 46 to 53 and the life plus 50 term are not confirmed.

## South and Southeast Asia and Oceania split between models

Australia, New Zealand, Singapore and India have full software exceptions. Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Vietnam have back-up and research rules with no decompilation clause.

### India

India's s.52(1) has four software clauses, added in 2000 ([Casemine, Copyright (Amendment) Act 1999](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5f5f124f9fca195c05ccefb4)). Clause (aa) allows copies for use and back-ups. Clause (ab) allows "any act necessary to obtain information essential for operating interoperability of an independently created computer programme". **Clause (ac) allows "observation, study or test of functioning"** to find the program's ideas. Clause (ad) allows copies or adaptations "from a personally legally obtained copy for non-commercial personal use" ([lawgist.in s.52 (consolidated text)](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/52)). The general fair dealing clause excludes programs ([Singhania & Partners](https://singhania.in/blog/applicability-of-section-52-of-the-copyrights-act-to-specific-works)). India's text lacks the EU limits, such as the ban on a substantially similar program (search snippet) ([Irish CRRA 2000 s.81](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/2000/en/act/pub/0028/sec0081.html)).

The lock rule in **s.65A punishes circumvention only "with the intention of infringing"**. It excuses circumvention "for a purpose not expressly prohibited by this Act" ([lawgist.in s.65A](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/65A)). It was added in 2012 to match the WCT ([BananaIP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/technological-protection-measures-copyright-act-india-drm/)). India is one of the most permissive countries for studying a game one owns. Libraries may store a work electronically for preservation if they hold a non-digital copy, under s.52(1)(n) ([lawgist.in s.52](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/52)). Programs last life plus 60 years, and films 60 years from publication ([The Legal School](https://thelegalschool.in/blog/copyright-time-period-in-india); [India Code, Copyright Act PDF](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1367/1/a195714.pdf)). Section 31A allows a licence for works whose owner cannot be found ([lawgist.in s.31A](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/31A)). Game cases are mostly about trade marks and site blocking ([IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/intellectual-property-rights/delhi-hc-backs-gameskraft-blocks-rogue-gaming-sites/); [MediaNama, July 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-delhi-high-court-gameskraft-dynamic-injunction/); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/delhi-high-court-orders-blocking-of-piracy-sites-streaming-stranger-things-friends-and-other-global-hits-86938.htm)). Delhi courts use independent experts to compare code ([Metacept](https://metacept.com/source-code-comparison/2)). No rule on shut-down games is known.

### Singapore

Singapore's Copyright Act 2021 follows the EU model in ss.237 to 242 ([LawPlayer, CA2021 Part 5 Division 7](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/section/part-5.division-7)). Section 239 allows decompilation under nine conditions. One bars using the information to make a substantially similar program ([LawPlayer s.239](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/239)). **Section 240 allows observing, studying and testing.** A data analysis exception cannot be excluded by contract ([Bird & Bird, 2021](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2021/singapore/coming-up-in-singapore-new-copyright-exception-for-text-and-data-mining); [Conventus Law, 21 Sept 2021](https://conventuslaw.com/report/coming-up-in-singapore-new-copyright-exception-for/)). The earlier rules date from 2005 ([Singapore Journal of Legal Studies, 2005](https://law.nus.edu.sg/sjls/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2024/07/1873-2005-sjls-jul-234.pdf)). Sections 425 to 435 ban circumvention ([LawPlayer, CA2021 Part 7 Div 4](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/section/part-7.division-4)). Section 430 excuses it for interoperability of "an independently created computer program" ([LawPlayer s.430](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/430)).

Singapore once had a lock exception for obsolete games in library collections ([WIPO Lex record](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7030)). That exception lapsed on 31 December 2024 ([Baker McKenzie, 2024](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/intellectual-property/singapore-public-consultation-on-prescribed-exceptions-in-copyright-regulations-2021); [MinLaw consultation](https://mlaw.gov.sg/public-consultation-on-ca-exceptions2024)). The 2024 regulations, in force to the end of 2028, instead let public collections circumvent to make preservation copies under s.232 ([Rajah & Tann Singapore, 19 Dec 2024](https://sg.rajahtannasia.com/?p=87460); [LawPlayer s.232](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/232)). Terms are life plus 70 ([vLex, Singapore IP Act 2004 highlights](https://vlex.com/vid/highlights-intellectual-property-act-29344420); [WIPO SME materials](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/mdocs/sme/en/wipo_smes_vte_15/wipo_smes_vte_15_t5.pdf)). No orphan scheme, game case or shutdown rule is known.

### Malaysia

Malaysia's s.40 allows a back-up copy "despite any contrary contract term" ([WIPO Lex, Act 332 consolidated to 30 June 2022 (unofficial updated text)](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/597931)). It has no general decompilation exception. Its lock rule in **s.36A excuses circumvention for interoperability**, security, encryption research, privacy and library acquisition checks ([WIPO Lex Act 332](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/597931)). These rules came in 2010 ([IIPA 2013 Special 301](https://iipa.org/files/uploads/2017/12/2013SPEC301MALAYSIA.pdf)). The last amendment took effect in 2022 ([WIPO Lex record](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/zh/legislation/details/22674)). A July 2026 consultation proposes to "materially expand" lock protection ([Baker McKenzie, July 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/malaysia-public-consultation-on-proposed-copyright-act-1987-reforms)). Only state archives and libraries get a public-interest use under s.13(2)(i). The term is life plus 50 ([WIPO Lex Act 332](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/597931)).

### Indonesia

Indonesia is unusual because its 2014 law names **"permainan video" (video games)** as a protected kind of work ([hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/klinik/a/perlindungan-hak-cipta-atas-program-komputer-cl4211); [UII journal](https://journal.uii.ac.id/JIPRO/article/download/17689/11696)). Pasal 45 lets a lawful user make one copy for research and development of the program, or as a back-up. The copy must be destroyed when use ends ([pasal.id, Pasal 45](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-28-tahun-2014/pasal-45)). Pasal 52 bans disabling technological controls, with no interoperability exception ([pasal.id, Pasal 52](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-28-tahun-2014/pasal-52)). **Pasal 59 gives games and programs 50 years from publication** ([pasal.id Pasal 59](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-28-tahun-2014/pasal-59)). The library article is believed to be Pasal 47 (not confirmed).

### The Philippines

The Philippines treats decompilation for interoperability as **a form of fair use** under s.185.1, since 2013 ([LawPhil, RA 10372](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2013/ra_10372_2013.html)). Section 189.1 allows one back-up or adaptation ([NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/can-a-computer-program-be-reproduced-for-back-up/)). Circumvention doubles damages under s.216.1(b), and no express lock exception exists. Non-profit libraries may make limited copies under s.188.1 ([LawPhil, RA 10372](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2013/ra_10372_2013.html)). The term is life plus 50 ([IPOPHL copyright page](https://ipophil.gov.ph/copyright)). A bill to change it has not passed ([Senate of the Philippines](https://legacy.senate.gov.ph/lisdata/4044836855!.pdf)).

### Thailand

Thailand's s.35 lets people do several acts on a program without profit, including **"research or study"**, subject to a three-step test in s.32 ([Wikisource translation of the 1994 Act](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:Copyright_Act,_2537_BE)). A 2015 amendment added system copies ([Tilleke & Gibbins, 2015 amendments](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thai-copyright-act-amendments-updating-law-digital-age)) and the first lock rules ([Tilleke & Gibbins, ten key changes](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/ten-key-changes-thailands-copyright-act/)). The 2022 amendment banned access-control circumvention with no intent requirement. **It removed the statutory exceptions** and promised ministerial regulations that are not known to exist ([Tilleke & Gibbins, 2022 amendment p.4](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/the-2022-amendment-of-thailands-copyright-act-the-good-the-bad-and-the-backstory/4/); [Tilleke & Gibbins, 2022 amendment](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/the-2022-amendment-of-thailands-copyright-act-the-good-the-bad-and-the-backstory)). Librarians may copy under s.34. The term is life plus 50, or 50 years for company works under s.19 ([Wikisource translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:Copyright_Act,_2537_BE)).

### Vietnam

Vietnam's 2022 amendment, in force from 1 January 2023, protects programs as literary works ([ASEAN Briefing](https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/vietnams-intellectual-property-law-new-amendments/)). It allows one back-up copy that cannot be passed on ([LawNet](https://lawnet.vn/en/laws/vietnam-can-organizations-and-individuals-allowed-to-use-a-copy-of-a-computer-program-make-a-backup-25583.html)). The 2025 amendments took effect on 1 April 2026 and added a research and AI training exception ([Baker McKenzie, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/alerts/2025/12/vietnam-2025-ip-law-overhaul)). Decrees 100/2026 and 134/2026 implement them ([Rouse, 2026](https://rouse.com/insights/news/2026/vietnam-ip-ai-law-updates-compliance-strategies-for-ai-training-and-book-digitization)). No decompilation exception is known. Lock rules were added in 2022, with exceptions unclear ([Tilleke & Gibbins, 23 Sept 2022](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/vietnams-amended-ip-law-will-strengthen-and-modernize-copyright-protection/)). Libraries may copy for preservation ([LawNet, copyright exceptions](https://lawnet.vn/en/laws/what-are-the-copyright-exceptions-in-vietnam-according-to-the-current-regulations-89118.html)). Films get 75 years from publication and other works life plus 50 ([LawNet, term of protection](https://lawnet.vn/thong-tin-phap-luat/en/chinh-sach-moi/term-of-copyright-protection-in-vietnam-116595.html)).

### Australia

Australia's Division 4A has sections for "normal use or study" (s.47B), back-ups (s.47C), interoperable products (s.47D), error correction (s.47E) and security testing (s.47F). Section 47H covers "Agreements excluding operation of certain provisions" ([Federal Register of Legislation, Copyright Act 1968, compilation C2026C00138](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1968A00063/latest/text)). These date from reforms around 2000 ([Computerworld NZ](https://www2.computerworld.co.nz/article/104071/australia_loosens_software_copyright_law/)). The exact scope of s.47D and the voiding of contract terms by s.47H are not confirmed. Section 116AN gives a civil action against circumventing access controls, with an interoperability exception ([AustLII s.116AN](https://20.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s116an.html)) (search snippet). Section 116AP has a parallel exception ([AustLII s.116AP](https://www6.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s116ap.html)). Further exceptions sit in regulations ([WIPO Lex record](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/zh/legislation/details/17787); [Federal Register of Legislation, F2017L01649](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01649/latest/text)).

Two cases frame Australian game law. In *Stevens v Sony* (2005) the High Court held that PlayStation mod chips were not unlawful devices under the old definition ([High Court judgment summary](https://mta-sts.hcourt.gov.au/assets/publications/judgment-summaries/2005/hca58-2005-10-6.pdf); [HCA judgment PDF](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/sites/default/files/eresources/2005/HCA/58.pdf); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevens_v_Kabushiki_Kaisha_Sony_Computer_Entertainment); [WIPO Lex judgment record](https://wipolex-res.wipo.int/edocs/lexdocs/judgments/en/au/au053-jen.pdf)). Later law reversed that result (not confirmed). In *Take-Two v Anderson* (2024) the Federal Court held that GTA V's online access checks were access controls. A cheat mod was a circumvention device ([Piper Alderman, 6 March 2025](https://piperalderman.com.au/insight/whats-in-a-game-technological-protection-measures-under-the-copyright-act/)). **Tools that patch a running game can be circumvention devices even when the user owns the game.**

Australia has the broadest preservation rule. Sections 113H to 113M, from 2017, let libraries, archives and key cultural institutions preserve any "copyright material", **with no exclusion for programs** ([Federal Register of Legislation, Act 2017 as made](https://legislation.gov.au/C2017A00049/asmade/2017-06-22/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html); [AustLII s.113M](https://20.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s113m.html)). The Copyright Amendment Act 2026 created an orphan works scheme on 1 April 2026 ([King & Wood Mallesons, 29 April 2026](https://pulse.mallesons.com/copyright/opening-the-archives-australias-new-orphan-works-scheme/); [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/orphan-works-remote-learning-copyright-amendment-bill-2025); [Bills Digest No. 33, 2025–26](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/bd/bd2526/26bd33)). The term is life plus 70 (not confirmed). Australia also leads on consumer law. The ACCC won A$3 million from Valve over Steam refunds ([ACCC media release](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/valve-to-pay-3-million-in-penalties-for-misrepresenting-gamers-consumer-guarantee-rights); [ACCC, Full Court confirms](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/full-federal-court-confirms-that-valve-misled-gamers); [MinterEllison, special leave refused](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/high-court-dismisses-valve-corporations-application-for-special-leave)). It won A$3.5 million from Sony ([Stevivor](https://stevivor.com/news/sony-fined-3-5-million-australia-digital-refund-policy/); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/sony-has-lost-a-court-case-over-declining-playstation-store-refunds)). Campaigners argue that shutting down paid games breaches consumer law, and no ACCC decision is known ([Kotaku Australia](https://kotaku.com.au/?p=1356554); [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/what-is-the-stop-killing-games-controversy-ross-scott-movement-explained/); [APH e-petition EN6080](https://www.aph.gov.au/e-petitions/petition/EN6080); [Checkpoint Gaming, July 2025](https://checkpointgaming.net/news/2025/07/stop-killing-games-petition-hits-1-million-signatures/)). ACMI led the Play It Again projects on 1980s and 1990s games ([ACMI, Play It Again I collection](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/119643--play-it-again-i-games-collection); [ACMI exhibition page](https://www.acmi.net.au/whats-on/play-it-again-preserving-australias-game-history/); [Flinders blog](https://blogs.flinders.edu.au/play-it-again/?p=11); [ACMI, Play It Again II](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/119644--play-it-again-ii-games-collection); [ACMI 1990s exhibition](https://www.acmi.net.au/whats-on/play-it-again-preserving-australias-videogames-from-the-1990s/)). The NFSA began collecting games in 2019 ([NFSA media release, 26 Sept 2019](https://www.nfsa.gov.au/about/media-releases/national/nfsa-collect-and-preserve-australian-video-games); [InfoDocket](https://infodocket.com/2019/09/26/national-film-and-sound-archive-of-australia-to-preserve-aussie-video-games-for-the-first-time)).

### New Zealand

New Zealand added EU-style rules in 2008 ([NZLII s.80B history](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/ca1994133/s80b.html); [NZLII 2008 Act s.43](https://mail.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/ctaa2008372/s43.html)). Section 80A allows decompilation for interoperability and bars a substantially similar program ([NZLII s.80A](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/ca1994133/s80a.html)). Section 80B allows copies for lawful use ([NZLII s.80B](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/ca1994133/s80b.html)). **Section 80C allows observing, studying and testing** ([NZLII s.80C](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/ca1994133/s80c.html)). Section 80D makes certain contract terms void ([WIPO Lex, NZ Copyright Act](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/449855)). The 2008 lock rule excluded measures that only control access for non-infringing purposes ([Davies Collison (DWW) 2008 summary](https://www.dww.com/articles/new-zealand-reforms-copyright-law)). The 2018 amendment added access controls with a general exception for non-infringing acts in s.226E (search snippet) ([NZLII CPTPP Amendment Act 2018 s.41](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/capaftpaa2018753/s41.html)). Section 226F allows getting past region locks ([NZLII CPTPP Amendment Act 2018](https://mail.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/capaftpaa2018753/s38.html)). A 2026 policy package promises stronger lock rules, library preservation exceptions and orphan works, with a bill possible in 2027 ([British Copyright Council, 9 June 2026](https://www.britishcopyright.org/new-zealand-copyright-update/)). The term is life plus 50 for now and must rise to 70 ([Tompkins Wake](https://tompkinswake.com/insights/knowledge/what-does-the-new-nz-uk-free-trade-agreement-mean-for-ip); [MBIE policy paper](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/dmsdocument/32052-policy-decisions-on-copyright-including-free-trade-agreement-obligations-proactiverelease-pdf)).

## The Americas outside the US range from Mexico's crimes to Argentina's silence

Canada has the strongest interoperability rules. Mexico has the harshest lock regime. Brazil and Argentina have neither a decompilation exception nor a modern lock ban.

### Canada

Canada's s.30.6 lets the owner of a program copy adapt it where "essential for the compatibility" with a computer, for their own use ([Justice Laws, Copyright Act, page 8](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-8.html)). **Section 30.61, from 2012, allows copying "for the sole purpose of obtaining information"** to make a program interoperable. Sections 30.62 and 30.63 cover encryption research and security testing ([Justice Laws, Copyright Act, page 9](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-9.html)). Section 41.1 bans circumventing access controls and dealing in tools. An individual who circumvents only for private purposes faces no statutory damages ([Justice Laws, Copyright Act, page 12](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-12.html)). **The interoperability exception in s.41.12 was widened in November 2024** to cover "a device in which it is embedded". A new repair exception sits in s.41.121 ([Justice Laws, page 12](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-12.html); [Justice Laws, page 11](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-11.html)). Commercial statutory damages run $500 to $20,000 per work ([Justice Laws, page 11](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-11.html)). A rule protecting libraries that unknowingly contravene is believed to sit in s.41.2 (not confirmed).

In *Nintendo v. King* (2017) the Federal Court awarded **$12.76 million** against a seller of game copiers and mod chips. It held that the shape of a game card was a lock. The homebrew defence failed for lack of evidence of real homebrew use ([Smart & Biggar summary](https://smartbiggar.ca/insights/publication/nintendo-awarded-12-7-million-for-circumvention-of-technological-protection-measures-in-precedent-setting-case); [judgment PDF](https://www.smartbiggar.ca/_Archives/files/2017%20FC%20246.pdf); [MBM](https://mbm.com/lit_success_story/nintendo-of-america-inc-v-king/); [Wikipedia, Modchip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modchip)). Section 30.1 lets libraries, archives and museums copy works in an obsolete format, unless a copy is commercially available ([Justice Laws, Copyright Act, page 8](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-8.html)). The term rose to life plus 70 on 30 December 2022, without reviving expired works ([McMillan](https://mcmillan.ca/insights/20-more-years-of-copyright-protection-starting-december-30-2022/); [UBC Copyright](https://copyright.ubc.ca/?p=7082); [University of Calgary](https://ucalgary.ca/news/end-year-copyright-change-will-have-significant-effects-public-domain)). The Copyright Board licenses works whose owners cannot be found ([Copyright Board, Unlocatable owners](https://www.cb-cda.gc.ca/en/unlocatable-owners); [Copyright Board ALAI presentation, 15 May 2025](https://www.cb-cda.gc.ca/sites/default/files/inline-files/2025-05-15%20PPT%20ALAI%20Unlocatables-EN_3.pdf)). It granted 277 licences from 1990 to 2013, none for games ([Barry Sookman, 2014](https://barrysookman.com/2014/04/27/orphan-works-the-canadian-solution/)). Nothing stops it licensing a game ([UBC Allard Video Game Law](https://videogamelaw.allard.ubc.ca/?p=4201)). Universities do most preservation ([University of San Diego symposium abstract, 2024](https://digital.sandiego.edu/symposium/2024/2024/5); [CIRCA, University of Alberta](https://circa.cs.ualberta.ca/index.php/CIRCA:Video_Games_Archive_Project); [Carleton University](https://carleton.ca/history/event/not-worth-mentioning-preserving-unremarkable-videogames/); [Loading journal, Érudit](https://releve.erudit.org/en/journals/loading/2021-v14-n23-loading06149/1078732ar)). No shutdown rule is known.

### Mexico

Mexico's art. 105 allows one use or back-up copy ([mLey, LFDA art. 105](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/105/index.html.md)). Art. 148 allows one private copy ([mLey, LFDA art. 148](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/148/index.html.md)). Its only reverse-engineering text sits in the lock article. **Art. 114 Quáter, from July 2020, excuses circumvention for interoperability** of "un programa de computación creado independientemente". Libraries may circumvent only to decide on acquisitions. INDAUTOR may add exceptions on evidence ([mLey, LFDA art. 114 Quáter](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/114-qu%C3%A1ter/index.html.md)). **Federal Penal Code art. 427 Bis punishes knowing circumvention "con fines de lucro" with six months to six years in prison** ([mLey, CPF art. 427 bis](https://mley.mx/CPF/articulo/427-bis/index.html.md); [WIPO Lex, CPF](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/edocs/lexdocs/laws/es/mx/mx251es.html)). The Supreme Court upheld these articles on 3 June 2024 ([SCJN, Acta de la Sesión Pública Núm. 54, 3 June 2024](https://www.scjn.gob.mx/sites/default/files/actas-sesiones-publicas/documento/2024-06-25/54%20-%203%20de%20junio%20de%202024.pdf); [SCJN admission order](https://www.scjn.gob.mx/sites/default/files/acuerdos_controversias_constit/documento/2020-08-14/MP_AccInconst-217-2020.pdf)). The NGO Article 19 criticised the ruling ([Zeta Tijuana, May 2024](https://zetatijuana.com/2024/05/scjn-valida-censura-digital-y-criminaliza-elusion-de-candados-digitales-acusa-articulo-19/)). On 7 October 2026 the Chamber of Deputies approved a bill on ISP liability, which is not in force ([Por Esto, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.poresto.com/mexico/2026/10/7/aprueba-camara-diputados-general-dictamen-reforma-ley-federal-derecho-autor.html); [Grupo Animal](https://grupoanimal.mx/explicaciones/reforma-a-la-ley-federal-del-derecho-de-autor-cuales-son-las-obligaciones-que-se-proponen-para-los-proveedores-de-internet)).

Art. 148 V lets a library make one copy of a work that is out of print and "en peligro de desaparecer" ([mLey, LFDA art. 148](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/148/index.html.md)). The term is **life plus 100**, the longest in the world ([mLey, LFDA art. 29](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/29/index.html.md)). No orphan scheme is known. Enforcement means raids. IMPI's "Operación Limpieza" closed Mexico City's Friki Plaza ([LevelUp](https://www.levelup.com/noticias/cierran-la-friki-plaza-de-ciudad-de-mexico-por-operativo-para-confiscar-videojuegos-consolas-y-figuras-pirata-asi-fue-la-operacion-limpieza/); [Excélsior](https://www.excelsior.com.mx/ciudad-de-mexico/friki-plaza-aseguran-mercancia-pirata)). It stopped 22,000 controllers at Manzanillo ([Xataka México](https://www.xataka.com.mx/legislacion-y-derechos/22-mil-controles-pirata-playstation-llegaron-a-mexico-china-impi-freno-manzanillo)). Nintendo and others filed criminal amparos ([UnoTV](https://www.unotv.com/nacion-gamer/nintendo-y-otras-marcas-escalan-la-batalla-contra-la-pirateria-en-mexico-el-debate-ya-no-es-solo-comercial/)). Arcades.mx restores arcade boards ([Leteo, UACH](https://revistascientificas.uach.mx/index.php/leteo/article/download/1296/2274/10161)), and INAH has run a heritage game jam ([UnoTV](https://www.unotv.com/nacional/con-videojuegos-buscan-conservar-patrimonio-cultural-de-mexico/)).

### Brazil

Brazil's Software Law, Lei 9.609 art. 6, lists the only non-infringing acts. They are a back-up copy, teaching quotations, similarity forced by function, and integration that is technically indispensable for the user's own use. **There is no decompilation or interoperability exception.** Infringing software rights is a crime under art. 12 ([Planalto, Lei 9.609/1998](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9609.htm)). The Copyright Law, Lei 9.610, has no software or library preservation exception in art. 46. Its art. 107 makes disabling copy-control devices a civil wrong, with no exceptions ([Planalto, Lei 9.610/1998](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm)). **Software is protected for only 50 years from publication** under Lei 9.609 art. 2. Art, music and audiovisual parts run longer ([Planalto, Lei 9.609](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9609.htm); [Planalto, Lei 9.610](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm)). The earliest Brazilian code may reach the public domain from about 2026 (not confirmed). No orphan scheme is known. "Operação 404" has blocked hundreds of piracy sites since 2019 ([Teletime, 14 Mar 2023](https://teletime.com.br/14/03/2023/operacao-bloqueia-mais-de-200-sites-e-aplicativos-de-pirataria/); [Teletime, 28 Nov 2023](https://teletime.com.br/28/11/2023/operacao-404-acao-internacional-tira-606-sites-de-streaming-pirata-do-ar/)).

Brazil has the most concrete shutdown bill. **PL 3612/2026 would require two years of support, 180 days' notice, and then an offline version, community tools or a partial refund** ([Game Overdrive](https://gameoverdrive.com.br/pl-3612-2026-preservacao-jogos/); [Diário do Comércio](https://diariodocomercio.com.br/?p=610178); [Ovício](https://ovicio.com.br/projeto-de-lei-pode-acabar-com-jogos-como-servico-entenda-a-situacao/)). Deputies across parties back it, and sources disagree on its author ([TecMundo](https://www.tecmundo.com.br/voxel/504923-kim-kataguiri-e-erika-hilton-unidos-em-apoio-ao-pl-inspirado-no-stop-killing-games-mas-com-ressalvas.htm); [Critical Hits](https://criticalhits.com.br/games/deputado-brasileiro-propoe-lei-para-obrigar-jogos-a-terem-modo-offline-e-aviso-previo-de-servidores/)). Citizens have proposed similar ideas in the Senate ([Senado e-Cidadania idea 220773](https://www12.senado.leg.br/ecidadania/visualizacaoideia?id=220773); [idea 185667](https://www12.senado.leg.br/ecidadania/visualizacaoideia?id=185667)). It is a separate bill from the 2024 "Marco Legal dos Games" ([Jornal de Brasília](https://jornaldebrasilia.com.br/noticias/politica-e-poder/camara-aprova-regulamentacao-de-jogos-eletronicos/)). The Museu do Videogame Itinerante was certified as a museum in 2014 ([Baguete](https://www.baguete.com.br/noticias/brasil-tem-1o-museu-oficial-do-videogame); [O Povo, 10 Sep 2026](https://mais.opovo.com.br/jornal/vidaearte/2026/09/10/mostra-gratuita-sobre-videogames-retorna-a-shopping-de-fortaleza.html); [Campo Grande News](https://www.campograndenews.com.br/lado-b/games/museus-do-videogame-terao-mostras-integradas-entre-brasil-e-portugal)). A 2025 thesis found legal and institutional gaps in Brazilian preservation ([UFSCar repository](https://repositorio.ufscar.br/items/f96f04e7-8b2c-480d-9c8d-8967222d3931); [UFRGS Biblioteca Central](https://www.ufrgs.br/bibliotecacentral/arquivando-diversao-para-eternidade/)).

### Argentina

Argentina's Ley 11.723 protects source and object code. Its art. 1 excludes "esas ideas, procedimientos, métodos y conceptos en sí". Art. 9 allows "una única copia de salvaguardia". **The law has no decompilation exception, no lock rule and no library exception** ([InfoLeg, Ley 11.723 texto actualizado](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm)). The term is life plus 70, with 50 years for anonymous company works, and the employer owns employees' software ([InfoLeg, Ley 11.723](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm)). No orphan scheme or shutdown rule is known. Local computing records are fragmented ([SAHTI paper](https://50jaiio.sadio.org.ar:443/pdfs/sahti/SAHTI-05.pdf)). A clean reimplementation that copies no expression is the stronger argument here.

### Chile

Chile's art. 71 Ñ, from 2010, is the broadest software exception in the region. **It allows reverse engineering of a lawful copy for interoperability "o para fines de investigación y desarrollo".** It also allows testing and correcting a program's functioning or security ([LeyChile, Ley 17.336 (XML)](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=28933)). Chile has no circumvention ban. Its only lock reference is in the ISP safe harbour. Art. 71 I lets non-profit libraries copy works off the market for three years, and art. 71 K allows on-site terminals. The term is life plus 70, or 70 years from publication for company works ([LeyChile, Ley 17.336](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=28933); [LeyChile XML metadata](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=28933)). No orphan scheme, game case or shutdown rule is known.

### Colombia

Colombia applies Andean Decision 351. It allows a copy or adaptation indispensable for use, and loading into memory for personal use ([CERLALC jurisprudence 477](https://cerlalc.org/wp-content/uploads/dar/jurisprudencia/477.pdf); [CERLALC 478](https://cerlalc.org/wp-content/uploads/dar/jurisprudencia/478.pdf)). Ley 1915 of 2018 makes circumvention a civil wrong in art. 12. **Art. 13(a) excuses reverse engineering for interoperability** of an independently created program. Art. 13(g) lets the DNDA add exceptions every three years. Art. 15 ties lock disclosure to consumer law. Art. 16 allows library lending and on-site terminals. Arts. 18 to 21 create an orphan-works scheme for institutions, which names audiovisual works but not software. Company works run 70 years from publication ([Secretaría del Senado, Ley 1915 de 2018](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1915_2018.html)). The term for natural persons is believed to be life plus 80 (not confirmed).

## Africa, the Middle East and wider Europe mostly copy the EU model

Ten of these twelve countries have a software exception. Several of the article numbers below are not confirmed against the official text.

### South Africa

South Africa's current Act protects computer programs as a separate category. **It has no decompilation or interoperability exception and no lock provisions** ([WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/)) (not confirmed). The Copyright Amendment Bill would add fair use, software exceptions, library exceptions, orphan works and lock rules (not confirmed). The Constitutional Court heard the referral in May 2025 ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/court-copyright-amendment-bill/); [acts.co.za](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2026/07/concourt-ruling-copyright-performers-protection-bills)). Its 2026 judgment struck down the education clauses only, and Parliament is reviewing those ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/constitutional-court-copyright/); [ENSafrica](https://www.ensafrica.com/news/detail/12103/a-decade-in-the-making-constitutional-court-r); [acts.co.za, 9 July 2026](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2026/07/concourt-ruling-copyright-performers-protection-bills)). The Bill was not law as of July 2026. Programs last 50 years from release (not confirmed). In *Golden China v Nintendo* (1996) the court held that games are "cinematograph films" (not confirmed).

### Nigeria

Nigeria's Copyright Act 2022, signed on 17 March 2023, is one of the most user-friendly laws covered ([Gazette text, PLAC](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf); [Mondaq review](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/nigeria/copyright/1462634/a-review-of-the-copyright-act-of-2022)). Section 20(1) is open fair dealing with four factors. Section 20(2) allows copies of programs for use, archives and repair. **Section 20(3) voids any contract term that restricts an act the Act permits.** Its interoperability permission sits in the lock section, s.50(8). Section 50 also lets libraries check before acquiring, and it excludes measures that only control non-infringing access. **Section 25 lets archives, libraries, museums and galleries copy for preservation**, and copy where permission cannot be had or the work is out of trade. Literary works last life plus 70 and audiovisual works 50 years from release ([Gazette text](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf)). Aiding circumvention carries a minimum fine of N1,000,000 or five years in prison ([Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2023/06/A-New-Dawn-for-Copyright-Protection-in-Nigeria-Final.pdf)). Goethe-Institut Nigeria's 2025 Echoes Game Jam made games from museum artefacts ([ThisDay, 10 April 2025](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2025/04/10/goethe-institut-africacomicade-conclude-echoes-game-jam-honouring-top-innovators/)).

### Kenya

Kenya's s.26A, from 2019, applies fair dealing to programs. It allows copies to fix errors, back up or test suitability. **It permits decompiling to get information needed to operate with other programs** ([CIPIT, Strathmore University](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/what-can-i-get-away-with-exceptions-to-computer-programme-copyright-infringement/)). The 2019 Act also added lock rules and extended the term, with details not confirmed ([Zigram summary](https://www.zigram.tech/resources/the-kenya-copyright-amendment-act-2019/)).

### Egypt, Israel and Turkey

Egypt's Law 82 of 2002 allows a back-up or use copy of a program in art. 171. It has no decompilation exception, no fair use, and a tool offence in art. 181. The term is life plus 50 ([WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/)) (all not confirmed). Israel's 2007 Act has open fair use in s.19. Its s.24 covers back-ups, maintenance and interoperability information. **Israel has no anti-circumvention law.** Libraries may copy for preservation under s.30, and the term is life plus 70 ([WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/)) (all not confirmed). Turkey's art. 38 matches the EU model, with observe, study and test and decompilation for interoperability. Art. 72 makes dealing in tools an offence, and the term is life plus 70 ([mevzuat.gov.tr](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuatmetin/1.3.5846.pdf)) (all not confirmed).

### The UAE and Saudi Arabia

The UAE's Decree-Law 38 of 2021 came into force on 2 January 2022. It lists "smart applications, computer programmes" as protected works ([Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2022/the-new-uae-copyright-law-2021-key-takeaways); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/de/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/10/protecting-online-data-and-ip-new-copyright-law-provides-clarity-for-uae-businesses)). Fines for unlicensed use run from AED 100,000 to AED 1,000,000 ([Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2022/the-new-uae-copyright-law-2021-key-takeaways)). It allows a back-up copy, and whether it covers decompilation is not confirmed ([creationbc summary](https://www.creationbc.com/corporate-laws-and-legislations/law-no-38-of-2021-on-copyrights-and-neighbouring-rights)). **Saudi Arabia's new Copyright Law came into force on 12 August 2026.** It gives lawful users acts for back-up, error correction, interoperability and security testing. Sources do not say whether it covers decompilation. It introduces lock protection, gives orphan works to a state body and "largely preserves" the term rules ([Baker McKenzie, July 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework); [Origami](https://origami.sa/en/blog/saudi-copyright-law-2026-software-digital-content/)). Regulations are due within 180 days ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/saudiarabia/copyright/1751404/saudi-arabias-new-copyright-law-key-changes-and-practical-steps-for-businesses)). It replaces the 2003 law ([AGIP](https://www.agip.com/News/en/news/25)).

### Russia and Ukraine

Russia's Civil Code art. 1280 follows the EU model. It allows adaptation, back-ups, decompilation for interoperability, and study of the running program. Art. 1299 bans circumvention and tools. Libraries may copy for preservation under art. 1275, and the term is life plus 70 ([consultant.ru](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_64629/)) (all not confirmed). Since March 2022 many Western stores have stopped sales in Russia, and Steam lost its main payment routes ([Massively OP, 31 March 2022](https://massivelyop.com/2022/03/31/international-sanctions-cause-steam-payment-snarl-ups-for-indie-game-devs-in-russia-and-ukraine/); [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.Net/gaming/news/valve-suspends-payments-to-developers-in-ukraine-and-russia); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/steam-ukraine-russia-payments/)). A draft Russian game law would add licensing-like controls, and its status is unclear ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/21441/russia-eyes-digital-sovereignty-with-proposed-video-game-licensing-system); [Kesarev, 19 September 2025](https://kesarev.partners/insights_19092025); [bne IntelliNews](https://bne.eu/russia-considering-crackdown-on-destructive-video-games-361855)). The Museum of Soviet Arcade Machines has run since 2007 (not confirmed). Where a game is no longer sold, an owned copy is often the only way to keep it playable.

Ukraine's 2022 law is newer and clearer. **Art. 25 allows back-ups, decompilation for interoperability, and observing and studying a program.** Art. 53(3) exempts the free-use cases, including software and libraries, from the lock rules. Art. 24 allows preservation copies, and art. 29 allows use of orphan works for preservation. The term is life plus 70 ([zakon.rada.gov.ua](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2811-20)). The older 1993 law had a similar article ([kodeksy.com.ua](https://kodeksy.com.ua/pro_avtors_ke_pravo_i_sumizhni_prava/statja-24.htm)).

### Switzerland and Norway

Switzerland's art. 21 allows decompilation for interface information. Art. 24(2) gives one back-up that contract cannot exclude. Art. 39a(4) lifts the lock ban for circumvention done solely for a permitted use. Art. 24(1bis) lets heritage institutions copy to preserve holdings, and art. 22b covers orphan works. **Programs last only life plus 50** ([fedlex](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1993/1798_1798_1798/en)) (all not confirmed). No separate observe, study and test article is known. Norway applies the EU directive through the EEA. Sections 41 and 42 cover use, back-up, study and decompilation, and the term is life plus 70 ([lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2018-06-15-40)) (all not confirmed).

Three groups emerge. The EU-model group (the EU, the UK, Korea, Singapore, New Zealand, Russia, Ukraine, Turkey, Norway and partly India) gives clear cover for observation and narrow cover for decompilation. In that group, decompilation has no express cover.

## What this means for GameFrame's own rules

GameFrame's current rules hold up worldwide. They need sharper reasons and a few additions.

**The rule against distribution is universal.** No country covered allows sharing game files, assets or decompiled code. Every software exception that allows decompilation also forbids passing the information on beyond interoperability, from Korea's Art. 101-4 to Ukraine's art. 25. GameFrame should keep this rule as the first line of every legal page. It needs no regional variation.

**Observation is the widest permission, and decompilation is the narrowest.** Observe, study and test clauses exist in the EU, the UK, Korea, Singapore, India, New Zealand, Australia, Ukraine, Turkey, Norway and Russia. China's Art. 17 covers studying design ideas while running a program. These clauses cover running the original, capturing frames and state, and recording calls, which is the work of the observe and emulate steps. Decompilation is lawful only for interoperability in most countries, and it has no express cover in a third of the countries covered. GameFrame should say this in its legal page. The skills could also ask each finding to note whether it came from observed behaviour or from decompiled code. That record would let a project show its footing in a strict country. This is a proposal, and the skills do not do it today.

**The "new code" rule answers the substantially similar limit.** Singapore, New Zealand, Korea, Russia, Turkey and the EU forbid using decompiled information to make a substantially similar program. Chile forbids a similar infringing program. A reimplementation that matches behaviour bit for bit is similar in function. It must stay different in expression. *SAS v World Programming* supports this in the EU, and the idea-expression rules in Argentina's art. 1 and Brazil's art. 6 III support it elsewhere. GameFrame's rule to describe behaviour in one's own words is the right one. The legal page should give this as the reason.

**Locks are where the risk is highest.** Japan has no known interoperability exception and criminal penalties for services. Thailand removed its exceptions. Mexico criminalises circumvention for profit. Australia holds that integrity checks can be locks. Many countries do excuse the act of circumvention for interoperability, among them China, Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia, Canada, Mexico, Colombia and Nigeria. Almost none excuse giving tools to others. When a user meets encrypted or protected files, an agent can tell them what is known about their country's law. Shipping keys, decryptors or patched binaries carries the most risk.

**Two additions follow from the case law.** Korea criminalises private servers even for single-player games. Any multiplayer addition kept apart from the exact core still carries that risk. An agent can mention this risk when a user plans an online service. Japan arrests people who sell edited save data. GameFrame's save round-trip tests are for the owner's own files. Offering or selling save editing as a service carries that risk.

**Contract terms need a regional note.** The EU's Art. 8, Nigeria's s.20(3) and New Zealand's s.80D void terms that override software exceptions. Singapore protects its data analysis exception from contract. Switzerland protects the back-up (not confirmed). Australia's s.47H appears to protect some exceptions (not confirmed). In most other countries a licence may still bind. The existing LEGAL.md line on licences is correct and should link to the regional pages.

**Age frees no game.** No commercial game from the 1970s onward is out of copyright anywhere covered. Brazil's 50-year term for code and Indonesia's 50 years from publication are the shortest. Even they free only the oldest code, and the art and music last longer. The site should never suggest that old games are free to share. Orphan works schemes in Japan, Korea, India, Canada, Australia, Ukraine and Switzerland license use by named routes. They do not excuse circumvention, and most serve institutions.

**Hosting does not change the user's law.** GitHub applies US rules to every repository, and a counter-notice submits to a US court. The user's own acts are judged where they live. Per-game repositories should hold no game material, so there is nothing to take down. The legal page should say that the US rules govern hosting and local law governs the work.

**Shutdown law supports the aim without granting rights.** Korea refunds money for closed games. Australia enforces consumer guarantees on digital games. Brazil's PL 3612/2026 would make an offline version or community tools a remedy. The EU and the UK declined new law. The site can cite these as signs of public concern. None of them gives anyone a right to copy a game.

The site's law chapter follows this report, with [one page per region](../law/README.md).

## Conclusion

The worldwide picture changes GameFrame's legal story in one important way. The US, EU and UK report framed the law as permission for interoperability. Outside those three, interoperability is the narrowest permission and is absent in many countries. Observation of a lawfully owned copy is the widest and most common one. GameFrame's strongest position is therefore its emulation and observation work, with decompilation as a supporting step that must stay disciplined. The site should lead with study of one's own copy and treat decompilation as the step that needs the most care.

The survey also shows that GameFrame's strict defaults are a feature for a global audience. A rule set built for Japan's lock law, Korea's private-server crimes and Brazil's missing exceptions is safe almost everywhere else. Publishing the regional pages makes that visible. It shows that the project chose its rules after looking at the whole world, and that a reader anywhere can check their own country's position before starting.
