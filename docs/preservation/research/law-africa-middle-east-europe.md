# Notes: Africa, the Middle East, Other Nations

Scope: South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, Israel, Turkey, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Russia, Ukraine, Switzerland, Norway. This is not legal advice.

---

## 1. Statutory exceptions for reverse engineering, decompiling and observing/studying/testing software

### Takeaway
Ten of the twelve jurisdictions have a software-specific exception. They are Russia, Ukraine, Switzerland, Norway, Turkey, Israel, Nigeria, Kenya, Saudi Arabia (new 2026 law) and, more narrowly, Egypt and the UAE (backup and use copies). Most copy the EU Software Directive model: a lawful user may observe, study and test the program, and may decompile only to get interface information for interoperability, under strict conditions. South Africa's current Act has only narrow computer-program exceptions. Its broader fair use and interoperability clauses sit in the Copyright Amendment Bill, which is still not law in October 2026. Nigeria is unusual: its decompilation-for-interoperability permission appears in the anti-circumvention section, not the general exceptions.

### Cited Findings

**Nigeria (Copyright Act 2022, Act No. 8 of 2023)**
- The Act is published in the Federal Republic of Nigeria Official Gazette No. 56, Vol. 110, Lagos, 27 March 2023, as "Copyright Act, 2022, 2023 No. 8". It repealed the 2004 Act and was signed on 17 March 2023 — [Gazette text, PLAC](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf); [Mondaq review](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/nigeria/copyright/1462634/a-review-of-the-copyright-act-of-2022)
- s.20(1) is a fair dealing clause with an open list ("for purposes such as"). It covers (a) private use and (c) non-commercial research and private study. It sets four US-style factors: purpose and character, nature of the work, amount used, and effect on the market — [Gazette text](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf)
- s.20(2) permits reproduction or adaptation of a computer program where necessary for (a) use with a computer for the purpose for which it was obtained, (b) archival purposes or replacement if the original copy is lost, destroyed or unusable, and (c) maintenance or repair of a machine — [Gazette text](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf)
- s.20(3) makes void any contract term that restricts or prevents an act the Act permits. This is significant: a EULA cannot override the exceptions — [Gazette text](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf)
- s.50(8) lets a person who has lawfully obtained the right to use a program circumvent an access control on a portion of it "for the sole purpose of identifying and analysing those elements" that are necessary for interoperability of an independently created program and were not previously readily available, to the extent the acts are not infringement. s.50(9) defines interoperability as the ability of programs to exchange information and use it. (The text says "For the purpose of subsection (9)", an apparent drafting slip for (8).) — [Gazette text](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf)
- "Computer programme" is defined in the interpretation section and listed among literary works. Video games are not named — [Gazette text](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf)

**Kenya (Copyright Act No. 12 of 2001, amended by the Copyright (Amendment) Act 2019)**
- s.26A was added in 2019. It replaced s.26 of the 2001 Act, which had similar computer-program provisions — [CIPIT, Strathmore University](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/what-can-i-get-away-with-exceptions-to-computer-programme-copyright-infringement/)
- s.26A(1) applies fair dealing (Part A of the Second Schedule) to computer programs. s.26A(2) lets a person in lawful possession make copies to correct errors, as a backup, to test suitability for their use, or for any purpose not prohibited by the licence. s.26A(3) permits decompiling, or converting to another language or notation, to obtain information needed for the program to operate with other programs. s.26A(4) says copies may be used only for the purpose obtained and must be destroyed when lawful possession ends — [CIPIT](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/what-can-i-get-away-with-exceptions-to-computer-programme-copyright-infringement/)
- The 2019 Act also extended copyright duration, strengthened enforcement and added TPM anti-circumvention rules — [Zigram summary](https://www.zigram.tech/resources/the-kenya-copyright-amendment-act-2019/)

**South Africa (Copyright Act 98 of 1978; Copyright Amendment Bill B13F-2017)**
- The current Act protects "computer programs" as a separate category, added by the 1992 amendment. s.19B gives lawful users only narrow exceptions (some fair-dealing subsections apply, plus a backup copy). There is no decompilation or interoperability exception (not confirmed) — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/)
- The Copyright Amendment Bill [B13F-2017] would add s.12A (open fair use), a new s.19B with interoperability and decompilation permissions, s.19C (libraries, archives, museums), s.22A (orphan works) and TPM provisions (proposed ss.28O–28Q) — (not confirmed)
- The President referred the Bill and the Performers' Protection Amendment Bill [B24F-2016] to the Constitutional Court under s.79(4)(b) of the Constitution (CCT 306/24). The Court heard the matter on 21–22 May 2025 — [GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/court-copyright-amendment-bill/); [acts.co.za](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2026/07/concourt-ruling-copyright-performers-protection-bills)
- Judgment was given in 2026, cited as [2026] ZACC 26. One source dated it 26 June 2026 (search snippet) — [GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/constitutional-court-copyright/); [ENSafrica](https://www.ensafrica.com/news/detail/12103/a-decade-in-the-making-constitutional-court-r). The exact date is not confirmed from the judgment itself.
- The Court declared the proposed s.12D(1)–(5) (reproduction for educational and academic activities, within clause 18) unconstitutional, as an arbitrary deprivation of property under s.25(1) of the Constitution. Parliament's Portfolio Committee on Trade, Industry and Competition will limit its review and public participation to those subsections and the matching Performers' Protection provisions — [acts.co.za, 9 July 2026](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2026/07/concourt-ruling-copyright-performers-protection-bills)
- Commentators call the ruling "landmark but incomplete" — [GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/constitutional-court-copyright/)
- An earlier, separate case, Blind SA v Minister of Trade, Industry and Competition ([2022] ZACC 33), read an accessible-format exception into the current Act (not confirmed)

**Egypt (Law No. 82 of 2002 on the Protection of Intellectual Property Rights, Book III)** (all not confirmed) — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/)
- Art. 171 lists limitations. They include personal use and a lawful owner making one copy of a computer program for backup or archival use, or for use with the program. There is no decompilation or interoperability exception and no general fair use.
- The Executive Regulations to Book III were issued by Prime Ministerial Decree 497 of 2005 (not confirmed).

**Israel (Copyright Act 2007, in force 25 May 2008)** (all not confirmed) — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/)
- s.19 is an open US-style fair use clause with four factors, and it applies to software.
- s.24 is a software exception. A lawful possessor may copy a program for backup, for maintenance, for security of the computer system, to correct errors and, where the information is not otherwise readily available, to obtain information needed to make an independently created program interoperable. The copy must be destroyed when possession ends. Exact sub-paragraph numbering is not confirmed.

**Turkey (Law No. 5846 on Intellectual and Artistic Works, 5 December 1951, FSEK)** (all not confirmed) — [mevzuat.gov.tr](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuatmetin/1.3.5846.pdf)
- Art. 38 (amended, 2001 harmonisation with the EU Software Directive) permits a lawful acquirer to make copies and adaptations necessary for use, including error correction. It also permits a backup copy, observing, studying and testing the program to find its underlying ideas and principles, and decompilation to get interface information for interoperability. The decompilation conditions mirror the EU's: information not otherwise available, use limited to interoperability, no use for a substantially similar program.

**UAE (Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2021 on Copyrights and Neighbouring Rights)**
- In force on 2 January 2022, repealing Federal Law No. 7 of 2002. It adds "smart applications, computer programmes and applications, databases" as a protected category — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2022/the-new-uae-copyright-law-2021-key-takeaways); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/de/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/10/protecting-online-data-and-ip-new-copyright-law-provides-clarity-for-uae-businesses)
- Fines for using a program without a licence run from AED 100,000 to AED 1,000,000 (previously AED 30,000). Authors cannot withdraw software from circulation — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2022/the-new-uae-copyright-law-2021-key-takeaways)
- The exceptions article, believed to be art. 22, includes one backup or use copy of a computer program made by its lawful owner. Commentary lists "fair use" and new exceptions as a topic of the law — [creationbc summary](https://www.creationbc.com/corporate-laws-and-legislations/law-no-38-of-2021-on-copyrights-and-neighbouring-rights). The article number and whether decompilation is covered are **not confirmed**.

**Saudi Arabia (new Copyright Law 2026, replacing the 2003 law)**
- Royal Decree No. M/169 (2 February 2026; one source dates the decree 14/8/1447H) and Cabinet Decision No. 560/1447 (27 January 2026). Published in the Official Gazette (Umm Al-Qura) on 13 February 2026. In force on 12 August 2026. Repeals the 2003 Copyright Law — [Baker McKenzie, July 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework); [Origami](https://origami.sa/en/blog/saudi-copyright-law-2026-software-digital-content/)
- The Saudi Authority for Intellectual Property (SAIP) must issue implementing regulations within 180 days. They apply from entry into force — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/saudiarabia/copyright/1751404/saudi-arabias-new-copyright-law-key-changes-and-practical-steps-for-businesses)
- Lawful software users get permitted acts covering backup, maintenance and error correction, interoperability with other systems, and security testing. Sources give no article numbers and do not mention decompilation by name — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework); [Origami](https://origami.sa/en/blog/saudi-copyright-law-2026-software-digital-content/)
- Art. 26(4) reportedly permits copying a lawfully obtained, published work to develop AI products — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/saudiarabia/copyright/1751404/saudi-arabias-new-copyright-law-key-changes-and-practical-steps-for-businesses)
- The old regime was the Copyright Law of 2003 (Royal Decree M/41, 1424H). Its implementing regulations were published in Umm Al-Qura on 4 June 2004 and in force from 2 August 2004 — [AGIP](https://www.agip.com/News/en/news/25). Its art. 15 limitations included a single backup copy of a program (not confirmed).

**Russia (Civil Code, Part IV, Federal Law No. 230-FZ of 18 December 2006, in force 1 January 2008)** (all not confirmed) — [consultant.ru](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_64629/)
- Art. 1280(1): a person lawfully possessing a copy may, without consent or payment, adapt the program so it works on their hardware, correct obvious errors, and make a backup copy.
- Art. 1280(2): decompilation is allowed to achieve interoperability of an independently developed program. The conditions are that the information was not previously available, the acts are limited to the necessary parts, and the information is not passed on, not used for other purposes, and not used to build a substantially similar program.
- Art. 1280(3): the lawful possessor may study, research or test the program's functioning to determine its ideas and principles, while loading, displaying, running, transmitting or storing it.
- Art. 1280.1 (added in 2014) covers the rights of an owner of a copy of a program (not confirmed).

**Ukraine (Law No. 2811-IX "On Copyright and Related Rights", adopted 1 December 2022)**
- Adopted on 1 December 2022 and published in Vidomosti VRU 2023, No. 57, art. 166 — [zakon.rada.gov.ua](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2811-20). Entry into force on 1 January 2023 is not confirmed.
- Art. 25 (free use of computer programs). Part 1 lets a lawful user make a backup copy, decompile for interoperability, and observe and study the program. Decompilation is allowed only if the interoperability information was not previously available from other sources, and the information may not be passed on except as needed for interoperability. Part 2 permits reproduction, translation, adaptation and other changes needed for proper use, including error correction, unless the contract says otherwise — [zakon.rada.gov.ua](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2811-20)
- The previous law (No. 3792-XII of 23 December 1993) had art. 24 on free copying, modification and decompilation of programs — [kodeksy.com.ua](https://kodeksy.com.ua/pro_avtors_ke_pravo_i_sumizhni_prava/statja-24.htm)

**Switzerland (Federal Act on Copyright and Related Rights, URG/LDA, 9 October 1992, SR 231.1)** (all not confirmed) — [fedlex](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1993/1798_1798_1798/en)
- Art. 21(1): a lawful user may obtain information on interfaces with independently developed programs by decompiling, or have a third party do it. Art. 21(2): the information may be used only to develop, maintain and use interoperable programs, and only if this does not unreasonably impair normal exploitation of the program or the rightholder's legitimate interests.
- Art. 24(2): a lawful user may make one backup copy, and this cannot be excluded by contract.
- Art. 19(4): the private-use exception does not apply to computer programs. Art. 12(2): a sold copy of software may be used and resold.
- There is no separate "observe, study, test" article like the EU's art. 5(3). How Swiss law covers this is not confirmed.

**Norway (Act relating to copyright in works and other subject matter, Åndsverkloven, LOV-2018-06-15-40, in force 1 July 2018)** (all not confirmed) — [lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2018-06-15-40)
- § 41: a lawful acquirer may make the copies and changes needed to use the program as intended, including error correction, and a backup copy. They may also observe, study and test how the program works to find its underlying ideas and principles.
- § 42: decompilation is allowed to get information needed for interoperability of an independently created program, under the EU conditions. The text implements the EU Software Directive through the EEA Agreement.

### Inferences
- In every jurisdiction with an EU-style rule, "observe, study, test" is the widest permission. Decompilation is narrow and tied to interoperability between programs. Rebuilding a whole game in new code is not obviously "interoperability" in the statutory sense in any of these countries. GameFrame's strongest footing is probably observation of a lawfully owned copy plus writing new code that reads the user's own files, with nothing distributed.
- Nigeria's s.20(3) contract override and Switzerland's non-excludable backup copy are pro-user features. Russia, Ukraine and Norway also follow the EU rule that contract terms contrary to the decompilation exception are void (not confirmed for each).
- Countries with only backup or use exceptions (Egypt, probably the UAE, and South Africa under its current Act) give no specific cover for decompiling. Israel's open fair use (s.19) and Nigeria's open-list fair dealing may give some room beyond the software-specific clauses.

### Gaps
- Exact sub-section numbers for Israel s.24, Turkey art. 38, Swiss art. 21/24, Norway §§ 41–42, Russia art. 1280, Egypt art. 171 and UAE art. 22 are not confirmed against the official text.
- Saudi 2026 law: the article numbers for the software exceptions, and whether "interoperability" covers decompilation, are not confirmed. The implementing regulations' content is not known.
- South Africa: the exact text of the Bill's s.19B in B13F-2017 and the full holdings of [2026] ZACC 26 on the other clauses (fair use s.12A and others) are not covered. The Bill was not law as of 9 July 2026.

## 2. Anti-circumvention of technological protection measures (TPMs) and exceptions

### Takeaway
Most of these countries ban circumventing TPMs. Exceptions for interoperability, research or preservation vary. Nigeria has the clearest written exceptions: interoperability analysis, security work, and library acquisition checks. Ukraine and Switzerland tie the ban to the free-use exceptions. Israel, unusually, has no general anti-circumvention law. South Africa's current Act has no TPM provisions; the pending Bill would add them.

### Cited Findings
- **Nigeria** s.50(1) bans knowingly circumventing a TPM that effectively protects access, "subject to the provisions of Part II" (the exceptions). s.50(2) bans trafficking in circumvention tools. s.50(4)–(5) let a non-profit library, archive or educational institution gain access only to decide in good faith whether to acquire a copy, where no identical copy is available in another form. s.50(6) excludes lawful investigation, information security, intelligence and computer security measures. s.50(7) says the section does not affect any exception in the Act. s.50(8) is the interoperability exception (section 1 above). s.50(3)(c) excludes measures that only control access for non-infringing purposes. s.52 gives civil remedies. Criminal penalties apply — [Gazette text](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf); [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2023/06/A-New-Dawn-for-Copyright-Protection-in-Nigeria-Final.pdf)
- **Ukraine** art. 53(2)(5) lists circumvention of TPMs as an infringement, including card-sharing and making or distributing tools. art. 53(3) says those rules do not apply to the free-use cases in arts. 22–29 and 43–44, which include art. 25 (software) and art. 24 (libraries) — [zakon.rada.gov.ua](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2811-20) (not confirmed word for word against the official text)
- **Kenya**: the 2019 Amendment Act added TPM anti-circumvention rules (digital rights management) — [Zigram](https://www.zigram.tech/resources/the-kenya-copyright-amendment-act-2019/). Section numbers and exceptions are not confirmed.
- **Russia**: Civil Code art. 1299 bans removing or circumventing TPMs and dealing in tools. There is believed to be a carve-out where the law allows use without consent (not confirmed) — [consultant.ru](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_64629/)
- **Switzerland**: URG art. 39a bans circumventing effective TPMs. Art. 39a(4) says the ban does not apply where circumvention is done solely for a legally permitted use. Art. 39b set up a monitoring office for TPMs (not confirmed) — [fedlex](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1993/1798_1798_1798/en)
- **Norway**: Åndsverkloven has TPM provisions implementing the EU InfoSoc Directive. Under the EU model, TPMs on computer programs follow the Software Directive (tool trafficking) rather than the InfoSoc rules. Exact section numbers are not confirmed — [lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2018-06-15-40)
- **Turkey**: Law 5846 art. 72 makes it an offence to produce or deal in tools whose purpose is to remove or circumvent protection of computer programs (not confirmed) — [mevzuat](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuatmetin/1.3.5846.pdf)
- **Egypt**: Law 82/2002 art. 181 makes it an offence to remove or disable technical protection (such as encryption) (not confirmed)
- **UAE** (38/2021) and **Saudi Arabia** (2026 law): both include TPM protection. The Saudi law introduces TPMs as a new concept, and sources give no details on exceptions — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework)
- **Israel**: the Copyright Act 2007 contains no anti-circumvention provisions (not confirmed)
- **South Africa**: the current Act has no TPM provisions (not confirmed). The Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002 s.86 creates offences for unauthorised access and for devices that overcome security measures (not confirmed).

### Inferences
- A tool that reads a user's own unencrypted game files touches no TPM. Where a game's files are encrypted or behind DRM, the rules differ sharply by country. Nigeria and Ukraine have express carve-outs that track the software exceptions. Israel has no TPM ban. Turkey and Egypt focus on tools and devices.

### Gaps
- Kenya's TPM section number and exceptions; Russia art. 1299 exceptions; the UAE TPM article; Norway's TPM section numbers.

## 3. Preservation exceptions for libraries, archives and museums

### Takeaway
Nigeria, Ukraine and Switzerland have express preservation-copy exceptions that cover any work type, and so software. Ukraine and Nigeria also let institutions copy works they cannot get through trade. Norway follows the EU model. Russia allows restoration and preservation copies. Others have older, narrower library rules.

### Cited Findings
- **Nigeria** s.25(1): archives, libraries, museums and galleries may, for non-commercial purposes, (a) make and distribute copies as part of their ordinary activities, (b) copy works in their collection for backup and preservation, (c) copy a missing part from another institution, (d) copy a work into a chosen format if it cannot reasonably be bought in that format, and (e) copy a work where permission cannot be obtained after reasonable effort or the work is not available in the trade. s.25(2) lets such copies be lent, or used for study or research on the premises. s.20(1)(r) allows making works available on dedicated terminals in libraries, museums and archives — [Gazette text](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf)
- **Ukraine** art. 24(1)(2) permits reproduction to preserve or replace a lost, damaged or unusable copy, where another way is impossible — [zakon.rada.gov.ua](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2811-20)
- **Switzerland** URG art. 24(1bis) (2008 revision): public libraries, educational institutions, museums and archives may make copies needed to secure and preserve their holdings, if not for commercial gain. Art. 24e (2020 revision) allows inventory images (not confirmed) — [fedlex](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1993/1798_1798_1798/en)
- **Russia** Civil Code art. 1275: libraries and archives may copy works to restore, replace or preserve them. Digital copies for preservation were added in 2014 (not confirmed)
- **Saudi Arabia**: the 2026 law expands the library and educational exception. Sources do not mention archives or preservation — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework)
- **South Africa**: the pending Bill's s.19C would add library, archive and museum exceptions. It is not yet law (not confirmed)
- **Israel** s.30: libraries and archives of prescribed types may copy works in their collection for preservation or replacement (not confirmed)
- **Norway**: the Act has library and archive copying for preservation (believed to be around § 30; number not confirmed) — [lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2018-06-15-40)

### Inferences
- Nigeria's s.25(1)(e) (copy where permission cannot be obtained or the work is out of trade) is one of the broadest preservation clauses covered here. It suits abandoned games held by institutions. It does not help a private individual.

### Gaps
- Kenya, Egypt, Turkey and UAE library and archive provisions are not covered.
- Whether legal deposit laws cover games (for example, Norway's National Library under the Legal Deposit Act 1989) is not confirmed.

## 4. Copyright term for software and games

### Takeaway
Most of these countries use life plus 70 years. Switzerland uses life plus 50 for software. Egypt, the UAE and Kenya use life plus 50. South Africa runs computer programs for 50 years from first release. Nigeria treats audiovisual works for 50 years from release, which matters if a game is classed as audiovisual.

### Cited Findings
- **Nigeria** s.19(1)(a): literary works (computer programs are literary works) last 70 years after the author's death. s.19(1)(c): audiovisual works last 50 years from first being made available. s.19(2): anonymous works last 70 years from being made available — [Gazette text](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf)
- **Ukraine** art. 31(2): 70 years from 1 January of the year after the author's death. Programs follow the general rule. art. 33 creates a 25-year sui generis right for non-original computer-generated objects. art. 21 creates a 15-year database right — [zakon.rada.gov.ua](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2811-20)
- **Russia** art. 1281: life plus 70 (not confirmed)
- **Turkey** art. 27: life plus 70 (not confirmed)
- **Israel** s.38: life plus 70 (not confirmed)
- **Norway** § 11: life plus 70 (not confirmed)
- **Switzerland** art. 29(2)(a): 50 years after death for computer programs; 70 for other works (not confirmed)
- **Egypt** art. 160: life plus 50 (not confirmed)
- **UAE** (38/2021): life plus 50 (not confirmed)
- **Saudi Arabia**: the 2026 law "largely preserves" duration rules. The old law used life plus 50 (not confirmed) — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework)
- **South Africa** s.3(2): computer programs last 50 years from the end of the year first made available or made (not confirmed)
- **Kenya**: the 2019 Act "extends the duration of copyright protection" — [Zigram](https://www.zigram.tech/resources/the-kenya-copyright-amendment-act-2019/). The new term is not confirmed.

### Inferences
- No game from the 1970s onwards is out of copyright in any of these countries.

### Gaps
- Kenya's post-2019 term.
- The Saudi 2026 term for software.

## 5. Orphan works schemes

### Takeaway
Ukraine (art. 29) and Switzerland (art. 22b) have formal orphan works rules. Norway likely does under the EEA. Saudi Arabia's 2026 law gives orphan works to a state body. Nigeria's library clause s.25(1)(e) works as an informal orphan-works permission for institutions. No scheme is known for the others.

### Cited Findings
- **Ukraine** art. 29 allows use of orphan works for digitisation, cataloguing, preservation or restoration after a diligent search fails. art. 1(51) defines "orphan work" — [zakon.rada.gov.ua](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2811-20)
- **Saudi Arabia**: the General Commission for Guardianship of Trust Funds will handle orphan works and works whose authors die without heirs — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/saudi-arabia-new-copyright-law-modernises-ksa-ip-framework)
- **Nigeria** s.25(1)(e) (section 3 above) — [Gazette text](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf)
- **Switzerland** art. 22b (2020 revision, in force 1 April 2020): orphan works in heritage institutions' holdings may be used through collecting societies. It was widened from audiovisual works to all work types (not confirmed)
- **South Africa**: the Bill's s.22A would add orphan works. It is not yet law (not confirmed)

### Gaps
- Norway's orphan works section; Russia, Turkey, Israel, Egypt, UAE and Kenya (none known, not covered exhaustively).

## 6. Notable cases on game piracy, mod chips, emulators, ROM sites or reverse engineering

### Takeaway
Little reported case law exists in this region on emulators or reverse engineering. The best-known case is South Africa's *Nintendo v Golden China* (1996), which classed video games as "cinematograph films". Russia has used site blocking heavily against piracy sites.

### Cited Findings
- **South Africa**: *Golden China TV Game Centre v Nintendo Co Ltd* 1997 (1) SA 405 (A), Appellate Division, 1996. Video games were held to be "cinematograph films" under the Copyright Act, so importing unlicensed cartridges infringed (not confirmed; widely cited)
- **Russia**: RuTracker was permanently blocked by the Moscow City Court in 2015/2016 under the anti-piracy "eternal block" law (not confirmed)
- **Nigeria**: s.50 criminal penalties include a minimum fine of N1,000,000 or at least five years' imprisonment for aiding circumvention — [Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2023/06/A-New-Dawn-for-Copyright-Protection-in-Nigeria-Final.pdf)

### Gaps
- No cases are known on emulators, ROM sites or mod chips in any of these twelve countries. Israel, Turkey, Switzerland and Norway may have mod-chip or console-piracy criminal cases that are not covered here.

## 7. Practical realities (sanctions, region locks, availability)

### Takeaway
Since March 2022 many Western publishers and stores have stopped sales in Russia and Belarus, and payments were disrupted in Russia and Ukraine. Russia is now drafting its own game law with licensing-like controls. Game availability in Gulf states is shaped by content classification.

### Cited Findings
- In March 2022, GOG and Microsoft halted sales in Russia. Ubisoft, EA, Epic Games and Activision Blizzard suspended sales and microtransactions. CD Projekt suspended supply in Russia and Belarus. Steam users in Russia lost the main payment methods because of US sanctions — [Massively OP, 31 March 2022](https://massivelyop.com/2022/03/31/international-sanctions-cause-steam-payment-snarl-ups-for-indie-game-devs-in-russia-and-ukraine/); [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.Net/gaming/news/valve-suspends-payments-to-developers-in-ukraine-and-russia)
- Valve's bank required intermediary bank details for payments to Russia and Ukraine and stopped payments to Belarus. Ukrainian developers were also affected — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/steam-ukraine-russia-payments/)
- A draft Russian law "On the Development and Distribution of Video Games" went to the State Duma in December 2024. It includes state support and compliance rules for foreign companies, and critics compare it to China's game licensing. As of the latest report it was at first reading. A 2025 proposal would fine up to 5 million rubles for prohibited content. Deputy Yana Lantratova proposed a 10% tax on foreign games in September 2025 — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/21441/russia-eyes-digital-sovereignty-with-proposed-video-game-licensing-system); [Kesarev, 19 September 2025](https://kesarev.partners/insights_19092025); [bne IntelliNews](https://bne.eu/russia-considering-crackdown-on-destructive-video-games-361855)
- The 2022–2026 status of the Russian game bill is unclear. Its adoption had been projected for late 2025 — [Kesarev](https://kesarev.partners/insights_19092025)

### Inferences
- In countries where a game is no longer sold, owning an original copy and studying it is often the only way to keep it playable. This is a neutral point the site can make without legal claims.

### Gaps
- Whether game consoles are on Russia's 2022 "parallel import" list (believed so, not confirmed).
- Region locking and classification regimes in Saudi Arabia (GCAM) and the UAE (Media Council) are not covered.

## 8. Who does game preservation in these regions

### Takeaway
Dedicated game preservation institutions are rare in this region. Russia has the best-known one, the Museum of Soviet Arcade Machines. African work is mostly new games about heritage rather than archives of old games.

### Cited Findings
- **Nigeria**: Goethe-Institut Nigeria's "Echoes Game Jam" (2025), run with AfricaComicade, built games from artefacts in the National Museum, Lagos. Winners are to be installed there with the National Commission for Museums and Monuments, Games Connect Africa and EUNIC. It is part of the "Dreaming New Worlds" project — [ThisDay, 10 April 2025](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2025/04/10/goethe-institut-africacomicade-conclude-echoes-game-jam-honouring-top-innovators/)
- **Russia**: the Museum of Soviet Arcade Machines (Muzey sovetskikh igrovykh avtomatov) has run in Moscow and St Petersburg since 2007 (not confirmed)

### Gaps
- No dedicated African, Middle Eastern, Turkish, Israeli, Swiss, Norwegian or Ukrainian game archive is confirmed. Possible leads include Norway's National Library and Norsk Teknisk Museum, Swiss institutions such as Memoriav (audiovisual, not games-specific), and Israeli and Turkish university game labs.
