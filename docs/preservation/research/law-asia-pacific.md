# Notes: South and Southeast Asia and Oceania

Scope: India, Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand, Vietnam, Australia and New Zealand. This is not legal advice.

## 1. Statutory exceptions for reverse engineering, decompiling, and observing/studying/testing software

### Takeaway
Most of these jurisdictions have some exception. Australia, New Zealand, Singapore and India have the full set: back-up, observe/study/test, and interoperability copying or decompilation. Singapore's and New Zealand's decompilation rules follow the EU model, with strict conditions. India's are shorter and looser. The Philippines treats interoperability decompilation as a form of fair use. Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Vietnam have back-up and research-type exceptions, but none of them has a known express decompilation exception.

### Cited Findings

**India: Copyright Act 1957**
- s.52(1)(aa): a lawful possessor may copy or adapt a computer programme "in order to utilise the computer programme for the purpose for which it was supplied", or make back-up copies "purely as a temporary protection against loss, destruction or damage". — [lawgist.in s.52 (consolidated text)](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/52)
- s.52(1)(ab): "the doing of any act necessary to obtain information essential for operating interoperability of an independently created computer programme with other programmes by a lawful possessor of a computer programme provided that such information is not otherwise readily available". — [lawgist.in s.52](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/52)
- s.52(1)(ac): "the observation, study or test of functioning of the computer programme in order to determine the ideas and principles which underline any elements of the programme while performing such acts necessary for the functions for which the computer programme was supplied". — [lawgist.in s.52](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/52)
- s.52(1)(ad): "the making of copies or adaptation of the computer programme from a personally legally obtained copy for non-commercial personal use". — [lawgist.in s.52](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/52)
- Clauses (ab)–(ad) were inserted after (aa) by the Copyright (Amendment) Act 1999, in force 15 January 2000 (Notification S.O. 50(E), 13 January 2000). — [Casemine, Copyright (Amendment) Act 1999](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5f5f124f9fca195c05ccefb4)
- The general fair-dealing clause s.52(1)(a) excludes computer programmes. Clauses (aa)–(ad) apply only to them. — [Singhania & Partners](https://singhania.in/blog/applicability-of-section-52-of-the-copyrights-act-to-specific-works)
- India's text lacks the EU-style limits (copy only what is needed, no disclosure, no substantially similar program). (search snippet) — comparison of [Irish CRRA 2000 s.81](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/2000/en/act/pub/0028/sec0081.html) against the Indian text above.

**Singapore: Copyright Act 2021 (Part 5, Division 7 "Use of computer programs", ss.237–242)**
- s.237: back-up copy, made by or for the owner of the original copy, not from an infringing copy, for use only if the original is lost, destroyed or unusable. — [LawPlayer, CA2021 Part 5 Division 7](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/section/part-5.division-7)
- s.238: copy or adaptation that is an essential step in running the program on a machine. — [LawPlayer Div 7](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/section/part-5.division-7)
- s.239 "Decompilation of computer program by lawful user": nine conditions. The program must be in a low-level language. X must be a lawful user. Decompiling must be necessary to get information to create an independent program that interoperates. The information must not be readily available otherwise. X must limit decompiling to that purpose. X must not use or supply the information beyond it. X must not use it to make a substantially similar program or to infringe. s.239(3) defines decompiling as converting low-level to higher-level code, with incidental copying. — [LawPlayer s.239](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/239)
- s.240 "Observing, studying and testing of computer program by lawful user": allowed to understand the ideas and principles behind any element, while doing acts the user is entitled to do (loading, running, storing). — [LawPlayer Div 7](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/section/part-5.division-7)
- s.241: copying or adapting where necessary for lawful use; s.241(3) includes error correction. s.242: "lawful user" means a person with a right to use the program, under licence or otherwise. — [LawPlayer Div 7](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/section/part-5.division-7)
- The new computational data analysis exception (text and data mining) covers commercial and non-commercial use. It needs "lawful access" to the first copy. It cannot be excluded or modified by contract. Any term that tries to exclude it is void, including in existing contracts. — [Bird & Bird, 2021](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2021/singapore/coming-up-in-singapore-new-copyright-exception-for-text-and-data-mining); [Conventus Law, 21 Sept 2021](https://conventuslaw.com/report/coming-up-in-singapore-new-copyright-exception-for/)
- Singapore's earlier reverse-engineering provisions were enacted in 2004 and came into force in 2005. — [Singapore Journal of Legal Studies, 2005](https://law.nus.edu.sg/sjls/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2024/07/1873-2005-sjls-jul-234.pdf)

**Malaysia: Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332)**
- s.40: making a back-up reproduction of a computer program does not infringe "despite any contrary contract term". It must be made by or for the owner of the original copy, only to replace a lost, destroyed or unusable copy. It does not cover back-ups made from an infringing copy, or against an express written direction given before the copy was acquired. — [WIPO Lex, Act 332 consolidated to 30 June 2022 (unofficial updated text)](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/597931)
- s.13(2) includes the commercial rental of computer programs where the program is not the essential object of the rental, and transient and incidental network copies. — [WIPO Lex Act 332](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/597931)
- The only known interoperability carve-out is in the TPM provision s.36A (see section 2). No general decompilation exception is known. — [WIPO Lex Act 332](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/597931)
- Latest amending act: Act A1645, in force 18 March 2022, with some sections in force 30 June 2022. — [WIPO Lex record](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/zh/legislation/details/22674)
- MyIPO published a consultation paper on 3 July 2026 proposing further reform of the Act. — [Baker McKenzie, July 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/malaysia-public-consultation-on-proposed-copyright-act-1987-reforms)

**Indonesia: Law No. 28 of 2014 on Copyright (UU 28/2014)**
- Pasal 40(1)(r) lists "permainan video" (video games) as a protected kind of work. One commentary says Indonesia is unusual in naming video games explicitly. — [hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/klinik/a/perlindungan-hak-cipta-atas-program-komputer-cl4211); [UII journal](https://journal.uii.ac.id/JIPRO/article/download/17689/11696)
- Pasal 45(1): a lawful user may make one (1) copy or adaptation of a computer program without permission, if it is used for (a) research and development of that program, or (b) archive or back-up of a lawfully obtained program, to prevent loss, damage or inoperability. Pasal 45(3): once use of the program ends, the copy or adaptation must be destroyed. — [pasal.id, Pasal 45](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-28-tahun-2014/pasal-45)
- Pasal 46(2)(d) excludes computer programs from the personal-use copying exception. This sits awkwardly next to Pasal 45. — [hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/klinik/a/perlindungan-hak-cipta-atas-program-komputer-cl4211)
- The law has no definition of a video game and no rules specific to them. — [UII journal](https://journal.uii.ac.id/JIPRO/article/download/17689/11696)

**Philippines: Intellectual Property Code (RA 8293) as amended by RA 10372**
- s.185.1 (amended by RA 10372, approved 28 February 2013): "Decompilation, which is understood here to be the reproduction of the code and translation of the forms of a computer program to achieve the interoperability of an independently created computer program with other programs may also constitute fair use under the criteria established by this section, to the extent that such decompilation is done for the purpose of obtaining the information necessary to achieve such interoperability." — [LawPhil, RA 10372](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2013/ra_10372_2013.html)
- s.189.1: the lawful owner of a computer program may make one back-up copy or adaptation without authorisation, where necessary to use the program or for archival purposes, to replace a lost, destroyed or unusable copy. — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/can-a-computer-program-be-reproduced-for-back-up/)

**Thailand: Copyright Act B.E. 2537 (1994), as amended**
- s.35 lists acts on a computer program that do not infringe, if done without a view to profit and if s.32 paragraph 1 is met: (1) research or study; (2) use for the benefit of the owner of the copy; (3) criticism with acknowledgment; (4) news reporting with acknowledgment; (5) copies "in a reasonable quantity by a person who has rightfully bought" a copy, for maintenance or to prevent loss; (6) court or official use; (7) exam use; (8) adaptation when needed for use; (9) copies kept for public reference or research. — [Wikisource translation of the 1994 Act](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:Copyright_Act,_2537_BE)
- s.32 paragraph 1 is a three-step-style test: the act must not conflict with the owner's ordinary exploitation and must not unreasonably harm the owner's legitimate interests. — [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:Copyright_Act,_2537_BE)
- The 2015 amendment added s.32/2: copies made in a computer system that are needed for it to operate normally do not infringe. — [Tilleke & Gibbins, 2015 amendments](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thai-copyright-act-amendments-updating-law-digital-age)
- Amendments: 2015 (in force August 2015), 2019 (Marrakesh, in force March 2019), and 2022 (Government Gazette 24 February 2022, in force 23 August 2022). — [Tilleke & Gibbins, 2022 amendment](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/the-2022-amendment-of-thailands-copyright-act-the-good-the-bad-and-the-backstory)

**Vietnam: Law on Intellectual Property (Law 50/2005/QH11, amended 2009, 2019, 2022 and 2025)**
- Law No. 07/2022/QH15 was passed 16 June 2022 and took effect 1 January 2023. It defines computer programs and protects them as literary works (Art. 22). — [ASEAN Briefing](https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/vietnams-intellectual-property-law-new-amendments/)
- Art. 22(1) as amended in 2022: a user with a lawful copy may make one back-up copy to replace it if the original is deleted, damaged or unusable, and may not pass it to others. — [LawNet](https://lawnet.vn/en/laws/vietnam-can-organizations-and-individuals-allowed-to-use-a-copy-of-a-computer-program-make-a-backup-25583.html)
- 2025 amendments were passed 10 December 2025 and took effect 1 April 2026. Back-up is allowed only to replace a deleted or failed original and cannot be transferred. For SaaS or online-platform software, back-up rights depend on the licence terms. A new exception allows use of lawfully published, publicly accessible text and data for scientific research, testing and AI training, if it does not unreasonably harm rights holders, subject to government regulations. — [Baker McKenzie, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/alerts/2025/12/vietnam-2025-ip-law-overhaul)
- Implementing decrees are reported as Decree 100/2026 and Decree 134/2026. — [Rouse, 2026](https://rouse.com/insights/news/2026/vietnam-ip-ai-law-updates-compliance-strategies-for-ai-training-and-book-digitization)

**Australia: Copyright Act 1968 (Cth), Part III Division 4A**
- Section headings, current compilation (April 2026): s.47B "Reproduction for normal use or study of computer programs"; s.47C "Back-up copy of computer programs"; s.47D "Reproducing computer programs to make interoperable products"; s.47E "Reproducing computer programs to correct errors"; s.47F "Reproducing computer programs for security testing"; s.47H "Agreements excluding operation of certain provisions". — [Federal Register of Legislation, Copyright Act 1968, compilation C2026C00138](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1968A00063/latest/text)
- Background: Australia loosened its software copyright law to allow interoperability reverse engineering (1999–2000 reform). — [Computerworld NZ](https://www2.computerworld.co.nz/article/104071/australia_loosens_software_copyright_law/)

**New Zealand: Copyright Act 1994**
- s.80A: a lawful user of a program in a low-level language may decompile it, but only where decompiling is needed to get interoperability information for an independent program. It is not allowed where the information is readily available without decompiling, or where it is used to create a substantially similar program. — [NZLII s.80A](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/ca1994133/s80a.html)
- s.80B: a lawful user may copy or adapt a program where needed for lawful use, such as fixing an error, if no working copy is available at a reasonable price and time. — [NZLII s.80B](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/ca1994133/s80b.html)
- s.80C: a lawful user may observe, study or test a program's functioning to work out its ideas and principles, while doing acts they are entitled to do (loading, running). — [NZLII s.80C](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/ca1994133/s80c.html)
- s.80D: "Certain contractual terms relating to use of computer programs have no effect". — [WIPO Lex, NZ Copyright Act](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/449855)
- Sections 80A–80D were inserted by the Copyright (New Technologies) Amendment Act 2008. — [NZLII s.80B history](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/ca1994133/s80b.html); [NZLII 2008 Act s.43](https://mail.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/ctaa2008372/s43.html)

### Inferences
- For a harness user who studies a game they own and writes new code that reads their own files: the observe/study/test exceptions (Aus s.47B, NZ s.80C, Sing s.240, India s.52(1)(ac)) fit running and watching the game best. Decompilation exceptions are all tied to interoperability of an "independently created" program. Whether a clean-room reimplementation of the whole game counts as "interoperable" with the original data files is untested in these countries.
- Singapore and NZ bar using decompiled information to make a "substantially similar program". A faithful game reimplementation may run into that limit, even if it ships no original code.
- India's s.52(1)(ad) (copies or adaptations from a personally legally obtained copy, for non-commercial personal use) is unusually broad. It seems helpful for personal study, but it does not cover distributing the result.
- Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Vietnam give no clear decompilation right. Thailand's s.35(1) "research or study" (not for profit, three-step test) and Indonesia's Pasal 45(1)(a) "research and development" copy are the nearest hooks.

### Gaps
- The body text of Australia ss.47B–47F is not confirmed against the official text. The following is not confirmed. They came from the Copyright Amendment (Computer Programs) Act 1999. s.47D allows reproduction or adaptation to get information needed to make an independent program or article that interoperates, only to the extent necessary, where the information is not readily available. s.47H makes contract terms that exclude 47B(3), 47C, 47D, 47E and 47F void. The commencement is believed to be 23 February 2000.
- New Zealand s.80 (back-up copy) and the exact text of s.80D were not confirmed.
- Singapore: which computer-program exceptions ss.186–187 protect from contract override is not confirmed.
- Malaysia s.13(2)(a) fair dealing (research, private study, criticism, review, news) was not confirmed from the text.
- Vietnam: no decompilation or interoperability exception is known. The 2025 law's number (possibly Law 131/2025/QH15) is unconfirmed.
- Indonesia Pasal 45(2) text is not confirmed.

## 2. Anti-circumvention of technological protection measures (TPMs) and their exceptions

### Takeaway
Every jurisdiction except Vietnam (where the details are unclear) now has TPM rules. Australia, Singapore, Malaysia and NZ have an express interoperability exception. India's s.65A is unusual and narrow: it is criminal, it needs an intention to infringe, and it excuses circumvention "for a purpose not expressly prohibited by this Act". Thailand removed its statutory exceptions in 2022 and moved them to ministerial regulations. Singapore's old obsolete-video-game exception for libraries appears to have lapsed on 31 December 2024.

### Cited Findings

**India**
- s.65A(1) (inserted by the Copyright (Amendment) Act 2012): "Any person who circumvents an effective technological measure applied for the purpose of protecting any of the rights conferred by this Act, with the intention of infringing such rights, shall be punishable with imprisonment which may extend to two years and shall also be liable to fine." — [lawgist.in s.65A](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/65A)
- s.65A(2) exceptions: (a) "doing anything referred to therein for a purpose not expressly prohibited by this Act", with a proviso that a person who facilitates circumvention for another must keep a full record of that person and the purpose; (b) encryption research using a lawfully obtained encrypted copy; (c) lawful investigation; (d) security testing of a computer system or network with the owner's authorisation; (e) [text garbled on source, "operator"; generally reported as circumvention of measures protecting the privacy of a user or operator]; (f) circumventing measures for identification or surveillance of a user; (g) national security. — [lawgist.in s.65A](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/65A)
- The 2012 TPM rules were added to bring India in line with the WCT and WPPT. — [BananaIP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/technological-protection-measures-copyright-act-india-drm/)

**Singapore: Copyright Act 2021, Part 7 Division 4 (ss.422–443)**
- s.425 bans circumventing an access control measure; ss.426–427 ban dealing in circumventing devices and services. Exceptions: s.428 (non-profit library, archive or educational access to decide on acquisition); s.429 (personal information); s.430 (interoperability); s.431 (encryption research); s.432 (preventing minors' access); s.433 (testing security flaws, with the computer owner's authority); s.434 (law enforcement and defence); s.435 (prescribed exceptions). s.439 is the offence. — [LawPlayer, CA2021 Part 7 Div 4](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/section/part-7.division-4)
- s.430(1): a person may circumvent a TPM on a protected copy of a computer program if the copy is not infringing, the act is in good faith, concerns elements not readily available, and is for "the sole purpose of achieving interoperability of an independently created computer program", and does not infringe copyright in the first program. s.430(2)–(3) allow limited dealing in devices and services for that purpose. — [LawPlayer s.430](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/430)
- s.435: the Minister may prescribe exceptions where the ban has impaired, or is likely to impair, a permitted dealing that is not itself infringing. — [LawPlayer Part 7 Div 4](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/section/part-7.division-4)
- The earlier regime (Copyright (Excluded Works) Order, S 649/2008) had a video-game exception. It treated a computer program or video game as obsolete if it could only be played on a machine or system no longer manufactured or commercially available, and allowed circumvention for library or archive preservation. — [WIPO Lex record](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7030)
- The prescribed exceptions in the Copyright Regulations 2021 (Part 6 Division 1) expired on 31 December 2024. The 2024 consultation asked whether to keep an exception for a computer program or video game in an obsolete format that can only be accessed with its original medium or hardware, and one for programs with obsolete authentication. — [Baker McKenzie, 2024](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/intellectual-property/singapore-public-consultation-on-prescribed-exceptions-in-copyright-regulations-2021); [MinLaw consultation](https://mlaw.gov.sg/public-consultation-on-ca-exceptions2024)
- Outcome: the Copyright (Access Control Measures – Prescribed Exceptions) Regulations 2024 are in force from 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2028. Only regulation 95 (accessible-format text conversion) was renewed. Two new exceptions allow circumvention to make a copy that is a permitted use under s.232(1) (preservation and replacement copying of material in public collections) and under s.233(1). — [Rajah & Tann Singapore, 19 Dec 2024](https://sg.rajahtannasia.com/?p=87460)
- s.232 "Preservation and replacement — copying material in public collections" lets the custodian of a public collection copy works, films and recordings for preservation, format change, replacement or other listed purposes. It requires a reasonable investigation and a declaration by an authorised officer. — [LawPlayer s.232](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/232)

**Malaysia: Copyright Act 1987 s.36A**
- s.36A(1): no person shall circumvent a TPM applied with the owner's authority. s.36A(2) exceptions, each "for the sole purpose" of: interoperability of an independently created computer program with the original or other programs; identifying and analysing flaws in encryption technology; testing or correcting the security of a computer, system or network; identifying and disabling undisclosed capabilities that collect personal information. Also exempt: lawful law enforcement, national security or statutory functions; a library, archive or educational institution deciding whether to acquire; and accessible-format copies for print-disabled persons. s.36A(3) bans devices and services. s.36A(4) lets the Minister exempt technologies. — [WIPO Lex Act 332 (consolidated 30 June 2022)](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/597931)
- The TPM provisions were added or strengthened by the Copyright (Amendment) Act 2010 (Malaysia then left the USTR Watch List). — [IIPA 2013 Special 301](https://iipa.org/files/uploads/2017/12/2013SPEC301MALAYSIA.pdf)
- The July 2026 MyIPO proposals would "materially expand" TPM and RMI protection under ss.36A and 36B. These are proposals only. — [Baker McKenzie, July 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/malaysia-public-consultation-on-proposed-copyright-act-1987-reforms)

**Indonesia: UU 28/2014**
- Pasal 52 bans damaging, destroying, removing or disabling "sarana kontrol teknologi" (technological control means) that protect a work. Exceptions: national defence and security; "other causes" under laws and regulations; or "diperjanjikan lain" (as otherwise agreed). — [pasal.id, Pasal 52](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-28-tahun-2014/pasal-52)

**Philippines: IP Code**
- s.171.12 (added by RA 10372): a "technological measure" is any technology, device or component that, in normal operation, restricts acts not authorised by the authors, performers or producers concerned, or not permitted by law. — [LawPhil, RA 10372](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2013/ra_10372_2013.html)
- s.216.1(b): damages are doubled against any person who "circumvents effective technological measures". — [LawPhil, RA 10372](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2013/ra_10372_2013.html)

**Thailand**
- The 2015 amendment added TPM protection. The old s.53/5 had exceptions, for example circumvention to test security problems of computer systems. — [Tilleke & Gibbins, ten key changes](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/ten-key-changes-thailands-copyright-act/)
- The 2022 amendment (in force 23 August 2022) covers both rights-control and access-control TPMs. Making an access-control TPM ineffective is infringement with no intent requirement. Circumvention services are banned, and anyone who makes, sells, distributes or advertises circumvention devices is liable. The old exceptions "were considered too broad". They were removed from the Act, and "the exceptions will be laid out in ministerial regulations to be issued later". — [Tilleke & Gibbins, 2022 amendment p.4](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/the-2022-amendment-of-thailands-copyright-act-the-good-the-bad-and-the-backstory/4/)

**Vietnam**
- The 2022 amendment adds definitions of "technological measures to protect rights" and "effective technological measures". — [ASEAN Briefing](https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/vietnams-intellectual-property-law-new-amendments/)
- The amended law "amends and introduces some acts of infringement in relation to technological measures and rights management information". — [Tilleke & Gibbins, 23 Sept 2022](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/vietnams-amended-ip-law-will-strengthen-and-modernize-copyright-protection/)

**Australia**
- s.116AN gives the owner or exclusive licensee a civil action against a person who circumvents an access control TPM, knowing or reasonably ought to know it would do so. s.116AN(3): the action does not apply to circumvention for interoperability with an independently created computer program, where the copy was lawfully obtained and is not infringing, the elements are not readily available, and the purpose is solely interoperability. s.116AP (circumvention services) has a parallel exception. s.132APC makes circumvention for commercial advantage an offence. (search snippet) — [AustLII s.116AN](https://20.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s116an.html); [AustLII s.116AP](https://www6.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s116ap.html)
- Prescribed acts were earlier in Schedule 10A of the Copyright (Technological Protection Measures) Regulations 1969 (reg 20Z). Item 1 covered reproducing or adapting a program for interoperability. — [WIPO Lex record](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/zh/legislation/details/17787)
- The Copyright Regulations 2017 (F2017L01649) Part 7 now deals with TPMs and the non-infringing acts that circumvention may enable. — [Federal Register of Legislation, F2017L01649](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01649/latest/text)
- Take-Two Interactive Software, Inc v Anderson (No 2) [2024] FCA 1459 (Nicholas J). GTA V "Infamous Mod". Access-control checks for Online Mode were access control TPMs. Real-time memory checks were not, because they end access already granted. Making and selling the mod was not actionable under s.116AN, which targets the act of circumvention itself. It was actionable under s.116AO (circumvention devices). — [Piper Alderman, 6 March 2025](https://piperalderman.com.au/insight/whats-in-a-game-technological-protection-measures-under-the-copyright-act/)

**New Zealand**
- The 2008 s.226 defined a TPM as a measure that prevents or inhibits infringement. It excluded measures that only control access for non-infringing purposes, giving geographic market segmentation as an example. — [Davies Collison (DWW) 2008 summary](https://www.dww.com/articles/new-zealand-reforms-copyright-law); [NZLII CPTPP Amendment Act 2018 s.41](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/capaftpaa2018753/s41.html)
- The CPTPP-related amendment (2018) added an "access control TPM" definition. It means a technology, device or component that "in the normal course of its operation, controls any access to a TPM work", but excludes one that can be circumvented accidentally. It replaced ss.226C–226J. s.226E is a general exception for acts that do not infringe, which regulations can modify (s.234(1)(qa)). ss.226F–226K are specific exceptions, including geographic market segmentation (s.226F) and encryption research. s.226L covers acts permitted by regulations (s.234(1)(qb)). s.226O covers not-for-profit entities. ss.226E–226L and 226O are read independently. (search snippet) — [NZLII CPTPP Amendment Act 2018 s.41](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/capaftpaa2018753/s41.html)
- s.226F: nothing in ss.226A–226C stops circumvention to the extent the measure controls geographic market segmentation by preventing, in NZ, playing a non-infringing copy, unless playing it infringes. — [NZLII CPTPP Amendment Act 2018](https://mail.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/capaftpaa2018753/s38.html)
- The 2026 NZ policy package says TPM rules will be "strengthened", without detail. — [British Copyright Council, 9 June 2026](https://www.britishcopyright.org/new-zealand-copyright-update/)

### Inferences
- In Australia, NZ, Singapore and Malaysia, the interoperability TPM exceptions are framed around "an independently created computer program". A harness that reads a person's own original files may arguably fit, but none of these has been tested for game reimplementations.
- Singapore's 2025 regulations seem to remove the specific obsolete-game exception, but add one tied to s.232 preservation copying by public collections. A gallery, library, archive or museum may still circumvent to make preservation copies. Private individuals get no equivalent.
- India's TPM rule needs an "intention of infringing". Circumvention for a lawful s.52 purpose (s.65A(2)(a)) is excused. This makes India one of the most permissive regimes here for study of a game a person owns.
- Australia's Take-Two case shows game-integrity and online checks can be TPMs. Tools that patch a running game may be "circumvention devices" even when the user owns the game.

### Gaps
- Australia (not confirmed): the Copyright Amendment Act 2006 (from 1 January 2007, implementing AUSFTA) created s.116AN and the access-control regime after Stevens v Sony. The s.10(1) TPM definitions exclude measures that only control geographic market segmentation for films and computer programs (region coding), and measures that restrict use of goods other than the work (e.g. printer cartridges). s.116AN(4)–(8) and (9) cover other exceptions, including libraries. The exact list in the Copyright Regulations 2017 Schedule 10 / Part 7 is not confirmed.
- NZ: the commencement of the 2018 access-control TPM changes (possibly 30 December 2018 with CPTPP) and the exact headings of ss.226G–226K were not confirmed.
- Thailand: there is no known evidence that the TPM ministerial regulations promised in 2022 have been issued.
- Vietnam: the article numbers for TPM protection and any exceptions are not known.
- Indonesia: criminal penalty for Pasal 52 (believed to be Pasal 112) was not confirmed. Pasal 53–54 text not confirmed.
- Philippines: no express TPM exceptions are known. Whether fair use (s.185) can excuse circumvention is unclear.

## 3. Preservation exceptions for libraries, archives and museums (software and games)

### Takeaway
Australia's 2017 reforms (ss.113H–113M) are the clearest. They cover all "copyright material", including computer programs, for preservation by libraries, archives and key cultural institutions. Singapore's 2021 Act has a full GLAM division (ss.221–236). Since 2025 it also lets public collections circumvent access controls to make preservation copies. Elsewhere the rules are older, narrower and written for print (India, Malaysia, Thailand, the Philippines, Vietnam). New Zealand plans new GLAM preservation exceptions in a bill expected in 2027.

### Cited Findings
- **Australia:** The Copyright Amendment (Disability Access and Other Measures) Act 2017 received Royal Assent on 22 June 2017. Schedule 1 (including ss.113H–113M) commenced by Proclamation, or at the latest six months after assent, listed as 22 December 2017. Schedule 2 (duration) commenced 1 January 2019. — [Federal Register of Legislation, Act 2017 as made](https://legislation.gov.au/C2017A00049/asmade/2017-06-22/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)
- s.113H: an authorised officer of a library or archives may use material to preserve the collection if the body holds the material in original form, or the officer is satisfied a copy cannot be obtained in a required version or format, "consistent with best practice for preserving such collections". An electronic preservation copy may be made available at the premises with reasonable steps against infringement. No unpublished-only limit and no commercial-availability test. — [Act 2017 as made](https://legislation.gov.au/C2017A00049/asmade/2017-06-22/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)
- s.113J research copies; s.113K uses directly related to care or control of the collection; s.113L defines "key cultural institution" (statutory function to develop and maintain a collection, or prescribed); s.113M preservation by key cultural institutions of material of "historical or cultural significance to Australia". — [Act 2017 as made](https://legislation.gov.au/C2017A00049/asmade/2017-06-22/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html); [AustLII s.113M](https://20.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s113m.html)
- These sections apply to "copyright material" ("anything in which copyright subsists"). Nothing excludes computer programs. By contrast, the s.113P education licence expressly excludes them. — [Act 2017 as made](https://legislation.gov.au/C2017A00049/asmade/2017-06-22/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)
- **Singapore:** Part 5 Division 6 "Public collections: galleries, libraries, archives and museums" (ss.221–236). s.232 covers preservation and replacement copying. — [LawPlayer s.232](https://lawplayer.com/sg/act/CA2021/232). Since 1 January 2025 a prescribed exception allows circumvention of access controls to make s.232 copies. — [Rajah & Tann, 19 Dec 2024](https://sg.rajahtannasia.com/?p=87460)
- **India:** s.52(1)(n): "the storing of a work in any medium by electronic means by a non-commercial public library, for preservation if the library already possesses a non-digital copy of the work". s.52(1)(o): up to three copies of a book for a non-commercial public library if the book is not available for sale in India. s.52(1)(p): reproduction for research or private study of an unpublished literary, dramatic or musical work in a library, museum or institution open to the public. — [lawgist.in s.52](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/52)
- **Malaysia:** s.13(2)(i) covers use by or under the Government, the National Archives or any State Archives, the National Library or any State library, in the public interest, compatible with fair practice and with no profit. — [WIPO Lex Act 332](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/597931)
- **Philippines:** s.188.1 (amended by RA 10372): "any library or archive whose activities are not for profit may, without the authorization of the author or copyright owner," make limited reproductions. — [LawPhil, RA 10372](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2013/ra_10372_2013.html)
- **Thailand:** s.34 lets a librarian copy without profit, if s.32(1) is met, for use in the library or another library, and parts of a work for another person's research or study. s.35(9) allows copies of a computer program kept for public reference or research. — [Wikisource translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:Copyright_Act,_2537_BE)
- **Vietnam:** Art. 25(1)(e) covers non-commercial library operations, including reproducing works stored in the library for preservation. A 2022-era summary also covers inter-library network access with limits on concurrent readers. — [LawNet, copyright exceptions](https://lawnet.vn/en/laws/what-are-the-copyright-exceptions-in-vietnam-according-to-the-current-regulations-89118.html)
- **New Zealand:** Government policy decisions (published by June 2026) include new exceptions for libraries, archives and museums, covering preservation copying and wider research and private-study uses, plus orphan works. A bill is possible in the first half of 2027, aiming to pass before May 2028. — [British Copyright Council, 9 June 2026](https://www.britishcopyright.org/new-zealand-copyright-update/)

### Inferences
- Australia's s.113H/113M is broad enough for an institution to make a preservation copy of a game, including a disk image. But making it playable at the premises still depends on getting past any TPM, which falls under s.116AN and its regulations.
- India's s.52(1)(n) only helps a library that already holds a non-digital copy. It fits physical media games, not born-digital ones.

### Gaps
- Indonesia: library and archive copying (believed to be Pasal 47 of UU 28/2014) was not confirmed.
- NZ current library and archive sections (ss.50–57) are not confirmed.
- Singapore s.233 heading was not confirmed.

## 4. Copyright term for software and games

### Takeaway
Software is a literary work almost everywhere here, so the usual term is life of the author plus a fixed period. That period is 70 years in Australia and Singapore, 60 in India, and 50 in Malaysia, the Philippines, Thailand, Vietnam and NZ (for now). Indonesia uniquely gives video games and computer programs 50 years from first publication. Games with film-like elements may also carry separate film or audiovisual terms.

### Cited Findings
- **Indonesia:** Pasal 59(1) gives video games (letter d) and computer programs (letter e) 50 years from first announcement (publication). — [pasal.id Pasal 59](https://pasal.id/peraturan/uu/uu-no-28-tahun-2014/pasal-59)
- **India:** s.22, published literary works (which include computer programmes): life plus 60 years from the start of the calendar year after the author's death. s.26, cinematograph films: 60 years from the start of the year after publication. — [The Legal School](https://thelegalschool.in/blog/copyright-time-period-in-india); [India Code, Copyright Act PDF](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1367/1/a195714.pdf)
- **Singapore:** the term for literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works rose from life plus 50 to life plus 70 under the 2004 amendment. Films and recordings run 70 years from publication. — [vLex, Singapore IP Act 2004 highlights](https://vlex.com/vid/highlights-intellectual-property-act-29344420); [WIPO SME materials](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/mdocs/sme/en/wipo_smes_vte_15/wipo_smes_vte_15_t5.pdf)
- **Malaysia:** computer programs are literary works, so s.17 applies: copyright subsists "until the expiry of a period of fifty years after his death". — [WIPO Lex Act 332](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/597931)
- **Philippines:** s.213: life of the author plus 50 years. — [IPOPHL copyright page](https://ipophil.gov.ph/copyright). Senate Bill 1734 (19th Congress) proposed amending ss.213–213.6. There is no known evidence it was enacted. — [Senate of the Philippines](https://legacy.senate.gov.ph/lisdata/4044836855!.pdf)
- **Thailand:** s.19: life plus 50 years; for a juristic-person author, 50 years from creation or first publication. — [Wikisource translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:Copyright_Act,_2537_BE)
- **Vietnam:** Art. 27: cinematographic works, photographic works, applied art and anonymous works get 75 years from first publication (100 years from fixation if unpublished within 25 years). Other works get life plus 50. — [LawNet, term of protection](https://lawnet.vn/thong-tin-phap-luat/en/chinh-sach-moi/term-of-copyright-protection-in-vietnam-116595.html)
- **New Zealand:** extension from 50 to 70 years is government policy. Under the NZ–UK FTA, NZ must implement it within 15 years of entry into force. The NZ–EU FTA requires changes by 1 May 2028. — [Tompkins Wake](https://tompkinswake.com/insights/knowledge/what-does-the-new-nz-uk-free-trade-agreement-mean-for-ip); [MBIE policy paper](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/dmsdocument/32052-policy-decisions-on-copyright-including-free-trade-agreement-obligations-proactiverelease-pdf); [British Copyright Council, 9 June 2026](https://www.britishcopyright.org/new-zealand-copyright-update/)
- **Australia:** the 2017 Act's Schedule 2 (duration, including unpublished works) commenced 1 January 2019. — [Act 2017 as made](https://legislation.gov.au/C2017A00049/asmade/2017-06-22/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)

### Inferences
- Every 1980s and 1990s game in these countries is still in copyright under all of these terms. Even Indonesia's 50-years-from-publication rule only frees games published before about 1976.

### Gaps
- Australia life plus 70 (from the 2004 AUSFTA Implementation Act, in force 1 January 2005) is not confirmed.
- Singapore 2021 Act term section numbers and the computer-program classification were not confirmed from the text.
- NZ–UK FTA entry into force (believed to be 31 May 2023) is not confirmed. One source said 2022 (search snippet).

## 5. Orphan works schemes

### Takeaway
India has had a compulsory-licence route since 1957 (s.31A, widened in 2012). Australia enacted a modern orphan works scheme in the Copyright Amendment Act 2026 (assent 1 April 2026). New Zealand plans one inside its GLAM reforms. No orphan works scheme is known in Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand or Vietnam.

### Cited Findings
- **Australia:** the Copyright Amendment Bill 2025 became the Copyright Amendment Act 2026 (Cth) on 1 April 2026. Users must (1) do a reasonably diligent search for the owner, (2) within a reasonable time before use, (3) keep a record of the search, (4) be unable to identify or locate the owner, and (5) give clear and reasonably prominent notice of the use. If the owner appears later, the user is not liable for past use, and the owner may seek orders against future use if no terms are agreed. — [King & Wood Mallesons, 29 April 2026](https://pulse.mallesons.com/copyright/opening-the-archives-australias-new-orphan-works-scheme/); [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/orphan-works-remote-learning-copyright-amendment-bill-2025); [Bills Digest No. 33, 2025–26](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/bd/bd2526/26bd33)
- The Bill also has schedules on online and hybrid teaching and technical changes. Commencement is the day after Royal Assent (per the Bill). (search snippet) — [Bills Digest and MinterEllison](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/bd/bd2526/26bd33)
- **India:** s.31A: any person may apply for a licence to publish, communicate or translate an unpublished work, or a published work withheld from the public in India, where the author is dead, unknown or untraceable, or the owner cannot be found. The applicant must advertise in an English daily first. The adjudicating body (now the Commercial Court) may direct the Registrar of Copyrights to grant a licence on royalty. — [lawgist.in s.31A](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/31A)
- **New Zealand:** the planned GLAM exceptions include orphan works. — [British Copyright Council, 9 June 2026](https://www.britishcopyright.org/new-zealand-copyright-update/)

### Inferences
- Australia's scheme looks well suited to abandoned games whose publisher is defunct and whose rights owner cannot be found. It does not by itself excuse circumventing TPMs.

### Gaps
- The Australian orphan works scheme's own section numbers, and whether it applies to software and games, were not confirmed. The Mallesons article cites only new s.113Q(2A)–(2D).
- India's s.31A footnote history (Copyright Board, then Appellate Board in 2017, then Commercial Court under the Tribunals Reforms Act 2021) shows inconsistently on the source. It is not confirmed against India Code.
- Orphan works rules in Southeast Asian jurisdictions are not covered exhaustively.

## 6. Notable cases

### Takeaway
Australia has the leading cases: Stevens v Sony (2005) on PlayStation mod chips, and Take-Two v Anderson (2024) on a GTA V cheat mod under the post-2006 TPM law. No reported court decisions on game mod chips, emulators or reverse engineering in India or Southeast Asia are known. Indian game-related cases are mostly about trademarks and site blocking.

### Cited Findings
- **Stevens v Kabushiki Kaisha Sony Computer Entertainment** [2005] HCA 58; (2005) 224 CLR 193, decided 6 October 2005. Stevens sold and installed mod chips in PlayStation consoles. The High Court allowed his appeal. Sony's access code and boot ROM arrangement did not fall within the then s.10(1) definition of "technological protection measure", so the mod chips were not unlawful circumvention devices. — [High Court judgment summary](https://mta-sts.hcourt.gov.au/assets/publications/judgment-summaries/2005/hca58-2005-10-6.pdf); [HCA judgment PDF](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/sites/default/files/eresources/2005/HCA/58.pdf); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevens_v_Kabushiki_Kaisha_Sony_Computer_Entertainment)
- The circumvention device provisions it applied came from the Copyright Amendment (Digital Agenda) Act 2000, in force 4 March 2001. — [WIPO Lex judgment record](https://wipolex-res.wipo.int/edocs/lexdocs/judgments/en/au/au053-jen.pdf)
- **Take-Two Interactive Software, Inc v Anderson (No 2)** [2024] FCA 1459: see section 2. The court permanently restrained Anderson from making the mod or any other circumvention device to distribute. — [Piper Alderman, 6 March 2025](https://piperalderman.com.au/insight/whats-in-a-game-technological-protection-measures-under-the-copyright-act/)
- **India:** in 2025 the Delhi High Court (Amit Bansal J) granted Gameskraft (online rummy and skill games) a dynamic injunction against rogue websites and apps using its marks and copyright content, mainly trademark misuse. — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/intellectual-property-rights/delhi-hc-backs-gameskraft-blocks-rogue-gaming-sites/); [MediaNama, July 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-delhi-high-court-gameskraft-dynamic-injunction/). Delhi courts also grant "dynamic plus" injunctions for film and TV piracy (January 2026). — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/delhi-high-court-orders-blocking-of-piracy-sites-streaming-stranger-things-friends-and-other-global-hits-86938.htm)
- India software-copyright context: in Campus Eai India v Neeraj Tiwari (2019), the Delhi High Court used an IIT-Delhi director as an independent expert to compare code. — [Metacept](https://metacept.com/source-code-comparison/2)
- Comparison: a Canadian court ruled for Nintendo against a mod-chip retailer in 2017 (homebrew defence failed). — [Wikipedia, Modchip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modchip)

### Inferences
- Stevens is still the best-known case on mod chips in the region. Its result was reversed by legislation, so it is history, not current law.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed: the Copyright Amendment Act 2006 answered Stevens and AUSFTA by creating the access-control TPM regime (ss.116AK–116AQ) from 1 January 2007. Sony Computer Entertainment v Ball (UK) and similar cases are outside scope.
- No reported mod-chip, emulator or reverse-engineering decisions are known in Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand or Vietnam. Local-language court reporters are not covered. Enforcement there is mostly raids and criminal or administrative action (not confirmed).
- No Indian case on game piracy by Nintendo, Sony, Krafton or Ubisoft is confirmed.

## 7. Consumer protection and online games being shut down

### Takeaway
Australia has the strongest record. The ACCC won Australian Consumer Law cases against Valve (A$3 million) and Sony PlayStation (A$3.5 million) over refunds for digital games. These show that ACL guarantees apply to digital games sold to Australians. No ACCC statement or decision on server shutdowns ("Stop Killing Games") is known. No comparable positions are known in the other jurisdictions.

### Cited Findings
- **ACCC v Valve:** the Federal Court ordered Valve to pay A$3 million in penalties (December 2016) for misrepresenting consumer guarantee rights in Steam's subscriber agreements and refund policies. The Full Federal Court dismissed Valve's appeal, and the High Court refused special leave. — [ACCC media release](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/valve-to-pay-3-million-in-penalties-for-misrepresenting-gamers-consumer-guarantee-rights); [ACCC, Full Court confirms](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/full-federal-court-confirms-that-valve-misled-gamers); [MinterEllison, special leave refused](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/high-court-dismisses-valve-corporations-application-for-special-leave)
- **ACCC v Sony Interactive Entertainment Network Europe:** A$3.5 million penalty plus A$100,000 costs after Sony admitted breaching the ACL. Staff told buyers refunds were unavailable after 14 days or after download, could only be PlayStation credit, or needed developer approval (2017 to at least September 2019). — [Stevivor](https://stevivor.com/news/sony-fined-3-5-million-australia-digital-refund-policy/); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/sony-has-lost-a-court-case-over-declining-playstation-store-refunds)
- **Stop Killing Games in Australia:** campaigners argue that shutting down paid games may breach the ACL. They point to the Valve precedent. A parliamentary e-petition (EN6080) was lodged. Owners of The Crew were urged to complain to Ubisoft and then the ACCC. No ACCC investigation or decision is confirmed. — [Kotaku Australia](https://kotaku.com.au/?p=1356554); [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/what-is-the-stop-killing-games-controversy-ross-scott-movement-explained/); [APH e-petition EN6080](https://www.aph.gov.au/e-petitions/petition/EN6080)
- The European Citizens' Initiative behind the campaign passed 1 million signatures in July 2025. — [Checkpoint Gaming, July 2025](https://checkpointgaming.net/news/2025/07/stop-killing-games-petition-hits-1-million-signatures/)

### Inferences
- The ACL consumer guarantees (acceptable quality, fitness for purpose) arguably cover a game that stops working because servers close. That has not been tested, and no public ACCC statement on it is known.

### Gaps
- Exact date and citation of the Sony judgment (believed to be [2022] FCA, mid-2022) and the Valve appeal ([2017] FCAFC 224) are not confirmed.
- Petition EN6080's outcome and dates were not confirmed.
- New Zealand (Consumer Guarantees Act 1993), India (Consumer Protection Act 2019) and the Southeast Asian countries: no statements on game shutdowns are known.

## 8. Who does game preservation in these countries

### Takeaway
Australia is the most active. ACMI holds a playable games collection and led the "Play It Again" projects on 1980s and 1990s Australasian games. The National Film and Sound Archive began collecting Australian video games in 2019. New Zealand partners joined Play It Again. No comparable public institutions are known for India or Southeast Asia.

### Cited Findings
- **Play It Again (I):** an ARC Linkage project, led by ACMI with Flinders University, the University of Melbourne, the New Zealand Film Archive (now Ngā Taonga Sound & Vision), the Berlin Computerspiele Museum and Victoria University of Wellington's research trust. Its aim was a playable history of 1980s Australasian games, building on research into Beam Software. It was completed in 2015. Melanie Swalwell was lead chief investigator. ACMI holds the donated games, audio captures and emulation files. — [ACMI, Play It Again I collection](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/119643--play-it-again-i-games-collection); [ACMI exhibition page](https://www.acmi.net.au/whats-on/play-it-again-preserving-australias-game-history/); [Flinders blog](https://blogs.flinders.edu.au/play-it-again/?p=11)
- **Play It Again II:** focuses on 1990s games, with Swinburne, RMIT, AARNet and UNESCO PERSIST as partners. — [ACMI, Play It Again II](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/119644--play-it-again-ii-games-collection); [ACMI 1990s exhibition](https://www.acmi.net.au/whats-on/play-it-again-preserving-australias-videogames-from-the-1990s/)
- **NFSA:** on 26 September 2019 it announced it would collect and preserve Australian video games for the first time, starting with eight titles spanning almost 40 years and formats from cassette to download. — [NFSA media release, 26 Sept 2019](https://www.nfsa.gov.au/about/media-releases/national/nfsa-collect-and-preserve-australian-video-games); [InfoDocket](https://infodocket.com/2019/09/26/national-film-and-sound-archive-of-australia-to-preserve-aussie-video-games-for-the-first-time)

### Inferences
- The NFSA and ACMI are probably "key cultural institutions" under s.113L (statutory collecting functions). That gives them the s.113M preservation route.

### Gaps
- India and Southeast Asia: game preservation institutions and community archives (for example national libraries, university labs, or fan archives) are not covered. Their absence here does not mean there are none.
- NZ: current game preservation work (e.g. at Ngā Taonga or MOTAT) was not confirmed.
