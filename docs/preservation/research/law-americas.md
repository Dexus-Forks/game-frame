# Notes: the Americas

Scope: Canada, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Chile and Colombia. This is not legal advice. Read "GameFrame" as: a person studies a game they own and writes new code that reads their own original files; nothing is distributed.

## 1. Statutory exceptions for reverse engineering, decompiling, studying or testing software for interoperability

### Takeaway
Only Canada has a clear copyright-level interoperability exception (s.30.61). Chile has a broad one (art. 71 Ñ, 2010) that also covers "research and development". Mexico and Colombia have interoperability reverse-engineering exceptions only inside their anti-circumvention rules. Brazil and Argentina have no decompilation or interoperability exception at all, only backup-copy and own-use rules.

### Cited Findings

**Canada (Copyright Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-42)**
- s.30.6 (computer programs): a person who owns a copy of a program may reproduce it by adapting, modifying, converting or translating it, if this is "essential for the compatibility of the computer program with a particular computer", is "solely for the person's own use", and the copy is destroyed when ownership ends. They may also make a backup. Amendments: 1997, c. 24, s. 18; 2012, c. 20, s. 31. — [Justice Laws, Copyright Act, page 8](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-8.html)
- s.30.61 (interoperability of computer programs): a person who owns or is licensed to use a copy of a program may reproduce it "for the sole purpose of obtaining information that would allow the person to make the program" interoperable with another program. They must not use or disclose the information beyond what interoperability needs. Subsection (2) says the exception applies "even if the other computer program incorporates the information". Added 2012, c. 20, s. 31 (Copyright Modernization Act). — [Justice Laws, Copyright Act, page 9](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-9.html)
- s.30.62 (encryption research) and s.30.63 (security testing, with the consent of the owner or administrator of the computer or network) are further 2012 exceptions. Both require adequate notice of a vulnerability to the copyright owner before it is made public. — [Justice Laws, page 9](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-9.html)

**Mexico (Ley Federal del Derecho de Autor, LFDA)**
- Art. 105: the legitimate user may make the number of copies the licence allows, or one copy if it is needed to use the program or as a backup. The backup "deberá ser destruida cuando cese el derecho del usuario". — [mLey, LFDA art. 105](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/105/index.html.md)
- No general copyright exception for decompilation is known outside the anti-circumvention article. The only reverse-engineering text is art. 114 Quáter fr. I (see section 2). — [mLey, LFDA art. 114 Quáter](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/114-qu%C3%A1ter/index.html.md)
- Art. 148 fr. IV: one private copy, once, for "uso personal y privado de quien la hace", not for profit. — [mLey, LFDA art. 148](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/148/index.html.md)

**Brazil (Lei 9.609 of 19 February 1998, the Software Law; Lei 9.610 of 19 February 1998, the Copyright Law)**
- Lei 9.609 art. 6 lists the only acts that do not infringe software rights: (I) one backup copy of a lawfully acquired copy; (II) partial quotation for teaching; (III) similarity forced by functional characteristics, technical standards or limited ways of expression; (IV) integration of a program into an application or operating system, "tecnicamente indispensável às necessidades do usuário", for the exclusive use of the person who did it. There is no decompilation or interoperability exception. — [Planalto, Lei 9.609/1998](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9609.htm)
- Lei 9.609 art. 12: infringing software rights is a crime (detention 6 months to 2 years or a fine). Reproduction for commerce carries 1 to 4 years' imprisonment plus a fine (§1). Prosecution generally needs a private complaint (§3). — [Planalto, Lei 9.609/1998](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9609.htm)
- Lei 9.610 art. 7 XII lists computer programs as protected works. Art. 46 (general limitations) has no software, research or interoperability exception. It allows small extracts for private use (II), quotation for study, criticism or debate (III), and small extracts in new works (VIII). — [Planalto, Lei 9.610/1998](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm)

**Argentina (Ley 11.723, as amended by Ley 25.036, B.O. 11/11/1998)**
- Art. 1 (replaced by Ley 25.036) protects "programas de computación fuente y objeto". It also says protection covers the expression of ideas, procedures, methods of operation and mathematical concepts, "pero no esas ideas, procedimientos, métodos y conceptos en sí". — [InfoLeg, Ley 11.723 texto actualizado](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm)
- Art. 9 (paragraph added by Ley 25.036 art. 3): a licensee may make "una única copia de salvaguardia". It must be labelled with the licensee and date, and used only to replace a lost or unusable original. — [InfoLeg, Ley 11.723](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm)
- Art. 55 bis: exploiting software includes licence contracts. No decompilation, interoperability, research or security-testing exception appears in the consolidated text. — [InfoLeg, Ley 11.723](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm)

**Chile (Ley 17.336 de Propiedad Intelectual, art. 71 Ñ added by Ley 20.435, 2010)**
- Art. 71 Ñ(a): the holder may adapt or copy a program when this is essential for its use, or for archive or backup. Adaptations may not be transferred without authorisation. Copies may be transferred only together with the original. — [LeyChile, Ley 17.336 (XML)](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=28933)
- Art. 71 Ñ(b): reverse engineering of a lawfully obtained copy is allowed "con el único propósito de lograr la compatibilidad operativa entre programas computacionales o para fines de investigación y desarrollo". The information must not be used to make or sell a similar infringing program, or for any other infringing act. — [LeyChile, Ley 17.336](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=28933)
- Art. 71 Ñ(c): activities on a lawfully obtained copy to test, investigate or correct its functioning or security, or that of other programs, the network or the computer. The information may be used only for those purposes. — [LeyChile, Ley 17.336](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=28933)
- The LeyChile XML shows a version dated 2010-05-04 among the article versions, which matches the Ley 20.435 reform. The current consolidated version is dated 2017-11-03. — [LeyChile XML metadata](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=28933)

**Colombia (Ley 23 de 1982; Andean Decision 351 of 1993; Ley 1915 of 12 July 2018)**
- Andean Decision 351 (directly applicable in Colombia) art. 24: the owner of a lawfully circulating copy may make one copy or adaptation when it is indispensable for using the program. Art. 26: loading the program into a device's memory by the lawful user for exclusive personal use is not illegal reproduction. This comes from Peruvian Indecopi decisions summarised in the CERLALC jurisprudence database. — [CERLALC jurisprudence 477](https://cerlalc.org/wp-content/uploads/dar/jurisprudencia/477.pdf); [CERLALC 478](https://cerlalc.org/wp-content/uploads/dar/jurisprudencia/478.pdf)
- Ley 1915 art. 13(a) (an exception to anti-circumvention liability, see section 2): good-faith, non-infringing reverse engineering of a lawfully obtained program copy, where the elements were not readily available, "con el único propósito de lograr la interoperabilidad de un programa de computación creado independientemente con otros programas". — [Secretaría del Senado, Ley 1915 de 2018](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1915_2018.html)
- Ley 1915 art. 16(a): temporary electronic reproduction that is part of a technological process with no independent economic value is allowed. This includes normal computer operation. — [Secretaría del Senado, Ley 1915](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1915_2018.html)

### Inferences
- Canada s.30.61 and Chile 71 Ñ(b) most clearly cover studying a game's code to write a new program that reads the same files. Chile's "investigación y desarrollo" wording is broader than Canada's.
- Canada s.30.6(a) covers adapting a program one owns for one's own computer. That is close to running an old game on new hardware for private use.
- Mexico and Colombia copy the US–FTA/USMCA wording ("independently created program"). Their reverse-engineering clause is framed as an exception to circumvention liability, not to the reproduction right. Whether the copying done during decompilation is lawful there rests on general limitations, which are narrow.
- In Brazil and Argentina, decompiling a game is not expressly authorised. A clean-room reimplementation that copies no expression is the safer argument there. That rests on the idea/expression split (Argentina art. 1) and the functional-similarity clause (Brazil Lei 9.609 art. 6 III).

### Gaps
- Mexico: the LFDA article text here comes from the mLey consolidation, which marks reform dates. It is not confirmed against the official text. Whether another LFDA article (for example art. 106) covers decompilation is not confirmed.
- Colombia: the summary of Decision 351 arts. 24–27 relies on Peruvian decisions and is not confirmed against the official text.
- No court decision is known in any of the six countries that interprets a software interoperability exception for games.

## 2. Anti-circumvention of technological protection measures (TPMs) and its exceptions

### Takeaway
Canada (2012), Mexico (2020, with criminal penalties) and Colombia (2018, civil liability) have full anti-circumvention regimes. Each has an interoperability exception. None has an exception for game preservation. Brazil has an older, narrower civil rule on copy-control devices with no exceptions. Argentina and Chile have no general anti-circumvention prohibition in their copyright statutes. Canada widened its interoperability and repair exceptions in November 2024. Mexico's Supreme Court upheld the 2020 TPM provisions on 3 June 2024.

### Cited Findings

**Canada**
- s.41 defines "circumvent" and "technological protection measure" ("any effective technology, device or component" that controls access to a work or restricts acts). s.41.1(1) prohibits (a) circumventing an access-control TPM, (b) offering circumvention services, and (c) making or distributing circumvention tools. Amendments: 2012, c. 20, s. 47; s.41 also 2024, c. 26, s. 1. — [Justice Laws, Copyright Act, page 12](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-12.html)
- s.41.1(3): the owner "may not elect under section 38.1 to recover statutory damages" from an individual who circumvents only for private purposes. — [Justice Laws, page 12](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-12.html)
- s.41.12 (interoperability), as amended 2024, c. 27, s. 1: 41.1(1)(a) does not apply to circumventing a TPM "that protects a lawfully obtained computer program". The purpose must be (a) "obtaining information that would allow the person to make the program or a device in which it is embedded" interoperable "with any other computer program, device or component", or (b) making it so interoperable. Tools and services may be supplied only for that purpose. Information may be shared for interoperability. Using the exception to infringe copyright removes it. — [Justice Laws, page 12](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-12.html)
- s.41.121 (diagnosis, maintenance and repair), added 2024, c. 26, s. 2: circumvention "for the sole purpose of maintaining or repairing a product, including any related diagnosing" is allowed, also on behalf of another person, but not if the person infringes copyright. — [Justice Laws, page 12](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-12.html)
- The Act was last amended on 2024-11-07, the date of the 2024 c. 26 and c. 27 changes. — [Justice Laws, page 11](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-11.html)
- s.41.11 (law enforcement and national security) and s.41.13 (encryption research) are further exceptions. — [Justice Laws, page 12](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-12.html)
- Statutory damages (s.38.1): $500–$20,000 per work for commercial infringement; $100–$5,000 for all works for non-commercial infringement. — [Justice Laws, page 11](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-11.html)
- *Nintendo of America Inc. v. King*, 2017 FC 246 (Federal Court, 1 March 2017; Campbell J. per secondary reports). The respondent Go Cyber Shopping (2005) Ltd. sold and installed game copiers and mod chips for the DS, 3DS and Wii. Total award $12,760,000: $11,700,000 TPM statutory damages ($20,000 maximum × 585 games, counted "per work"), $60,000 copyright statutory damages ($20,000 × 3 header-data works), and $1,000,000 punitive. The court held that the physical shape of DS/3DS game cards and slots was an effective TPM ("lock and key"). The homebrew/interoperability defence failed: Go Cyber could not prove any user actually used the devices for homebrew. Their primary purpose was circumvention. — [Smart & Biggar summary](https://smartbiggar.ca/insights/publication/nintendo-awarded-12-7-million-for-circumvention-of-technological-protection-measures-in-precedent-setting-case); [judgment PDF](https://www.smartbiggar.ca/_Archives/files/2017%20FC%20246.pdf); [MBM](https://mbm.com/lit_success_story/nintendo-of-america-inc-v-king/)

**Mexico (USMCA/T-MEC implementation, decrees published DOF 1 July 2020)**
- LFDA art. 114 Quáter (added DOF 01-07-2020) lists the cases in which circumventing an effective access-control TPM is not a violation:
  - I. good-faith, non-infringing reverse engineering of a lawfully obtained copy of a program, on elements not previously available, "con el único propósito de lograr la interoperabilidad de un programa de computación creado independientemente con otros programas";
  - II. components to stop minors reaching inappropriate content;
  - III. security testing with the authorisation of the owner of the computer, system or network;
  - IV. access by non-profit library, archive, educational or research staff "con el único propósito de decidir si se adquieren ejemplares";
  - V. disabling undisclosed collection of personal data;
  - VI. law enforcement and national security;
  - VII. encryption research on a lawfully obtained copy;
  - VIII. accessibility for persons with disabilities;
  - IX. any other exception for a class of works "cuando así lo determine el Instituto [INDAUTOR] a solicitud de la parte interesada basado en evidencia".

  — [mLey, LFDA art. 114 Quáter](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/114-qu%C3%A1ter/index.html.md)
- Código Penal Federal art. 427 Bis (added DOF 01-07-2020): whoever "a sabiendas y con fines de lucro eluda sin autorización cualquier medida tecnológica de protección efectiva" faces 6 months to 6 years in prison and a fine of 500 to 1,000 days. Art. 427 Ter (making or selling circumvention devices) and art. 427 Quáter (offering circumvention services) carry the same penalty. — [mLey, CPF art. 427 bis](https://mley.mx/CPF/articulo/427-bis/index.html.md); [WIPO Lex, CPF](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/edocs/lexdocs/laws/es/mx/mx251es.html)
- Constitutional challenge: the CNDH filed Acción de Inconstitucionalidad 217/2020, admitted 17 August 2020. Senators filed 249/2020, which was joined to it. The rapporteur was Justice Javier Laynez Potisek. The draft proposed partial invalidity of art. 114 Octies (notice-and-takedown). On 3 June 2024 the Pleno's final resolutivos were:
  - "Es procedente, pero infundada" (admissible but unfounded);
  - the action was dismissed for lack of a qualified majority on art. 114 Octies fr. II inciso b);
  - validity was recognised for LFDA arts. 114 Quáter, 114 Quinquies, 114 Octies (frs. I b, II a) numeral 1 and second paragraph, d, e, and III), 232 Bis, 232 Ter, 232 Quinquies and 232 Sexies, and CPF arts. 424 Bis, 427 Bis, 427 Ter, 427 Quáter and 427 Quinquies.

  — [SCJN, Acta de la Sesión Pública Núm. 54, 3 June 2024](https://www.scjn.gob.mx/sites/default/files/actas-sesiones-publicas/documento/2024-06-25/54%20-%203%20de%20junio%20de%202024.pdf); [SCJN admission order](https://www.scjn.gob.mx/sites/default/files/acuerdos_controversias_constit/documento/2020-08-14/MP_AccInconst-217-2020.pdf)
- Article 19 (an NGO) criticised the ruling for validating the criminalisation of circumvention even where it is needed to exercise human rights. — [Zeta Tijuana, May 2024](https://zetatijuana.com/2024/05/scjn-valida-censura-digital-y-criminaliza-elusion-de-candados-digitales-acusa-articulo-19/)
- Pending in October 2026: on 7 October 2026 the Chamber of Deputies approved "en lo general" (325–114–1) a dictamen from President Sheinbaum's initiative. It amends art. 114 Octies and adds arts. 114 Nonies and 232 Septies, creating secondary liability for internet service providers (repeat-infringer policies). It was sent to the Senate and is not in force. The report says nothing about TPMs, software or preservation. — [Por Esto, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.poresto.com/mexico/2026/10/7/aprueba-camara-diputados-general-dictamen-reforma-ley-federal-derecho-autor.html); [Grupo Animal](https://grupoanimal.mx/explicaciones/reforma-a-la-ley-federal-del-derecho-de-autor-cuales-son-las-obligaciones-que-se-proponen-para-los-proveedores-de-internet)

**Brazil**
- Lei 9.610 art. 107: whoever (I) alters, removes or disables "dispositivos técnicos introduzidos nos exemplares das obras ... para evitar ou restringir sua cópia", or (II) disables coded signals restricting communication or copying, is liable for damages. The damages are no less than the art. 103 measure. The equipment used is forfeited. This is civil and focused on copy control. The law contains no exceptions to it. — [Planalto, Lei 9.610/1998](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm)

**Argentina**
- The consolidated Ley 11.723 on InfoLeg contains no provision on technological protection measures or circumvention. Its penal articles (arts. 71–72 bis) address unauthorised reproduction, for example of phonograms. — [InfoLeg, Ley 11.723](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm)

**Chile**
- Ley 17.336 contains no circumvention prohibition. The only TPM reference is in the ISP safe harbour (art. 85 O(b)): a hosting provider must "No interferir en las medidas tecnológicas de protección y de gestión de derechos". — [LeyChile, Ley 17.336](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=28933)

**Colombia (Ley 1915 of 12 July 2018)**
- Art. 12: whether or not copyright is infringed, a person incurs civil liability who (a) circumvents without authorisation effective TPMs controlling access to works or protecting rights, or (b) makes, imports, distributes or sells devices or services promoted for, of limited other use than, or designed mainly for circumvention. — [Secretaría del Senado, Ley 1915](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1915_2018.html)
- Art. 13 exceptions:
  - (a) interoperability reverse engineering (see section 1);
  - (b) good-faith encryption research, after a good-faith attempt to get authorisation;
  - (c) components protecting minors;
  - (d) security testing authorised by the computer's owner;
  - (e) non-profit libraries, archives and educational institutions deciding on acquisitions;
  - (f) disabling personal-data collection;
  - (g) non-infringing uses of a class of works where there is substantial evidence of adverse impact, to be reviewed by the Dirección Nacional de Derecho de Autor (DNDA) "en intervalos de no más de tres años";
  - (h) disability access.

  — [Secretaría del Senado, Ley 1915](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1915_2018.html)
- Art. 15: anyone who uses a TPM must inform about "su existencia y alcance", within the Consumer Statute (Ley 1480 de 2011). — [Secretaría del Senado, Ley 1915](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1915_2018.html)

### Inferences
- Canada's 2024 wording ("a device in which it is embedded ... any other computer program, device or component") is the strongest interoperability circumvention exception in the region. It plausibly covers circumventing a console or game's TPM to make one's own lawfully obtained program interoperate with new code or hardware. *King* shows that courts demand evidence that the claimed use is real. It also shows that sellers of piracy-enabling devices get no shelter.
- A preservation-minded user in Mexico faces the region's harshest TPM regime, but the criminal articles require knowledge and "fines de lucro". A non-commercial owner studying their own copy falls outside 427 Bis, though civil/administrative exposure under 114 Quinquies remains possible.
- Mexico (114 Quáter IX) and Colombia (13(g)) both have a DMCA-style rule-making route for new exceptions. A game-preservation exception could in principle be requested from INDAUTOR or the DNDA. There is no known evidence that anyone has asked.
- In Argentina and Chile, removing a TPM is not itself unlawful under copyright statute. Any copying still needs an exception. Contract terms and, in Brazil, art. 107 civil liability remain risks.

### Gaps
- Canada: s.41.2 limits libraries, archives and museums that unknowingly contravene to an injunction (not confirmed). Sections 41.15–41.18 cover security, perceptual disabilities, broadcasters and unlocking wireless devices (not confirmed). The text of ss.41.14–41.21 is not confirmed against the official text.
- Mexico: LFDA art. 114 Quinquies and 232 Bis–Sexies (administrative penalties) are not covered in full. The SCJN's published reasoning (sentencia) is not covered; the account above rests on the minutes.
- Colombia: whether Colombia's Código Penal (art. 272, amended by Ley 1032 de 2006) criminalises circumvention is not confirmed. Whether the Constitutional Court reviewed Ley 1915 is not confirmed (the consolidation flags "Jurisprudencia Vigencia" next to art. 13). No DNDA concept issued under art. 13(g) is known.
- Chile: no primary source is known that confirms Chile's unmet US–Chile FTA TPM obligation. The absence is inferred from the statute text.
- Brazil: no court decision applying art. 107 to console mod chips is known.

## 3. Preservation exceptions for libraries, archives and museums

### Takeaway
Canada (s.30.1) and Chile (art. 71 I) let heritage institutions make preservation copies of works in their collections. Mexico allows one copy of an out-of-print work at risk of disappearing (art. 148 V). Colombia has lending and on-site access exceptions plus an orphan-works scheme. Brazil and Argentina have no library preservation exception. No country has a games-specific rule. No country lets institutions circumvent TPMs for preservation.

### Cited Findings
- Canada s.30.1(1): a library, archive or museum may, "for the maintenance or management of its permanent collection", copy a work (a) if the original is rare or unpublished and is deteriorating, damaged or lost, or at risk of that; (b) for on-site consultation when the original cannot be handled because of its condition; (c) in an alternative format if the original's format is obsolete or becoming obsolete, or the technology to use it is unavailable or becoming unavailable. s.30.1(2): (a)–(c) do not apply "where an appropriate copy is commercially available". Amendments: 1997, c. 24; 1999, c. 31; 2012, c. 20, s. 28. — [Justice Laws, Copyright Act, page 8](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/page-8.html)
- Mexico LFDA art. 148 fr. V: "la reproducción de una sola copia, por parte de un archivo o biblioteca", for "seguridad y preservación", where the work "se encuentre agotada, descatalogada y en peligro de desaparecer". — [mLey, LFDA art. 148](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/148/index.html.md)
- Mexico 114 Quáter IV lets library and archive staff circumvent only to decide whether to acquire a work. — [mLey, art. 114 Quáter](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/114-qu%C3%A1ter/index.html.md)
- Chile Ley 17.336 art. 71 I lets non-profit libraries and archives reproduce a work not available on the market without authorisation or payment:
  - (a) to preserve or replace a copy in the permanent collection, up to two copies;
  - (b) to replace another library's lost copy;
  - (c) to add a copy to the permanent collection.

  The work must not have been on sale in the national or international market in the last three years. Art. 71 K allows electronic reproduction for on-site consultation on the institution's terminals, with no electronic copies possible. — [LeyChile, Ley 17.336](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=28933)
- Colombia Ley 1915 art. 16(b) allows non-profit lending of lawfully acquired copies by libraries, archives or documentation centres. Art. 16(c) allows making works available on dedicated terminals on their premises for research or personal study. Art. 17 requires DNDA public hearings every three years to review exceptions. — [Secretaría del Senado, Ley 1915](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1915_2018.html)
- Brazil Lei 9.610 art. 46 contains no library, archive or preservation exception. — [Planalto, Lei 9.610](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm)
- Argentina Ley 11.723 contains no library or archive preservation exception in the consolidated text. — [InfoLeg, Ley 11.723](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm)

### Inferences
- Canada's s.30.1(1)(c) obsolete-format clause is the best fit for game preservation, for example migrating a game off a dying medium. It does not override the TPM ban (s.41.1). It fails where a re-release is "commercially available".
- Chile's three-year "not on the market" test and Mexico's "agotada, descatalogada y en peligro" test both fail for games re-sold digitally.
- These exceptions benefit institutions, not individuals. A private owner's preservation copy relies on backup-copy rules (Canada 30.6(b), Mexico 105, Brazil 9.609 art. 6 I, Argentina art. 9, Chile 71 Ñ(a)).

### Gaps
- No court or agency guidance is known on whether these exceptions apply to software or games in any of the six countries.
- Argentina: pending library-exception bills are not covered.

## 4. Copyright term for software and games

### Takeaway
Mexico is the longest in the world at life + 100 years. Colombia is believed to be life + 80 (not confirmed). Canada, Chile and Argentina are life + 70. Brazil protects software for only 50 years from publication. Legal-person or employer works are usually counted from publication (70 years in Colombia and Chile).

### Cited Findings
- Canada: the general term was extended from life + 50 to life + 70, effective 30 December 2022, by Division 16 of Part 5 of the Budget Implementation Act, 2022, No. 1 (Bill C-19). It is not retroactive: works already in the public domain stay there. Authors who died in 1972 are protected until the end of 2042. — [McMillan](https://mcmillan.ca/insights/20-more-years-of-copyright-protection-starting-december-30-2022/); [UBC Copyright](https://copyright.ubc.ca/?p=7082); [University of Calgary](https://ucalgary.ca/news/end-year-copyright-change-will-have-significant-effects-public-domain)
- Mexico LFDA art. 29: "La vida del autor y, a partir de su muerte, cien años más" (fr. I), or 100 years after disclosure (fr. II). Reformed DOF 23-07-2003. — [mLey, LFDA art. 29](https://mley.mx/LFDA/articulo/29/index.html.md)
- Brazil Lei 9.609 art. 2 §2: software is protected "pelo prazo de cinqüenta anos, contados a partir de 1º de janeiro do ano subseqüente ao da sua publicação ou, na ausência desta, da sua criação". §3: no registration needed. Other works (art, music, audiovisual) last life + 70 (Lei 9.610 art. 41). Audiovisual and photographic works last 70 years from 1 January after disclosure (art. 44). — [Planalto, Lei 9.609](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9609.htm); [Planalto, Lei 9.610](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm)
- Argentina Ley 11.723 art. 5 (replaced by Ley 24.870, B.O. 16/9/1997): life + 70 from 1 January after death. Art. 8: anonymous works belonging to institutions, corporations or legal persons, 50 years from publication. Art. 4(d) (Ley 25.036) makes the employer the owner of software written by employees. — [InfoLeg, Ley 11.723](http://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/40000-44999/42755/texact.htm)
- Chile Ley 17.336 art. 10: life + 70. Where the employer is a legal person (art. 8, second paragraph), 70 years from first publication. — [LeyChile, Ley 17.336](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=28933)
- Colombia Ley 1915 art. 4 (amending Ley 23 de 1982 art. 27): works owned by a legal person are protected 70 years from the end of the year of first authorised publication. If unpublished within 50 years of creation, 70 years from creation. — [Secretaría del Senado, Ley 1915](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1915_2018.html)

### Inferences
- A game is usually several works: code, art, music and possibly an audiovisual work. In Brazil the code may enter the public domain decades before the art and music. The earliest Brazilian software (published 1975–1976) would reach the public domain from 2026–2027 onward. How this fits with when Lei 9.609 and its predecessor Lei 7.646/1987 applied is not confirmed.

### Gaps
- Colombia: the natural-person term (Ley 23 de 1982 art. 21, believed to be life + 80) is not confirmed against the official text.
- Mexico: how art. 29 applies to works where a company commissioned the work (obra por encargo) is not covered.

## 5. Orphan works schemes

### Takeaway
Canada (Copyright Board licences under s.77) and Colombia (Ley 1915 arts. 18 onward, for institutions) have orphan-works schemes. None is known in Mexico, Brazil, Argentina or Chile. No use of either scheme for a video game is known.

### Cited Findings
- Canada s.77: the Copyright Board may issue non-exclusive licences for published works when the copyright owner cannot be located, if the applicant made reasonable efforts to find them. — [Copyright Board, Unlocatable owners](https://www.cb-cda.gc.ca/en/unlocatable-owners)
- Licences are non-exclusive and valid only in Canada. The Board has discretion over terms. — [Copyright Board ALAI presentation, 15 May 2025](https://www.cb-cda.gc.ca/sites/default/files/inline-files/2025-05-15%20PPT%20ALAI%20Unlocatables-EN_3.pdf)
- From 1990 to 2013 the Board granted 277 licences, for books, newspapers, music, photos and films. Video games are not listed. — [Barry Sookman, 2014](https://barrysookman.com/2014/04/27/orphan-works-the-canadian-solution/)
- A UBC video game law post notes that nothing stops s.77 from applying to video games. — [UBC Allard Video Game Law](https://videogamelaw.allard.ubc.ca/?p=4201)
- Colombia Ley 1915 art. 18 defines orphan works: works or phonograms first published (or broadcast) in Colombia whose rights holders are unidentified, or unlocated after a recorded diligent search (art. 21). Art. 20 lets publicly accessible libraries, educational institutions, museums, archives, film and sound heritage bodies and public broadcasters domiciled in Colombia use orphan works in their collections for public-interest mission purposes. The scheme covers (a) printed works, (b) cinematographic or audiovisual works and phonograms, and (c) public broadcasters' audiovisual works. — [Secretaría del Senado, Ley 1915](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1915_2018.html)

### Inferences
- Colombia's list of orphan-work categories does not name software. A game might qualify only as an "audiovisual" work. Canada's s.77 is open to any published work, so it could license use of an abandoned game in Canada.

### Gaps
- Whether the Copyright Board decision database holds any licence for a "video game" or "software" is not confirmed.
- No orphan-works provision is known in the Mexican, Brazilian, Argentine or Chilean texts. Bills may exist.

## 6. Notable cases on game piracy, mod chips, emulators, ROM sites or reverse engineering

### Takeaway
Canada has the leading case (*Nintendo v. King*, 2017). Elsewhere, enforcement is mostly raids, customs seizures and site blocking, not reported court judgments. No court ruling on emulators or game reverse engineering is known in any of the six countries.

### Cited Findings
- Canada: *Nintendo of America Inc. v. King*, 2017 FC 246, $12.76 million (see section 2). — [Smart & Biggar](https://smartbiggar.ca/insights/publication/nintendo-awarded-12-7-million-for-circumvention-of-technological-protection-measures-in-precedent-setting-case)
- Mexico: IMPI, with the Navy (Semar) and the Mexico City police (SSC), ran "Operación Limpieza" at Friki Plaza, Fan Center and Plaza Meave in Mexico City. It seized pirated games, consoles, accessories and figures worth more than 1 million pesos, and Friki Plaza closed. — [LevelUp](https://www.levelup.com/noticias/cierran-la-friki-plaza-de-ciudad-de-mexico-por-operativo-para-confiscar-videojuegos-consolas-y-figuras-pirata-asi-fue-la-operacion-limpieza/); [Excélsior](https://www.excelsior.com.mx/ciudad-de-mexico/friki-plaza-aseguran-mercancia-pirata)
- Mexico: IMPI stopped more than 22,000 PlayStation-related controllers from China at Manzanillo. — [Xataka México](https://www.xataka.com.mx/legislacion-y-derechos/22-mil-controles-pirata-playstation-llegaron-a-mexico-china-impi-freno-manzanillo)
- UnoTV reports that Nintendo and other companies filed about twenty criminal amparos in Mexico City against the federal IP crimes prosecutor's office. Modified consoles are common in informal markets. — [UnoTV](https://www.unotv.com/nacion-gamer/nintendo-y-otras-marcas-escalan-la-batalla-contra-la-pirateria-en-mexico-el-debate-ya-no-es-solo-comercial/)
- Brazil: "Operação 404", a continuing action of the Ministry of Justice since 2019. Phase 5 (March 2023) took down 102 streaming and game addresses, 63 music apps and 128 dynamic domains. Phase 6 (November 2023) took down 606 sites. The US Entertainment Software Association supported the 2023 phase. — [Teletime, 14 Mar 2023](https://teletime.com.br/14/03/2023/operacao-bloqueia-mais-de-200-sites-e-aplicativos-de-pirataria/); [Teletime, 28 Nov 2023](https://teletime.com.br/28/11/2023/operacao-404-acao-internacional-tira-606-sites-de-streaming-pirata-do-ar/)

### Inferences
- The Mexican 2020 criminal TPM articles (427 Bis–Quáter) give prosecutors a direct tool against mod-chip sellers. No reported conviction is known.

### Gaps
- No reported judgments on mod chips, emulators, ROM sites or game reverse engineering are known in Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Colombia or Mexico. Spanish- and Portuguese-language court databases are not covered.
- The date of Mexico's Operación Limpieza and the outcome of the Nintendo amparos were not confirmed.

## 7. Consumer-protection positions on online games being shut down

### Takeaway
Brazil has a live bill, PL 3612/2026 ("PL dos Games Vivos"), inspired by Stop Killing Games. It would require a minimum support period, 180 days' notice, and an offline mode, community tools or a partial refund. No Procon or Senacon action on a game shutdown is known. Colombia's Ley 1915 art. 15 ties TPM disclosure to consumer law. Nothing is known for the other countries.

### Cited Findings
- Brazil PL 3612/2026, by Deputy Jandira Feghali (PCdoB-RJ), is under analysis in the Chamber of Deputies. Companies must state how long they will support a game, at least two years. They must give at least 180 days' notice of shutdown. They must then offer at least one option: a version that works without internet, tools for the community to keep it running, or a partial refund. — [Game Overdrive](https://gameoverdrive.com.br/pl-3612-2026-preservacao-jogos/); [Diário do Comércio](https://diariodocomercio.com.br/?p=610178); [Ovício](https://ovicio.com.br/projeto-de-lei-pode-acabar-com-jogos-como-servico-entenda-a-situacao/)
- On 22 July 2026 Deputy Kim Kataguiri publicly supported the bill, with reservations about parts he saw as unrelated to the core problem. Erika Hilton also supported it. Critical Hits credits the proposal to Kataguiri; other outlets name Feghali as author. — [TecMundo](https://www.tecmundo.com.br/voxel/504923-kim-kataguiri-e-erika-hilton-unidos-em-apoio-ao-pl-inspirado-no-stop-killing-games-mas-com-ressalvas.htm); [Critical Hits](https://criticalhits.com.br/games/deputado-brasileiro-propoe-lei-para-obrigar-jogos-a-terem-modo-offline-e-aviso-previo-de-servidores/)
- Citizens' legislative ideas on the Senate's e-Cidadania portal address the same subject. — [Senado e-Cidadania idea 220773](https://www12.senado.leg.br/ecidadania/visualizacaoideia?id=220773); [idea 185667](https://www12.senado.leg.br/ecidadania/visualizacaoideia?id=185667)
- Do not confuse PL 3612/2026 with PL 2796/2021 (Kataguiri's "Marco Legal dos Games", on manufacturing, tax and sale of games). The Chamber approved that bill in 2024 and sent it for sanction. — [Jornal de Brasília](https://jornaldebrasilia.com.br/noticias/politica-e-poder/camara-aprova-regulamentacao-de-jogos-eletronicos/)
- Colombia Ley 1915 art. 15: whoever uses a TPM must disclose its existence and scope, under the Consumer Statute (Ley 1480 de 2011). — [Secretaría del Senado, Ley 1915](http://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_1915_2018.html)

### Inferences
- If enacted, PL 3612/2026 would be one of the first national laws to make an offline mode or community server tools a remedy for shutdowns. Those remedies line up directly with GameFrame's aim of keeping owned games playable.

### Gaps
- No Procon, Senacon, PROFECO (Mexico), SERNAC (Chile), SIC (Colombia) or Argentine consumer-agency action on a game shutdown is known.
- Authorship and status of PL 3612/2026 come from news reports. They are not confirmed against the official Chamber record (tramitação).
- The Marco Legal dos Games law number (believed to be Lei 14.852/2024) was not confirmed.

## 8. Who does game preservation in these countries

### Takeaway
Preservation is mostly done by universities, collectors and small museums. National libraries play little visible role. The best documented are the University of Toronto Mississauga collection and Brazil's Museu do Videogame Itinerante.

### Cited Findings
- Canada: the University of Toronto Mississauga library acquired collector Syd Bolton's collection, described as the largest video game collection in Canada. It is being processed for on-site access, with digital preservation planned from 2024. — [University of San Diego symposium abstract, 2024](https://digital.sandiego.edu/symposium/2024/2024/5)
- Canada: the University of Alberta CIRCA Video Games Archive Project built an archive from about 1,000 physical items. — [CIRCA, University of Alberta](https://circa.cs.ualberta.ca/index.php/CIRCA:Video_Games_Archive_Project)
- Canada: a Carleton University talk on preserving "unremarkable" games was held at Library and Archives Canada, Ottawa, on 13 October 2026. — [Carleton University](https://carleton.ca/history/event/not-worth-mentioning-preserving-unremarkable-videogames/)
- Canada: a study of the BAnQ (Bibliothèque et Archives nationales du Québec) catalogue assessed game preservation in Quebec. — [Loading journal, Érudit](https://releve.erudit.org/en/journals/loading/2021-v14-n23-loading06149/1078732ar)
- Brazil: the Museu do Videogame Itinerante (Campo Grande, MS) was certified in 2014 by Ibram as the first museum of its kind in the country. It holds 576 consoles, tours free of charge, and has a cooperation agreement with the Nostálgica museum in Lisbon. — [Baguete](https://www.baguete.com.br/noticias/brasil-tem-1o-museu-oficial-do-videogame); [O Povo, 10 Sep 2026](https://mais.opovo.com.br/jornal/vidaearte/2026/09/10/mostra-gratuita-sobre-videogames-retorna-a-shopping-de-fortaleza.html); [Campo Grande News](https://www.campograndenews.com.br/lado-b/games/museus-do-videogame-terao-mostras-integradas-entre-brasil-e-portugal)
- Brazil: a 2025 UFSCar thesis mapped preservation initiatives and found methodological, legal and institutional gaps. Instituto Bojogá is cited in research as a reference point. — [UFSCar repository](https://repositorio.ufscar.br/items/f96f04e7-8b2c-480d-9c8d-8967222d3931); [UFRGS Biblioteca Central](https://www.ufrgs.br/bibliotecacentral/arquivando-diversao-para-eternidade/)
- Mexico: Artemio Urbina's Arcades.mx project restores and preserves arcade boards, with a focus on Capcom CPS II. — [Leteo, UACH](https://revistascientificas.uach.mx/index.php/leteo/article/download/1296/2274/10161)
- Mexico: INAH ran a "Mini Game Work Jam Virtual" on heritage conservation. — [UnoTV](https://www.unotv.com/nacional/con-videojuegos-buscan-conservar-patrimonio-cultural-de-mexico/)
- Argentina: the SAHTI symposium (SADIO, 50 JAIIO) published work on local arcade history, for example SACOA. It notes fragmented records of national computing heritage. — [SAHTI paper](https://50jaiio.sadio.org.ar:443/pdfs/sahti/SAHTI-05.pdf)

### Inferences
- None of these bodies appear to use the statutory preservation exceptions for software. Most collect hardware and physical media.

### Gaps
- Chile and Colombia: no specific game preservation institutions are known.
- Whether national libraries' legal-deposit rules cover games (for example Library and Archives Canada or Brazil's Biblioteca Nacional) is not confirmed.
