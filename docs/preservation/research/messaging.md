# Notes: how preservation projects describe their work

A finding that comes only from a search-result summary is marked "(search snippet)".

## How do preservation bodies and respected projects phrase their mission statements?

### Takeaway
Mission statements are short and positive. They use verbs like preserve, celebrate, teach, collect, share and keep playable. They name a cultural or consumer value (history, heritage, play, ownership). None of them lead with technique, and none mention free games.

### Cited Findings
- VGHF: a nonprofit "dedicated to preserving, celebrating, and teaching the history of video games" (search snippet) — [gamehistory.org](https://gamehistory.org/)
- VGHF's archive covers source code, development tools, raw art and documentation. It also runs a research library and a digital library (search snippet) — [ilovelibraries.org](https://ilovelibraries.org/article/preserving-video-game-history/); [gamehistory.org](https://gamehistory.org/five-ways-youve-helped-save-video-game-history/)
- The Strong: explores how play "encourages learning, creativity, and discovery" (search snippet) — [thestrong.org/about](https://thestrong.org/about)
- ICHEG (part of The Strong) collects, studies and interprets electronic games for present and future generations. This wording is from Wikipedia and is not official — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Center_for_the_History_of_Electronic_Games)
- Software Heritage: "collect, preserve and share all software that is publicly available" in source form. It frames source code as part of "our heritage that we should not lose". UNESCO supports it (agreement signed 2017) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_Heritage); [linux.com](https://www.linux.com/news/software-heritage-backed-unesco/)
- Digital Preservation Coalition: "working together for a sustainable digital legacy". It works against "technical obsolescence, media degradation and organizational change" (search snippet) — [dpconline.org/about](https://dpconline.org/about)
- GOG Preservation Program, launched November 2024, with the slogan "make games live forever". GOG commits its own resources to keeping games compatible "with modern and future systems". Buyers get offline installers, the most complete edition and tech support — [GOG news post](https://gog.com/ru/news/welcome_to_the_gog_preservation_program_making_games_live_forever); [GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2024/11/gog-launch-their-preservation-program-to-make-games-live-forever-with-hundreds-of-classics-being-re-released). Date conflict: one summary of GOG's press release gave 13 October 2024, while [OzBargain](https://ozbargain.com.au/node/876747) and GamingOnLinux give 13 November 2024. GOG's own page is not confirmed.
- Stop Killing Games: "a global coalition of gamers, consumer advocates, and developers". Its aim is to "ensure games remain playable after publisher support ends". Its home-page tagline is "They kill games. We fight back." — [stopkillinggames.com](https://www.stopkillinggames.com/)
- Nightdive Studios restores abandoned games by acquiring the rights. It calls a game a remaster when it uses the original source code "or a reverse-engineered version" with the original assets (search snippet) — [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/nightdive-remakes-remasters-star-wars-system-shock); [Willamette Week](https://wweek.com/technology/2021/01/12/how-a-local-company-is-saving-forgotten-video-games-from-the-dustbin-of-history)
- OpenRCT2 calls itself "An open-source re-implementation of RollerCoaster Tycoon 2". It credits the original: "originally written by Chris Sawyer in x86 assembly" — [OpenRCT2 GitHub](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2)
- DevilutionX's GitHub tagline: "Diablo build for modern operating systems" — [GitHub](https://github.com/diasurgical/devilutionX)
- MAME's about page is not quoted here.

### Inferences
- The site could follow this pattern: verb + object + who it is for. For example: "Keep the games you own playable, and document how they worked." Lead with what the project keeps alive. Leave technique to later on the page.
- Two families of framing exist. Institutions (VGHF, Strong, Software Heritage, DPC) talk about history, heritage and study. Projects and stores (GOG, OpenRCT2, DevilutionX) talk about modern systems and playability. A harness can use both: "playable on modern systems" for players, "documented for study" for researchers.
- Stop Killing Games is adversarial ("We fight back"). A tool project that wants goodwill from rights holders should avoid that tone, though it can cite the consumer-rights problem.

### Gaps
- The official ICHEG and MAME mission wording could not be checked live (MAME is usually described as documenting hardware, but this was not confirmed here).
- The Internet Archive's own wording for its software collections is not covered.
- The exact text of the GOG Preservation Program page could not be read (503).

## Which terms do they prefer, and which do they avoid?

### Takeaway
Respected projects say "original game data", "requires original files", "re-implementation", "port", "modern operating systems" and "legally". They reject "abandonware" outright. Institutions say "out of print", "unavailable" and "cultural heritage". The industry opposition links "hacking" to piracy, so words like "crack", "hack", "rip" and "free" invite that reading.

### Cited Findings
- ScummVM's FAQ says "abandonware" "is not a proper legal concept in any country" — [ScummVM FAQ](https://docs.scummvm.org/en/latest/help/faq.html)
- ScummVM: it is not legal to copy a game "because the company is no longer selling it" — [ScummVM FAQ](https://docs.scummvm.org/en/latest/help/faq.html)
- ScummVM: "Our project has a strict no-piracy policy." — [ScummVM FAQ](https://docs.scummvm.org/en/latest/help/faq.html)
- OpenRCT2: "requires original files of RollerCoaster Tycoon 2 to play." — [OpenRCT2 GitHub](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2)
- re3/reVC's disclaimer said "We do not encourage piracy or commercial use." It was still taken down (see below) — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2021/11/gta-modders-behind-re3-and-revc-fire-back-in-court)
- VGHF's study uses "out of print" and "critically endangered" for 87% of US games released before 2010 — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2023/researchers-find-87-of-u-s-classic-video-games-are-out-of-print-and-critically-endangered/); [VGHF explainer](https://gamehistory.org/study-explainer)
- In the 2015 rulemaking, the ESA (Entertainment Software Association) called hacking "an activity closely associated with piracy in the minds of the marketplace" — [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2015/04/videogame-publishers-no-preserving-abandoned-games-even-museums-and-archives)
- In 2024 the Copyright Office accepted industry fears of a "free online arcade" when it refused remote access — [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/us-copyright-office-refuses-to-allow-remote-access-to-out-of-print-games); [Slashdot/Ars](https://games.slashdot.org/story/24/10/25/2046233/video-game-libraries-lose-legal-appeal-to-emulate-physical-game-collections-online)
- The Register said preserved games could be used for "recreation" and not only research, and this weighed against the exemption (headline wording) — [mp1st](https://mp1st.com/news/us-copyright-office-blocks-game-preservation-effort-saying-preserved-video-games-would-be-used-for-recreation)
- GOG's "preservation" label drew some user scepticism. Commenters called it marketing, or said it means little if original versions are not kept — [GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2024/11/gog-launch-their-preservation-program-to-make-games-live-forever-with-hundreds-of-classics-being-re-released)

### Inferences
- Words to prefer: preservation, playable, your own copy, original files, legally obtained, re-implementation, documentation, interoperability, out of print, modern systems, study.
- Words to avoid: abandonware, ROMs, cracks, cracking, rip, warez, free games, "unlock", "bypass". Avoid "hack" too: the ESA has used it to mean piracy.
- "Preservation" on its own can sound like marketing (see the GOG criticism). Back it up with concrete things: documentation, tests, no game material, a requirement to own the game.
- Because "recreation" weighed against the 2024 exemption, the page should not lean only on "play it again". It should also stress research, documentation and how games worked.

### Gaps
- No source here confirms whether "interoperability" is used publicly by these projects. It is the legal term in EU (Software Directive Art. 6) and US (DMCA 1201(f)) reverse-engineering exceptions.
- No source here confirms whether "bring your own copy" (BYOC) is a phrase these projects use publicly.

## How do respected projects state their legal position, and what do they offer rights holders?

### Takeaway
The standard pattern is one short sentence near the top: the project needs the original game files. Next to it are links to buy the game on GOG or Steam, and a note on freeware releases made with the rights holder's permission. A clean-room claim and a disclaimer do not protect a project by themselves (re3).

### Cited Findings
- OpenRCT2's README links straight to the Steam and GOG store pages next to the requirement — [OpenRCT2 GitHub](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2)
- DevilutionX tells users either to copy DIABDAT.MPQ from an original CD or to buy the game from GOG.com or Battle.net. A shareware spawn.mpq is the free option — [devilutionx.com/mpq](https://devilutionx.com/mpq)
- GOG lists DevilutionX as a free mod that "Requires Diablo + Hellfire" (search snippet; the page itself returned 503). Whether Blizzard approved the listing was not confirmed — [GOG DevilutionX](https://www.gog.com/en/game/devilutionx)
- ScummVM refuses support "when it becomes evident" that a copy was not obtained legally. It points to freeware releases and "some digital options" — [ScummVM FAQ](https://docs.scummvm.org/en/latest/help/faq.html)
- ScummVM lists only mods made "with the permission of the original rights holders" — [ScummVM FAQ](https://docs.scummvm.org/en/latest/help/faq.html)
- Publisher pushback: in February 2021 Take-Two took re3 and reVC (GTA III and Vice City reimplementations) off GitHub under the DMCA. The code returned after a counter-notice. Take-Two then sued the leads, alleging they "sought unlawfully to copy, adapt, and distribute" the source code. The projects came down again. The defence argued fair use and reverse engineering — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2021/11/gta-modders-behind-re3-and-revc-fire-back-in-court); [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/take-two-gta-reverse-engineering-project-takedown/); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210224-dmca-take-down-reverse-engineered-gta)
- One forum claim says re3 decompiled Rockstar's binaries rather than working clean-room. This is unverified — [GamingOnLinux comments](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2021/11/gta-modders-behind-re3-and-revc-fire-back-in-court)

### Inferences
- The page should copy the ScummVM/OpenRCT2 pattern: "You need your own copy of the game. Buy it here: [GOG] [Steam]." Then: "We ship no game files, code or assets."
- The re3 case shows that projects tied to a big, still-earning franchise carry risk. The harness distributes no per-game output, which sets it apart. The page could say plainly that the harness ships tools and a method, tested only on its own sample programs.
- A visible contact for rights holders (a takedown or concerns address) is a reasonable step. No source confirms that ScummVM or OpenRCT2 shows one on its front page, so it should be presented as good practice, not as what they do.
- Linking to the store where the game is still sold (e.g. Banished on Steam/GOG) shows the project helps sales instead of replacing them.

### Gaps
- Whether ScummVM, OpenRCT2 or MAME publish a dedicated rights-holder or takedown contact was not confirmed.
- The outcome of the Take-Two v. re3 lawsuit was not found (reports are from 2021).
- Whether Blizzard or Microsoft has commented on DevilutionX's GOG listing was not found.

## Have developers or publishers praised or adopted community reimplementations?

### Takeaway
Clear, sourced praise is rarer than people assume. The strongest documented adoption is commercial: GOG distributes DevilutionX as a mod and has ScummVM-style releases, and freeware releases go through ScummVM with rights-holder permission. Claims that Chris Sawyer dislikes OpenRCT2 are hearsay. Ron Gilbert's praise of ScummVM is documented only second-hand.

### Cited Findings
- ScummVM's 2005 news log says Ron Gilbert made "a nice reference to ScummVM" on his Grumpy Gamer blog. The original post was not found. ScummVM had credited him in its readme since 2002 — [ScummVM news log](https://lists.scummvm.org/pipermail/scummvm-git-logs/2005-August/021431.html)
- No on-record statement from Chris Sawyer about OpenRCT2 was found. Hacker News commenters claim he dislikes it but give no source — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42391158)
- Sawyer pursued his own official modern version of RCT2 (RollerCoaster Tycoon Classic). He called bringing RCT2 to mobile his "pet project" (2016 Eurogamer, quoted second-hand) — [OpenRCT2 forum](https://forums.openrct2.org/topic/977-rct2mobile/); [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46130335)
- GOG hosts DevilutionX as a free mod requiring the purchased game (search snippet) — [GOG](https://www.gog.com/en/game/devilutionx)
- Nightdive, a respected restorer, has used "a reverse-engineered version" of source code in licensed remasters. This shows that rights holders accept reverse engineering inside a licensed deal (search snippet) — [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/nightdive-remakes-remasters-star-wars-system-shock)
- Nightdive's Larry Kuperman called piracy a risky way to preserve games (search snippet) — [Galaxus](https://www.galaxus.de/en/page/nightdive-studios-is-fighting-to-keep-long-lost-games-out-of-obscurity-39484)
- Shining Rock (Banished): the developer added mod support with his own toolset. The mod kit ships most of the game data "so people can see how things are built" (search snippet). Version 1.0.7 (2017) is the last beta found — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/banished-mod-support-is-on-the-way-says-developer); [Shining Rock 2017](https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2017-02-13-1-0-7-beta/)
- Publisher pushback: Take-Two v. re3/reVC (see the section above).

### Inferences
- The page should not claim that developers endorse the harness or reimplementations in general. It can cite specific, sourced cases (GOG distributing DevilutionX; Nightdive using reverse engineering under licence).
- For Banished, the honest framing is: the developer supported modding and the game is still on sale, so the case study respects that by requiring a purchased copy and shipping no assets. No Shining Rock statement about Un-Banished was found, so the page should not imply one.

## Which preservation arguments resonate, and what critiques exist?

### Takeaway
The arguments that resonate are scale of loss (87% out of print), cultural heritage, research and education, and consumer ownership (games should stay playable after support ends). The main critique, from the industry and the Copyright Office, is that preservation access becomes a free arcade or covers piracy. Credible bodies answer with safeguards, research framing and concrete limits.

### Cited Findings
- 87% of US games released before 2010 are out of print. The C64 is at 4.5%, the Game Boy at 5.87% and the PS2 at 12% — [Stevivor](https://stevivor.com/news/87-per-cent-of-classic-games-unavailable-says-study); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2023/researchers-find-87-of-u-s-classic-video-games-are-out-of-print-and-critically-endangered/)
- "only 1 of the 132 games in our sample released before 1980 has been reissued" (VGHF study) — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2023/07/11/87-percent-classic-video-games-unavailable-study-preservation)
- A University of Virginia library official called the loss "not a market failure; it's a policy failure." (fragment, under 15 words) — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2023/researchers-find-87-of-u-s-classic-video-games-are-out-of-print-and-critically-endangered/)
- Software Heritage argues that hosting sites shut down and old toolchain tapes "rot in basements" (search snippet) — [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/softwaredev/preserving-softwares-legacy/)
- Stop Killing Games frames it as consumer rights. It collected signatures through the European Citizens' Initiative (1 million needed) — [stopkillinggames.com](https://www.stopkillinggames.com/)
- The Copyright Office refused remote access to preserved games in 2024, its fourth refusal since 2015. It said the proposed safeguards were not specific enough — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/community/topics/us-copyright-office-denies-dmca-exemption-deals-blow-to-video-game-preservation-efforts.288839/); [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/us-copyright-office-refuses-to-allow-remote-access-to-out-of-print-games)
- Games are "often highly expressive in nature", so a remote-sharing exemption for functional software did not extend to them — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/community/topics/us-copyright-office-denies-dmca-exemption-deals-blow-to-video-game-preservation-efforts.288839/)
- The ESA argued the 2015 abandoned-games exemption could enable "the use of pirated games" — [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2015/04/videogame-publishers-no-preserving-abandoned-games-even-museums-and-archives)
- Opponents in 2024 cited the Internet Archive's public game collection as harming reissues — [SPN/LCA reply comment](https://www.copyright.gov/1201/2024/comments/reply/Class%206(b)%20-%20Reply%20-%20Software%20Preservation%20Network%20and%20Library%20Copyright%20Alliance.pdf)

### Inferences
- The strongest answer to "preservation is cover for piracy" is structural, not rhetorical. Show it: no game files distributed, the player's own copy required, store links, tests only on the project's own sample programs. These match the "specific safeguards" the Copyright Office said were missing.
- For the harness, the fitting arguments are: (1) longevity of purchases (games still sold but not updated, e.g. Banished, that may stop running on new systems); (2) documentation of how games worked (research and education, which is less exposed than "recreation"); (3) accessibility and modern platforms (OpenRCT2/DevilutionX framing); (4) cultural heritage (the VGHF and Software Heritage framing).
- Avoid claiming that the harness solves the 87% problem. Reimplementation helps only players who already have a copy, so the page should say that honestly.

- No GDC preservation talk is covered.
- Whether the Stop Killing Games ECI reached its threshold was not confirmed here (the page counter showed placeholders).

## Ethics of AI-assisted reverse engineering, and public concerns

### Takeaway
A long-running human decompilation community (decomp.dev) shut down on 15 October 2026, saying AI had "broken" its community. Press links AI decompilation to quick, cheap game clones. A page promoting AI agents for reverse engineering must face this directly.

### Cited Findings
- 404 Media, 15 October 2026: decomp.dev is shutting down because "the community aspect of decompilation has been broken" by AI — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/video-game-reverse-engineering-community-shuts-down-because-ai-has-made-it-too-easy/)
- The admin says it is now impossible to tell human-led projects from AI-driven ones. Matching decompilation used to take years with many contributors — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/video-game-reverse-engineering-community-shuts-down-because-ai-has-made-it-too-easy/)
- The admin also allowed that, "with the right guidance, human input, and code reviews", AI can speed the work up — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/video-game-reverse-engineering-community-shuts-down-because-ai-has-made-it-too-easy/)
- Related coverage: vibecoding can produce "a cheap rip off of a video game in just a few hours" — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/video-game-reverse-engineering-community-shuts-down-because-ai-has-made-it-too-easy/)
- Naavik's analysis of AI decompilation and games names REA (Reverse Engineer Anything) as a tool that lets Claude build mods end to end. It also reports Capcom restructuring its RE Engine to be readable by AI (search snippet) — [Naavik](https://naavik.substack.com/p/viral-game-mash-ups-what-ai-decompilation)
- Academic work in 2026 questions how faithful LLM decompilers are, e.g. "When LLM Decompilers Recompile More and Preserve Less" (title only; not read) — [arXiv 2609.05370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05370)

### Inferences
- The page should name the concern openly: AI makes copying easier. Then it should state what the harness does differently: it requires the player's own copy, distributes nothing, checks every result against the original (bit-exact tests, which are the opposite of a loose "vibe" clone), cites evidence, and keeps humans reviewing the work.
- The harness's case for being "preservation, not cloning" is its verification and documentation. It rebuilds the same game exactly and records how it worked. It does not invent a knock-off. Say this in concrete terms rather than as a slogan.
- Credit to human reverse-engineering communities (ScummVM, OpenRCT2, decomp projects) and respect for their norms would answer the decomp.dev concern. One option is a statement that the harness's methods are meant to support documented, reviewed work.
- REA is used by this harness and is named in the Naavik piece. The page should be ready for readers who link the harness to that coverage.

### Gaps
- The Naavik piece and the arXiv paper are known only from search snippets.
- No formal ethics guideline for AI-assisted reverse engineering from a preservation body (VGHF, DPC, Software Heritage) was found.
- No public statement from rights holders specifically about AI-agent reimplementation was found.
